💡 Clinical Differentiation Tips
  • CNS disorders affect brain/spinal cord: think cognitive, motor control, consciousness.
  • PNS disorders affect nerves outside CNS: think sensation, muscle strength, reflexes.
  • Remember FAST for stroke: Face drooping, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911.
  • Key differentiator: "Sudden" onset = emergency. Chronic or gradual symptoms = schedule appointment.
  • Time is brain: Stroke treatment must begin within 3-4.5 hours for best outcomes.
✓ Nervous System Disorders Classified!
CNS Disorders (Brain & Spinal Cord):
• Multiple Sclerosis: Autoimmune demyelination of CNS
• Parkinson's: Degeneration of substantia nigra (dopamine)
• Alzheimer's: Progressive dementia with plaques/tangles
• Stroke (CVA): Brain blood flow interruption
• Epilepsy: Recurrent seizures from abnormal electrical activity
• Meningitis: Inflammation of meninges (brain/spinal cord covering)

PNS Disorders (Nerves Outside CNS):
• Carpal Tunnel: Median nerve compression at wrist
• Bell's Palsy: Facial nerve paralysis (usually temporary)
• Guillain-Barré: Autoimmune attack on peripheral nerves
• Diabetic Neuropathy: Nerve damage from chronic high glucose
• Sciatica: Compression/irritation of sciatic nerve
• Myasthenia Gravis: Autoimmune affecting neuromuscular junction

Stroke Recognition - CRITICAL THINKING:
✓ IMMEDIATE 911 REQUIRED (FAST Criteria):
• Sudden facial drooping or asymmetry
• Arm weakness or drift (can't raise both arms equally)
• Speech difficulty or slurred speech (new onset)
• Sudden severe headache ("worst headache of life")
• Sudden vision loss in one or both eyes
• Sudden confusion or trouble understanding
• Sudden trouble walking or loss of balance

✗ Schedule Appointment (Not Immediate 911):
• Mild headache for several days (chronic, not sudden/severe)
• Chronic numbness/tingling in hands (months duration)
• Gradual memory changes over past year (progressive, not sudden)
• Occasional dizziness when standing up (likely orthostatic hypotension)
• General tiredness or fatigue (non-specific symptom)

Clinical Judgment: The key differentiator is "SUDDEN" onset. Chronic or gradual symptoms warrant medical evaluation but don't require emergency 911 response. When in doubt, always err on the side of calling 911 for possible stroke.

💡 Patient Care & Communication Tips
  • EEG vs Other Procedures: EEG doesn't require fasting, contrast, or breath-holding. Hair preparation is unique to EEG.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Use reflective listening, validate feelings, avoid giving advice or false reassurance.
  • Mental Health Stigma: Use person-first language ("person with depression" not "depressed person").
  • HIPAA Compliance: Always verify authorization before releasing ANY PHI, even to family members.
  • Documentation: Record patient education provided, concerns expressed, and safety assessments.
✓ Clinical Procedures & Communication Mastered!
Correct EEG Preparation Sequence (7 Steps):
1. Explain procedure (painless brain activity recording)
2. Wash hair night before (no products - critical for electrode contact)
3. Continue regular medications (unless directed otherwise)
4. Sleep instructions (adequate sleep, possible deprivation if ordered)
5. Explain electrode placement (scalp with conductive paste)
6. Inform about activation procedures (breathing, lights)
7. Schedule and provide written instructions

Distractors/Incorrect Steps Explained:
• "Hold breath during procedure" - INCORRECT: This is for MRI, not EEG
• "Fast for 8 hours before" - INCORRECT: EEG doesn't require fasting
• "Drink contrast dye 1 hour before" - INCORRECT: This is for CT scan with contrast

Why Sequence Matters: Hair products interfere with electrode-scalp contact. Sleep deprivation may be ordered for certain epilepsy types. Continuing medications prevents withdrawal symptoms. Understanding what's NOT needed prevents unnecessary patient burden.

Therapeutic Response Analysis:
• Response 4 is MOST therapeutic: Validates experience, uses clinical terminology, offers collaboration with provider
• Avoid: Minimizing ("snap out of it"), giving personal advice ("exercise helps me"), or false reassurance
• Key technique: Reflective listening + offering professional support

HIPAA Correct Action:
• Option 2 is correct: Verify authorization before releasing ANY PHI
• NEVER assume family involvement equals authorization
• Document the inquiry and your response in the patient's record
• Appropriate response: "I need to verify your mother's authorization first. Would you like me to have her call you?"

Clinical Documentation: Always document patient education provided, safety concerns addressed (suicidal ideation screening), and HIPAA-related inquiries. For EEG prep, document which instructions were given and patient understanding.

🧠 Neurological Assessment Insights

  • CNS vs PNS: CNS = brain/spinal cord (cognitive, consciousness, motor control). PNS = peripheral nerves (sensation, reflexes, muscle strength). Accurate differentiation guides diagnostic testing and treatment approaches.
  • Stroke Recognition: TIME IS BRAIN. Know FAST: Face, Arm, Speech, Time. Document exact symptom onset time - it determines thrombolytic therapy eligibility (3-4.5 hour window). Key: "Sudden" onset distinguishes emergency from chronic conditions.
  • EEG vs Other Procedures: EEG requires hair prep (no products), doesn't require fasting/contrast. Sleep deprivation may be ordered. Document all patient education and ensure understanding of what's NOT required.
  • Seizure Safety: During seizure: Time it, protect from injury (move objects), turn on side (recovery position), NEVER put anything in mouth. Document duration and characteristics.

💬 Mental Health Communication & Ethics

  • Therapeutic Communication: Use reflective listening ("It sounds like..."), validate feelings, avoid advice-giving. Person-first language reduces stigma ("person with schizophrenia" not "schizophrenic").
  • Safety Assessment: MUST ask about suicidal/homicidal ideation with depressed patients. Document exact wording of patient response. Know your facility's emergency protocols.
  • HIPAA in Neurology/Mental Health: Extra sensitivity required. Family involvement doesn't equal authorization. Verify release forms for EVERY disclosure, even to involved family members.
  • Documentation Standards: Record mental status exam findings objectively. Quote patient statements when possible. Document safety assessments, education provided, and follow-up plans.

⚠️ Critical Safety Alerts

  • STAT Response Required: Stroke symptoms (FAST criteria), status epilepticus (seizure >5 minutes), sudden severe headache ("worst headache of life"), acute mental status changes.
  • Mandatory Reporting: Elder abuse, child abuse, domestic violence, Tarasoff duty (threat to identifiable person). Know your state's specific requirements.
  • Procedure Errors: Don't confuse EEG prep with MRI/CT instructions. EEG: hair prep important. MRI: no metal, possible contrast. CT: possible contrast, possible fasting.
