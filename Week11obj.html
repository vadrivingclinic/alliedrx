Cardiopulmonary Assessment & Care

Pulmonary Diagnostics • ECG Procedures • Respiratory Treatment

Week 11 combines comprehensive pulmonary medicine with advanced electrocardiography skills. You'll develop expertise in respiratory assessment and treatment while mastering ECG procedures, creating essential competencies for managing patients with cardiopulmonary conditions.

These chapters connect through cardiopulmonary interdependence. Respiratory conditions affect cardiac function, while cardiac disorders impact pulmonary status. Understanding both systems is essential for comprehensive patient assessment and management of conditions like COPD with cardiac complications.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Perform comprehensive pulmonary function testing including spirometry and pulse oximetry.
  • Administer respiratory treatments including nebulizer therapy and inhaler education.
  • Perform accurate 12-lead ECG procedures with proper electrode placement.
  • Recognize common cardiac arrhythmias and pulmonary disease patterns.
  • Educate patients on cardiopulmonary disease management and treatment adherence.
COPD Management: For COPD patients, teach pursed-lip breathing to improve oxygen exchange. Use peak flow monitoring daily to track disease progression. Administer bronchodilators 15-30 minutes before meals to improve breathing during eating. Always have rescue inhalers accessible.
ECG Electrode Placement: Use the "White on Right, Smoke over Fire" mnemonic for limb leads: White (RA) on right arm, Black (LA) on left arm, Red (LL) on left leg, Green (RL) on right leg. For chest leads, V1: 4th intercostal space right sternal border; V2: 4th ICS left sternal border; V4: 5th ICS midclavicular line.
Pulse Oximetry Accuracy: For accurate SpO2 readings: Clean probe site (remove nail polish), warm cold extremities, position probe correctly (light source and detector opposite). Normal range: 95-100%. Below 90% requires immediate intervention. Remember: carbon monoxide poisoning gives false high readings.
ECG Artifact Reduction: Minimize artifacts by: Cleaning skin with alcohol, shaving hairy areas, ensuring good electrode contact, keeping patient still, positioning limbs comfortably. Common artifacts: 60-cycle interference (wiggly baseline), wandering baseline (poor contact), muscle tremor (jagged lines).
