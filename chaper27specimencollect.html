QC Rules (Westgard Rules):
• 1₃₅: One point outside ±3SD - REJECT
• 2₂₅: Two consecutive points outside ±2SD - REJECT
• R₄₅: Range of 4 points exceeds 4SD - REJECT
• 4₁₅: Four consecutive points outside ±1SD - TREND
• 10ₓ: Ten consecutive points on one side of mean - TREND

Patient Safety: Never report patient results when QC is out of control. Document all troubleshooting steps and repeat QC until in control before resuming testing.

🩺 Clinical Laboratory Excellence

  • Patient-Centered Care: Adapt communication for arthritis (assistive devices), language barriers (visual aids), and adolescent confidentiality (private explanation). Always maintain dignity during specimen collection.
  • Chain of Custody: For legal specimens (drug tests, paternity), document every handler from collection to disposal. Use tamper-evident seals and require signatures at each transfer point.
  • Quality Control Documentation: Maintain daily QC logs with corrective actions. Document reagent lot numbers, expiration dates, and equipment maintenance. Out-of-control results require investigation before patient testing.
  • Specimen Integrity: Process STAT specimens within 30 minutes, routine within 2 hours. Maintain proper storage temperatures (2-8°C for most). Label specimens at bedside with two patient identifiers.
  • Critical Values: Report immediately to ordering provider and document time, method, and person notified. Common critical values: glucose <50 or >400 mg/dL, potassium <2.5 or >6.5 mEq/L, sodium <120 or >160 mEq/L.

📋 Documentation & Legal Compliance

  • CLIA Requirements: Waived tests still require training documentation. Moderate complexity tests require biannual competency assessment. High complexity requires specialized training and certification.
  • OSHA Compliance: Annual bloodborne pathogen training, accessible Safety Data Sheets, documented exposure control plan. Report needlestick injuries within 24 hours.
  • Result Reporting: Document normal/abnormal result communication. Use approved abbreviations only. Never use pencil - all entries must be in permanent ink with corrections properly made (single line, initial, date).
  • Equipment Records: Log daily temperature checks, weekly cleaning, monthly calibration, and annual maintenance. Document out-of-service equipment and alternative testing arrangements.
