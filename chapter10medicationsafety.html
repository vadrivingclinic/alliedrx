💡 Critical Thinking Tips
  • Critical information is needed for EVERY dose: Drug name, strength, expiration, and route.
  • Supporting information is for inventory, tracking, or reference but doesn't affect the immediate dose.
  • Always verify expiration dates - expired medications lose potency or become toxic.
  • Convert units before calculating: g → mg, mL → L, etc. Write out your conversions to avoid errors.

🧮 Pediatric Dosage & Injectable Reconstitution

Calculate safe pediatric doses and practice reconstituting powdered injectable medications. Apply weight-based dosing formulas correctly.

Scenario 1: Pediatric Weight-Based Dosing
Dr. Angio orders Amoxicillin 25 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses for a child weighing 15 kg. The pharmacy supplies Amoxicillin suspension 125 mg/5 mL.
Step-by-Step Calculation:
1. Calculate total daily dose: 25 mg/kg/day × 15 kg = ? mg/day
2. Divide by 3 doses: ? mg/day ÷ 3 = ? mg/dose
3. Convert to mL using concentration: 125 mg/5 mL = 25 mg/mL
Scenario 2: Injectable Powder Reconstitution
Reconstitute Ceftriaxone 1 g vial for IM injection. Instructions: Add 2.1 mL of sterile water to yield 350 mg/mL. The provider orders 750 mg IM.
Reconstitution Steps:
1. Add 2.1 mL diluent to vial (creates 350 mg/mL concentration)
2. Calculate volume for 750 mg dose
3. Round to nearest measurable amount on syringe
💡 Pediatric & Reconstitution Safety Tips
  • Pediatric doses are weight-based: Always verify current weight in kg.
  • Double-check calculations: Pediatric dosing errors have more severe consequences.
  • Use oral syringes for accuracy: Never use household spoons for medication measurement.
  • Reconstitution tip: Gently roll the vial, don't shake vigorously, to avoid foaming.
  • Label immediately: After reconstitution, label with drug name, concentration, date, time, and your initials.
🚨
CRITICAL SAFETY CHECKPOINTS
• Pediatric doses require two-person verification in most facilities.
• Reconstituted medications have short stability times (check package insert).
• Document lot numbers and expiration for all injectable medications.
• NEVER administer a dose you haven't independently verified.
✓ Dosage Calculations Mastered!
Scenario 1 Solution (Pediatric Dose):

2. 375 mg ÷ 3 doses = 125 mg/dose
3. 125 mg ÷ 25 mg/mL = 5 mL per dose
• Correct device: Oral syringe (most accurate for liquid medications)

Scenario 2 Solution (Reconstitution):
1. 750 mg ÷ 350 mg/mL = 2.14 mL
2. Rounded to measurable amount: 2.1 mL
3. Correct syringe: 3 mL syringe (allows accurate measurement to 0.1 mL)

Clinical Reasoning: TB syringes (1 mL) are most accurate for small volumes (<1 mL). For 2.1 mL, a 3 mL syringe provides adequate accuracy while allowing room for proper administration technique. Always round to the nearest measurable increment on your chosen syringe.

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Label Reading: Critical information (name, strength, expiration, route) must be verified against the provider's order for EVERY dose. Supporting information (NDC, lot#, manufacturer) is for tracking and inventory.
  • Unit Conversion: Always convert to the same units before calculating. Common error: confusing grams (g) with milligrams (mg). Remember: 1 g = 1000 mg.
  • Pediatric Dosing: Weight-based dosing requires current weight in kilograms. Double-check calculations and use appropriate measuring devices (oral syringes, not household spoons).
  • Reconstitution: Follow manufacturer's instructions exactly. Label immediately after mixing with drug name, concentration, date, time, expiration, and your initials.

📋 Documentation & Legal Compliance

  • Verification Required: High-risk medications (pediatric doses, insulin, anticoagulants) require two-person verification. Document both signatures.
  • Error Reporting: All calculation errors or near-misses must be reported through your facility's incident reporting system, regardless of outcome.
  • Competency Validation: Pharmacology math competency must be validated annually. Maintain documentation of successful completion.
  • Scope of Practice: Medical assistants may calculate doses but cannot independently determine or modify prescribed doses. Always follow provider orders exactly.
