PEDIATRIC SAFETY ALERT
Children are NOT small adults! Their metabolism, organ function, and body composition differ significantly. Always use pediatric-specific dosing guidelines and double-check calculations. Consider using the more conservative dose when methods give different results.
✓ Pediatric Dosing Mastered!
Correct Calculations:
• BSA Method: (0.68 ÷ 1.73) × 500mg = 196.5mg every 8 hours
• Weight-Based Method: 18kg × 25mg/kg/day = 450mg/day ÷ 3 = 150mg every 8 hours

Key Differences:
• BSA Method: More accurate for medications that distribute in body water or fat
• Weight-Based: Simpler, commonly used for antibiotics and emergency drugs
• Age-Based: Sometimes used for vaccines and some medications

Clinical Decision: For this case, the weight-based method (150mg) is more conservative/safer. However, the provider would consider infection severity, patient history, and local guidelines before deciding.

🩺 Pediatric & Reconstitution Mastery

  • Pediatric Dosing Principles: Children have different pharmacokinetics - faster metabolism, higher body water percentage, immature organ systems. Always use pediatric-specific references and calculate based on weight or BSA, never age alone.
  • BSA vs Weight-Based: BSA method (Mosteller formula: √[height(cm)×weight(kg)/3600]) is more accurate for drugs with narrow therapeutic windows. Weight-based is standard for antibiotics. When in doubt, use the more conservative dose.
  • Reconstitution Accuracy: Powder adds volume! Final volume = diluent volume + powder displacement. Always roll, don't shake, to prevent foaming and protein denaturation. Label immediately with concentration, date, time, and expiration.
  • Table Interpretation: Drug reference tables provide critical information: concentration options, stability data, compatibility, and administration guidelines. Choose concentration based on dose accuracy needs and storage requirements.
  • Measurement Precision: For small pediatric doses, use smaller syringes (1mL insulin syringes for accuracy to 0.01mL). Less concentrated solutions allow more precise measurement of small volumes.

📋 Documentation & Safety Protocols

  • Double-Check System: All pediatric doses and reconstituted medications require independent double-check by another qualified healthcare professional before administration.
  • Expiration Tracking: Reconstituted medications have short stability. Implement a system to track expiration times (room temp vs refrigerated) and discard appropriately.
  • Competency Validation: Pediatric medication competency must be validated separately from adult competencies. Include BSA calculations, weight-based dosing, and reconstitution skills in annual competency assessments.
  • Parent Education: When parents administer medications at home, provide clear instructions, demonstrate measurement, and use teach-back method. Provide oral syringes for liquid medications.
