OSHA Needlestick Prevention
Never recap needles! Use safety-engineered devices when available. If recapping is absolutely necessary, use one-handed scoop technique only. Report ALL needlesticks immediately - document incident, source patient, and follow facility exposure control plan.
✓ Equipment Competency Achieved
Equipment Selection Guidelines:

1. Needle Gauge: Lower number = larger bore. 18-21G for IM, 22-25G for SubQ, 25-27G for ID. Viscous medications need larger gauge (18-20G).

2. Needle Length: IM: 1-1.5" (ventrogluteal/dorsogluteal), 1" (deltoid). SubQ: 3/8-5/8". ID: 1/4-1/2". Consider patient size and injection site.

3. Syringe Types: Standard (3mL, 5mL) for IM, Tuberculin (1mL) for precise small doses, Insulin (units) for insulin only.

4. OSHA Compliance: Use safety-engineered devices, immediate sharps disposal, no recapping, proper PPE, and exposure incident reporting.
💉 Clinical Pro Tip: When drawing from a vial, inject EQUAL volume of air before withdrawing medication. This prevents negative pressure and makes withdrawal easier, especially with viscous medications.

Injection Technique Critical
Aspiration is controversial! Current guidelines: Aspirate for IM ONLY if giving medication that could cause tissue necrosis if injected intravenously (e.g., vaccines no, medications maybe). Check facility policy. Always aspirate when giving medications that are NOT vaccines.
✓ Parenteral Technique Mastery
Parenteral Administration Standards:

1. Intramuscular (IM): 90° angle. Sites: Deltoid (0.5-1mL), Ventrogluteal (up to 3mL - safest), Vastus lateralis (up to 2mL, pediatrics), Dorsogluteal (avoid - sciatic nerve risk).

2. Subcutaneous (SubQ): 45° or 90° (depending on needle length). Sites: Abdomen (best absorption), Thigh, Upper arm. Volumes: Usually 0.5-1mL max per site.

3. Intradermal (ID): 10-15° angle. Site: Ventral forearm. Volume: 0.1mL max. MUST see wheal (6-10mm). Used for TB tests, allergies.

4. Z-track Technique: For irritating medications (iron, some antibiotics). Pull skin laterally, inject, wait 10 seconds, release skin. Prevents tracking and staining.
🩹 Clinical Pro Tip: When giving multiple injections, administer in this order: 1) Intradermal (PPD), 2) Subcutaneous (heparin), 3) Intramuscular (vaccine). This prevents medication tracking between layers and maintains test validity.

💉 Technical Competency Insights

  • Needle Gauge Selection: 18-20G for viscous IM, 21-23G for standard IM, 25G for SubQ insulin, 26-27G for SubQ other meds, 27G for ID. Larger gauge = smaller number.
  • Site Rotation Critical: Document injection sites! For frequent injections (insulin, heparin), rotate systematically: Clockwise around abdomen, alternating thighs/arms. Prevents lipohypertrophy.
  • Volume Limitations: Deltoid: 0.5-1mL adult, 0.5mL child. Ventrogluteal: 1-3mL adult (preferred for volumes >1mL). Vastus lateralis: 0.5-2mL based on age/size. Never exceed recommended volumes.
  • Z-track Technique: Required for: Iron dextran, certain antibiotics, antipsychotics. Pull skin 1-1.5 inches, inject, wait 10 seconds, release. Prevents medication tracking to surface.
  • PPD Administration: MUST be intradermal. 0.1mL producing 6-10mm wheal. Read at 48-72 hours. Document in mm of induration, not erythema. Patient must return for reading.

🛡️ OSHA & Safety Compliance

  • Sharps Safety: Use safety-engineered devices. Dispose immediately in puncture-resistant container at point of use. Never recap, bend, or break needles.
  • Exposure Control Plan: Know facility plan. Report ALL exposures immediately. Document: Date/time, procedure, device, injury details, source patient, your actions.
  • PPE Requirements: Gloves for all parenteral procedures. Face shield if splashing possible. Gown if soiling likely. Change gloves between patients and when contaminated.
  • Medication Safety: Double-check: Right patient, medication, dose, route, time. Have another nurse verify high-alert medications (insulin, heparin, opioids).
  • Documentation: Document: Medication, dose, route, site, lot #, expiration, your name/credentials, patient response. For vaccines: Document VIS date and patient receipt.
