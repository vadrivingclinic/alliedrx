🚨 Duty to Report: 5 Scenarios

Evaluate each situation and determine the mandatory reporting action.

Scenario 1: Physical Signs 1/5
3-year-old with multiple bruises in various stages of healing on torso and back of ears. Parent says "he fell."
Confront the parent immediately.
Document objectively and notify provider; suspicion requires reporting.
Wait for next visit to confirm.
Scenario 2: Medical Neglect 2/5
7-year-old with severe untreated dental decay causing pain/infection. Parents refuse treatment stating "they are baby teeth anyway."
Report as medical neglect; pain/infection requires treatment.
Accept parent's decision; it's their right.
Scenario 3: Emotional Indicators 3/5
4-year-old flinches when arm is raised. Parent constantly yells "You're stupid" and "I wish you weren't born" during visit.
Ignore; parenting styles differ.
Document behavioral observations; emotional abuse is reportable.
Scenario 4: Sexual Abuse Signs 4/5
12-year-old complains of difficulty walking/sitting. Exam reveals stained underwear and bruising on inner thighs.
Follow immediate sexual abuse protocol (exam/collection); report immediately.
Ask the parent to explain the staining.
Scenario 5: Nutritional Neglect 5/5
6-month-old losing weight (Failure to Thrive). Parent admits stretching formula with extra water to save money.
Report immediately to police.
Refer to social work/WIC first; report if resources refused/failure continues.
✓ Duty to Report Verified!
Mandatory Reporting Principles:
• Reasonable Suspicion: You do not need proof, only a reasonable belief.
• Immunity: Reporters are protected from liability when reporting in good faith.
• Neglect: Includes failure to provide medical care, food, shelter, or supervision.
• Documentation: Quote the patient/parent directly. Describe injuries objectively (size, color, shape).

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Disorder Classification: Distinguishing infectious causes dictates isolation protocols immediately upon arrival.
  • Immunization Logic: Understanding the "DIMV" schedule (DTaP, IPV, MMR, Varicella) creates confidence when counseling parents.
  • Abuse Recognition: Bruising on soft tissue (torso, ears) is highly suspicious compared to bony prominences (shins).

📋 Key Learning Points

  • Vaccine Sites: Infants/Toddlers = Vastus Lateralis. Children >3 = Deltoid.
  • Mandatory Reporting: Medical assistants are mandated reporters. Immunity is granted for good faith reporting.
  • SIDS Prevention: "Alone, Back, Crib" remains the gold standard education for all infants <1 year.
