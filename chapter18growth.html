🧠 Theorist in Action

Apply developmental theories (Erikson/Piaget) to solve patient cooperation issues. Select the best approach for Susie.

Scenario 1: The "No!" Toddler
A 2-year-old refuses to sit on the scale. According to Erikson (Autonomy vs. Shame), what is the best strategy?
Force them onto the scale quickly to get it over with.
Offer a choice: "Do you want to take off your shoes or your coat first?"
Tell them "Good boys sit still" to shame them into compliance.
Scenario 2: The Logical School-Ager
A 7-year-old is asking about their flu shot. According to Piaget (Concrete Operational), how should you explain it?
Be honest: "It will pinch for a second, but it helps your body fight germs."
Use abstract metaphors: "It's like a magic shield."
Lie: "It won't hurt at all."
✓ Theory Applied!
Clinical Application:
• Toddlers need perceived control. Forced choices (this or that) build autonomy.
• School-age children value logic and cause-and-effect. Honesty builds trust; lying destroys it forever.

Key Differences:
• Well-Child: Focus is on growth, development, prevention (vaccines), and safety education.
• Sick-Child: Focus is on the chief complaint (CC), diagnosis, and immediate treatment.
Note: A sick child may be rescheduled for their well-visit procedures (like vaccines) until they recover.

🚩 Recognizing Red Flags

Analyze the vignette to identify the critical clinical concern. Select the correct assessment.

Vignette 1: The Quiet Mother
During a 2-month checkup, the mother avoids eye contact with the baby, speaks in a flat monotone, and says, "He cries all the time. I can't do anything right." The baby is clean but quiet.
Assess for failure to thrive in the infant.
Screen mother for Postpartum Depression (PPD) immediately.
Document "normal maternal fatigue."
Vignette 2: The Regression
A 3-year-old who was fully potty trained for 6 months has started wetting their pants daily and using "baby talk" since their new sister arrived last week.
Normal regression due to sibling rivalry/stress. Reassure parents.
Urgent referral for developmental delay.
Test immediately for urinary tract infection.
✓ Assessments Correct!
Clinical Insights:
• PPD: Maternal depression affects infant bonding and development. Screening is a vital part of pediatric care.
• Regression: Common during stress (new sibling, moving). It is usually temporary. However, loss of skills *without* a stressor is a major red flag for neurological issues.

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Triage Competence: Understanding milestones allows you to distinguish between a worried parent ("My 9-month-old isn't walking") and a true delay ("My 18-month-old isn't walking").
  • Communication Strategy: Tailoring your approach to the child's developmental stage (choices for toddlers, honesty for school-age) reduces procedure time and anxiety.
  • Family-Centered Care: Recognizing PPD or sibling stress acknowledges that the patient exists within a family unit. Treating the family helps the child.

📋 Key Learning Points

  • Erikson's Stages: Trust (Infant) → Autonomy (Toddler) → Initiative (Preschool) → Industry (School) → Identity (Teen).
  • Well-Child Visits: Follow a specific periodicity schedule (e.g., 2mo, 4mo, 6mo) focused on growth, vaccines, and anticipatory guidance.
  • Denver II: A standardized tool used to screen for developmental delays in personal-social, fine motor, language, and gross motor domains.
