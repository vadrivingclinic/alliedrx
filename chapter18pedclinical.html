📞 Advanced Telephone Triage

Handle 3 difficult calls. Choose the safest, most professional response.

Call 1: The "Teething" Fever

Mother: "My 4-month-old has a fever of 101°F. My mom says it's just teething, so I shouldn't worry, right?"

"Yes, teething causes fevers. Give Tylenol." (Incorrect: Teething rarely causes fever >100.4)
"A fever over 100.4°F in an infant under 6 months needs evaluation. Please come in." (Correct)
"Wait 24 hours and call back if it goes higher." (Dangerous delay)
Call 2: Dehydration Risk

Father: "My 3-year-old has been vomiting all night. He hasn't peed in 12 hours and looks pale."

"Try giving sips of Gatorade every 15 minutes." (Incorrect: Gatorade has too much sugar/wrong electrolytes)
"No urine for 12 hours is a sign of severe dehydration. Go to the ER immediately." (Correct)
Call 3: Medication Error

Parent: "I think I gave my 5-year-old a double dose of liquid antibiotic by mistake."

"Skip the next dose and he'll be fine." (Practicing medicine without license)
"Please hold while I consult the provider immediately. Do you have the bottle handy?" (Correct)
✓ Triage Complete
Telephone triage requires caution. When in doubt, always escalate to the provider or recommend evaluation. Never guess on medication errors.

⚖️ Ethics & Legal Dilemmas

Navigate complex legal scenarios involving minors.

Case 1: Divorced Parents

A non-custodial father asks for his daughter's medical records. The mother (custodial parent) said "don't give him anything."

Refuse. The custodial parent's word is final.
Check the legal decree in the chart. Usually, non-custodial parents still have right to records unless rights were terminated.
Case 2: The "Mature Minor"

A 16-year-old requests birth control and asks that her parents not be billed.

"I have to bill the insurance on file, which goes to your dad."
"We can provide confidential care, but we need to discuss how billing/EOBs might alert them."
Case 3: Vaccine Refusal

Parents refuse all vaccines for their infant based on internet blogs. They want you to sign a school exemption form.

Refer to provider. You cannot sign exemptions or debate medical policy, but you should document the refusal form.
Sign the form to keep the peace; it's their choice.
✓ Ethical Standards Met
Pediatric ethics balances parental rights, patient privacy (especially teens), and the child's safety. Always defer to legal documents (custody papers) rather than verbal instructions.

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • ● Math Accuracy: Pediatric dosing leaves zero room for error. Always convert lbs to kg correctly (2.2) before provider calculation.
  • ● Urinalysis: A contaminated bag specimen is useless. The "clean-dry-seal" technique prevents false positives for infection.
  • ● Triage Safety: "Dehydration" and "High Fever in Infants" are immediate red flags. When in doubt, bring them in.

📋 Key Learning Points

  • ● Cuff Size: Bladder width must cover 40% of arm circumference; length 80-100%. Incorrect size invalidates the reading.
  • ● Teens: Confidentiality regarding reproductive health is protected by law in most states, even from parents.
  • ● Documentation: For refusals, always have parents sign a "Refusal to Vaccinate" waiver to protect the practice legally.
