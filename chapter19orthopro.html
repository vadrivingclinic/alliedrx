Cast Application Sequence

Drag cast application steps into the correct sequence. Proper casting technique prevents complications and ensures optimal healing for patients with fractures.

🩹 Critical Safety Protocol
CMS Checks (Circulation, Motor, Sensation): Must be documented BEFORE and AFTER cast application. Any change requires immediate intervention. Check capillary refill (<2 seconds), motor function (wiggle fingers/toes), and sensation (light touch recognition).
⚠️ Emergency Warning Signs
Compartment Syndrome (Surgical Emergency): Teach patients the 6 P's: Pain (out of proportion, unrelieved by elevation), Pallor, Paresthesia (numbness/tingling), Paralysis, Pulselessness (late sign), Poikilothermia (cool extremity). Requires immediate fasciotomy.
✓ Cast Application Sequence Correct!
🔍 Correct Cast Application Sequence:

1. Apply stockinette to protect skin
• Extends 2-3 inches beyond intended cast margins
• Prevents skin irritation from rough cast edges
• Provides smooth surface for padding application

2. Add cotton padding evenly (2 layers)
• Apply with 50% overlap for uniform thickness
• Extra padding over bony prominences (malleoli, styloid processes)
• Avoid wrinkles that create pressure points
• Should extend 1-2 cm beyond stockinette edges

3. Immerse plaster/fiberglass strips in water
• Plaster: Lukewarm water (accelerates setting)
• Fiberglass: Room temperature water
• Squeeze gently – don't wring out completely
• Ready when dripping stops (plaster) or activation complete (fiberglass)

4. Apply strips with 50% overlap (no wrinkles)
• Start at most distal point, work proximally
• Smooth each layer before adding next
• Maintain uniform thickness (6-10 layers)
• Use extra reinforcement at stress points (joints)

5. Smooth and shape while still malleable
• Use palms, not fingertips, to avoid indentations
• Contour to anatomical landmarks
• Ensure proper alignment of fracture/joint
• Trim edges neatly when semi-set

6. Allow to dry completely (24-48 hours)
• Plaster: 24-48 hours for full strength
• Fiberglass: 15-30 minutes to harden, 24 hours for full cure
• Keep elevated on pillows to prevent dependent edema
• Use fan for faster drying (not heat – causes burns)

7. Provide written cast care instructions
• Include warning signs requiring immediate care
• Demonstrate elevation technique
• Show how to check capillary refill
• Provide waterproofing options (plastic bags, cast covers)

📋 Documentation Essentials:
• Date/time of application
• Type of cast (short arm, long leg, etc.)
• Material used (plaster vs fiberglass)
• Position of immobilization
• Pre/post CMS findings
• Patient education provided
• Follow-up appointment scheduled

🎯 Key Teaching Points for Patients:
• Keep cast dry – use plastic bag for showers
• No weight-bearing unless specifically instructed
• Elevate above heart level for first 48 hours
• Do not insert objects under cast (risk of skin breakdown/infection)
• Schedule follow-up in 1-2 weeks for cast check
• Contact provider immediately for: increased pain, swelling, numbness, discoloration, foul odor, or fever

Rehabilitation Protocol Matching

Drag each rehabilitation exercise to the correct phase of rotator cuff repair recovery. Understanding progression prevents re-injury and optimizes healing.

Phase 1: Immediate Post-op (0-4 weeks)
Phase 2: Early Active (4-8 weeks)
Phase 3: Strengthening (8-12 weeks)
Phase 4: Functional (12+ weeks)
✓ Rehabilitation Protocol Mastered!
🔍 Detailed Rehabilitation Protocol Analysis:

Phase 1: Immediate Post-op (0-4 weeks)
• Sling immobilization (except exercises): Worn continuously except during prescribed exercises. Prevents stress on repair. Should be snug but not constrictive.
• Pendulum exercises (Codman's): Gravity-assisted motion. Patient bends at waist, lets arm hang, makes small circles. 10-15 circles clockwise/counterclockwise, 3x daily.
• Passive ROM with therapist: Therapist moves joint through available range. No active muscle contraction by patient. Focus on forward flexion, abduction, external rotation.
• Goals: Protect surgical repair, control pain and swelling, prevent adhesions, maintain distal joint mobility.

Phase 2: Early Active (4-8 weeks)
• Active-assisted ROM: Patient uses opposite hand or pulley to assist movement. Gradual transition from passive to active motion.
• Begin gentle AROM within pain limits: Patient initiates movement without assistance. Pain should not exceed 4/10. Stop if sharp pain occurs.
• Scapular stabilization: Retraction, protraction, elevation, depression. Strengthens scapular muscles that support shoulder function.
• Goals: Restore range of motion, improve scapulohumeral rhythm, begin gentle strengthening.

Phase 3: Strengthening (8-12 weeks)
• Light resistance bands: Start with lightest resistance (yellow theraband). Progress as tolerated. Focus on controlled movements.
• Isometric strengthening: Muscle contraction without joint movement. Push against immovable object. Builds strength without stressing repair.
• Goals: Restore muscle strength, improve endurance, prepare for functional activities.

Phase 4: Functional (12+ weeks)
• Sport-specific drills: Mimics movements required for patient's sport/activity (throwing for baseball, serving for tennis).
• Return to full activity: Gradual reintegration to pre-injury level. Monitor for pain or weakness that suggests incomplete recovery.
• Goals: Return to pre-injury functional level, prevent re-injury, maintain long-term shoulder health.

⚠️ Critical Precautions:
• No lifting >5 lbs for first 6 weeks
• Avoid overhead activities for 3 months
• No pushing/pulling activities for 8 weeks
• Ice after exercises for swelling control (20 minutes on, 40 off)
• Report increased pain, popping sensation, or sudden weakness immediately

📊 Progression Criteria:
• Move to next phase when: pain controlled, ROM goals met, strength adequate for current phase
• Healing confirmed by surgeon at follow-up visits (typically 2, 6, 12 weeks)
• X-ray confirmation of bone healing if tuberosity repair was performed

🎯 Patient Education Essentials:
• Importance of compliance with restrictions
• Proper sling use and skin care under sling
• Sleep positioning (often reclined with pillow support)
• Medication management for pain control
• Signs of complications (infection, nerve injury, repair failure)
• Realistic timeline for return to work/sport (4-6 months for full recovery)

🩹 Clinical Insights: Casting & Rehabilitation

  • Cast Application Safety: Always check for pressure points at bony prominences (malleoli, styloid processes). Cast should be snug but not constrictive. Document CMS checks pre/post application. Use the "two-finger rule" – should be able to fit two fingers between cast and skin at proximal edge.
  • Compartment Syndrome: Teach patients the 6 P's: Pain (out of proportion), Pallor, Paresthesia, Paralysis, Pulselessness (late sign), Poikilothermia. This is a surgical emergency requiring immediate fasciotomy. Pain with passive stretch is the earliest and most reliable sign.
  • Rehabilitation Timing: Rotator cuff repairs: No active motion for 4-6 weeks. Total knee replacement: Begin ROM same day with CPM machine. Spinal fusion: No bending/lifting/twisting for 3 months (BLT restrictions).
  • Patient Education: Provide written instructions with pictures. Use teach-back method. Include warning signs requiring immediate medical attention. Schedule follow-up before discharge. Document comprehension in chart.
  • Documentation Essentials: Record cast material (plaster vs fiberglass), number of layers, position of immobilization, CMS findings, patient education provided, and follow-up plan. Use objective measurements (degrees of motion, mm of swelling).

📋 Orthopedic Procedure Documentation

  • Pre-procedure: Verify consent, allergies, NPO status if sedation planned. Confirm correct limb/site with patient (mark site per protocol). Document baseline neurovascular status. Check for contraindications (open wounds, infection).
  • During procedure: Record start/end time, materials used (size, type, lot # if implant), patient tolerance, any complications (vasovagal response, skin compromise), and provider performing procedure. Note any deviations from standard protocol.
  • Post-procedure: Document post-procedure CMS, cast drying time, elevation instructions, pain management plan, and return precautions. Schedule follow-up appointments before patient leaves.
  • Quality Indicators: Monitor infection rates, cast complications, patient satisfaction scores, and time to follow-up. Track readmission rates for cast-related issues.
