Key EEG Preparation Points:
• Sleep deprivation: 4-5 hours only - increases chance of capturing abnormal activity
• No hair products: Oils and conditioners interfere with electrode contact
• Continue medications: Unless specifically instructed to hold
• No fasting: Low blood sugar can alter brain waves

Therapeutic Communication Skills:

• Provide accurate information about sensations and safety
• Avoid false reassurance and personal anecdotes

MA Role in Diagnostic Procedures:
• EEG: Patient education, electrode preparation, assist with hyperventilation/photic stimulation

💡 Neurological Assessment Tip
Always compare bilaterally! Neurological findings are most meaningful when compared side-to-side. Document "within normal limits bilaterally" or note specific asymmetries. Use standard grading scales for reflexes (0-4+) and muscle strength (0-5).

MA Role in Neurological Exam:
• Before: Room prep, equipment readiness, vital signs, consent verification
• During: Patient positioning, instrument handling, response observation
• After: Patient assistance, documentation, follow-up scheduling

Abnormal Finding Recognition:
• Ataxia: Uncoordinated movement → Cerebellar dysfunction
• Diplopia: Double vision → Cranial nerve III, IV, VI issues
• Hyperreflexia: Increased reflexes → Upper motor neuron lesion
• Romberg positive: Falls with eyes closed → Proprioception loss

Patient Education Priorities:
• Fall prevention: Remove hazards, install safety equipment
• Medication adherence: Use pill organizers, set reminders
• Symptom monitoring: Teach warning signs requiring urgent care
• Rehabilitation: Encourage prescribed exercises and therapies

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • EEG Preparation: Sleep deprivation (4-5 hours) increases yield for seizure detection. No hair products ensure proper electrode contact. Document any medications that could affect results.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Use empathy statements, provide accurate procedural information, avoid false reassurance. For anxious patients: "I understand this might feel overwhelming. Let me explain what to expect step-by-step."
  • Neurological Exam Techniques: Always compare bilaterally. Use standardized scales (reflexes 0-4+, strength 0-5). Document "within normal limits" or specific abnormalities with location and severity.
  • Abnormal Finding Recognition: Ataxia = cerebellar, Diplopia = cranial nerves III/IV/VI, Hyperreflexia = upper motor neuron, Romberg positive = proprioception loss. Report significant findings immediately.
  • Patient Safety Education: Fall prevention is priority #1 for neurological patients. Remove throw rugs, install grab bars, ensure proper lighting. Use teach-back method to verify understanding.

📋 Documentation & Procedure Protocols

  • Pre-procedure Checklist: Verify consent, NPO status if required, medication holds, allergy status, pregnancy screening (for imaging), and metal screening (for MRI).
  • Intra-procedure Assistance: Maintain sterile field for LPs, assist with patient positioning, monitor vital signs during stressful procedures, provide emotional support.
  • Post-procedure Care: Monitor for complications (headache after LP, contrast reactions after imaging), provide discharge instructions, schedule follow-up, document patient tolerance and outcomes.
  • Equipment Competency: Maintain and calibrate neurological instruments (tuning forks, reflex hammers, monofilaments). Ensure EEG electrodes are properly stored and maintained.
