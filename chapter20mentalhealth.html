💡 Clinical Assessment Tip
Always assess for safety risks in mental health presentations: suicidal ideation, homicidal thoughts, self-harm behaviors, and ability to care for self. Use standardized screening tools (PHQ-9 for depression, GAD-7 for anxiety) when available. Document specific quotes from patients when reporting symptoms.
✓ Mental Health Recognition Complete!
Key Differentiations:
• Major Depressive Disorder: ≥2 weeks of depressed mood/anhedonia + other symptoms
• Dementia vs Depression: Progressive decline vs mood-related "pseudodementia"
• Agoraphobia: Fear of situations where escape might be difficult/help unavailable
• Anxiety Disorders: Excessive fear/anxiety that causes functional impairment

Impact on Patient Interaction:
• Depression: May have psychomotor retardation, poor eye contact, flat affect
• Anxiety: May need longer appointments, clear explanations, reduced stimuli
• Dementia: Use simple language, repeat instructions, involve caregivers
• All patients: Maintain non-judgmental attitude, validate experiences, ensure safety

Screening & Documentation: Use validated tools, document specific behaviors/quotes, assess risk factors, and collaborate with mental health professionals as needed.

TARASOFF DUTY TO WARN
If a patient makes a specific threat against an identifiable person, healthcare providers have a duty to warn the intended victim and/or law enforcement. This duty overrides normal confidentiality rules. Document threats verbatim and follow facility protocols.

Therapeutic Communication Techniques:
• Active listening: Full attention, minimal encouragers
• Reflection: Paraphrase content/emotion ("Sounds like you're feeling...")
• Clarification: Ensure understanding ("Help me understand...")
• Empathy: Validate emotions without judgment
• Open-ended questions: Encourage elaboration ("Tell me more about...")

Legal/Ethical Framework:
• HIPAA: Protects PHI, allows sharing for treatment/payment/operations
• Confidentiality limits: Danger to self/others, abuse reporting, court orders
• Tarasoff duty: Warn identifiable victims of specific threats
• State reporting: Varies for impaired drivers, elder abuse, infectious diseases

Patient Education Approach:
• Provide accurate information about risks/requirements
• Discuss alternatives and resources
• Use teach-back method to verify understanding

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Disorder Differentiation: Major Depressive Disorder requires ≥2 weeks of depressed mood/anhedonia + other symptoms. Dementia shows progressive cognitive decline. Anxiety disorders involve excessive fear causing impairment. Always rule out medical causes first.
  • Therapeutic Communication: Use empathy statements, open-ended questions, reflection, and clarification. Avoid false reassurance, personal anecdotes, and judgmental language. Match communication style to patient's cognitive/emotional state.
  • Safety Assessment: Always screen for suicidal/homicidal ideation, self-harm, and ability to care for self. Use standardized tools (PHQ-9, GAD-7) when available. Document specific quotes and behaviors.
  • Confidentiality Limits: HIPAA protects health information but has exceptions: danger to self/others, abuse reporting (child/elder), court orders, certain public health risks. Know your state's specific reporting laws.
  • Tarasoff Duty: When a patient makes a specific threat against an identifiable person, you must warn the intended victim and/or law enforcement. Document threats verbatim and follow facility protocols immediately.

📋 Documentation & Legal Protocols

  • Sensitive Conversation Documentation: Use direct quotes (in quotation marks), document education provided, note patient response, record safety assessments. Maintain objective, non-judgmental language. Include date, time, and your signature.
  • Mandatory Reporting Procedures: Know state requirements for child/elder abuse, impaired drivers, infectious diseases. Report through proper channels (CPS, APS, DMV, health department). Document what was reported, to whom, and when.
  • Patient Education Documentation: Record topics discussed, materials provided, patient's demonstrated understanding (teach-back method), and any barriers to compliance. Note follow-up plans.
  • Ethical Decision Making: When faced with ethical dilemmas: 1) Identify the problem, 2) Gather relevant information, 3) Consider ethical principles (autonomy, beneficence, non-maleficence, justice), 4) Consult with supervisor/ethics committee, 5) Document the decision-making process.
