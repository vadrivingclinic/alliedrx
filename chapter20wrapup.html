Clinical Knowledge

Common Neurological Disorders

Review these common neurological conditions and their key characteristics:

Cerebrovascular Accident (CVA/Stroke)

Interruption of blood flow to brain causing neurological deficits. Types: ischemic (87%) and hemorrhagic (13%).

FAST: Facial droop, Arm weakness, Speech difficulty, Time to call 911

Epilepsy

Chronic disorder characterized by recurrent seizures. Classified by seizure type: partial or generalized.

Seizures, aura, postictal confusion, automatisms

Multiple Sclerosis (MS)

Autoimmune disorder where immune system attacks myelin sheath of CNS nerves.

Fatigue, numbness, vision problems, muscle weakness, coordination issues

Parkinson's Disease

Progressive neurodegenerative disorder affecting movement control.

Tremor, rigidity, bradykinesia, postural instability

Mai's Neurology Practice Challenge

Mai's Scenario

Mai Lee works in Dr. Song's neurology practice and is training new staff while taking on more patient education responsibilities. She must prepare patients for various neurological diagnostic procedures.

Common Neurological Diagnostic Procedures:

  • Electroencephalogram (EEG)
    Records brain's electrical activity. Preparation: Clean hair, avoid caffeine, may need sleep deprivation.
  • Lumbar Puncture (Spinal Tap)
    Collects cerebrospinal fluid. Position: Lateral recumbent or sitting. Monitor for post-procedure headache.
  • CT Scan (Computed Tomography)
    Cross-sectional brain imaging. May require contrast dye. Check for iodine allergies.
  • MRI (Magnetic Resonance Imaging)
    Detailed brain imaging using magnets. Screen for metal implants. Claustrophobia considerations.
  • EMG/NCS (Electromyogram/Nerve Conduction)
    Assesses muscle and nerve function. Minor discomfort from electrical stimulation.

Mental Health Focus

Common Mental Health Disorders

Medical assistants in neurology often encounter patients with co-occurring mental health conditions:

Major Depressive Disorder

Persistent sadness, loss of interest, changes in sleep/appetite. Suicide risk assessment crucial.

Generalized Anxiety Disorder

Excessive worry, restlessness, muscle tension, sleep disturbance affecting daily functioning.

Bipolar Disorder

Cycling between depressive episodes and manic/hypomanic episodes with elevated mood/energy.

Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder

Following trauma: flashbacks, avoidance, hyperarousal, negative mood/cognition changes.

Therapeutic Communication: Active listening, empathy, non-judgmental attitude, clear explanations, and maintaining appropriate boundaries are essential when working with mental health patients.

Key Terminology

Neurology Vocabulary

Master these essential neurological terms for effective communication and documentation:

Ataxia
Failure or irregularity of muscle actions and coordination; often seen in cerebellar disorders, MS, or stroke.
Aura
Peculiar sensation preceding a more definite disturbance; commonly experienced before migraines or seizures.
Diplopia
Double vision; can be monocular or binocular, often indicating cranial nerve pathology or muscle imbalance.
Blood-Brain Barrier
Structure made of astrocyte glial cells that prevents/slows transfer of chemicals into CNS neurons; affects drug penetration.
