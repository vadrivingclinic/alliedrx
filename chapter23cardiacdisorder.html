Correct Matches:
• Chest pain radiating to left arm = Coronary Artery Disease (CAD)
• Bilateral ankle swelling = Congestive Heart Failure (CHF)
• Calf pain while walking = Peripheral Artery Disease (PAD)
• Irregular heartbeat sensations = Cardiac Arrhythmia
• Extreme fatigue & shortness of breath = Severe Anemia (mimics cardiac)
• Fainting with exertion = Aortic Valve Stenosis
Key Differentiators:
• CHF swelling is bilateral (both ankles)
• CAD pain often radiates to arm/jaw
• PAD pain occurs with walking, relieved by rest
• Syncope with exertion suggests valvular disease

Patient Education: "The swelling in both ankles could mean your heart isn't pumping as efficiently as it should."

Medication Error Identification

Review simulated medication orders and identify potential errors in cardiac prescriptions.

Scenario: You're reviewing medication orders for a 68-year-old patient with hypertension and atrial fibrillation. Identify ALL potential errors in this order set:
Warfarin 10mg daily (patient's usual dose is 5mg)
Lisinopril 20mg + Losartan 50mg (both are ACE/ARBs)
Metoprolol XL 50mg daily (appropriate for rate control)
Aspirin 81mg daily with Warfarin (no documented indication)
Furosemide 80mg BID without potassium supplement
✓ Feedback
Potential Errors Identified:
• Warfarin 10mg daily: Double the usual dose - risk of bleeding
• Lisinopril + Losartan: Duplicate therapy - both affect RAAS system
• Aspirin with Warfarin: Increased bleeding risk without clear indication
• Furosemide 80mg BID: High dose without potassium monitoring/supplement
Cardiology Medication Safety:
1. Warfarin: Check INR before dose changes
2. ACE/ARB combo: Usually contraindicated - increases renal risk
3. Diuretics: Monitor electrolytes, especially potassium
4. Dual antiplatelet/anticoagulant: Requires clear indication & monitoring

Adam's Action: "I would alert the provider about the warfarin dose change and ask about the ACE/ARB combination therapy."

Women's MI Symptoms Recognition

Recognize atypical myocardial infarction symptoms in women, which Adam must understand for patient education.

A 62-year-old female presents with unusual symptoms. Which combination of symptoms is MOST suggestive of myocardial infarction in women?
A
Classic chest pain radiating to left arm
B
Shortness of breath, nausea, fatigue, back/jaw pain
C
Headache and dizziness only
D
Abdominal cramps and diarrhea
💡 Did You Know?
Women are more likely to experience "atypical" MI symptoms. Up to 50% of women having a heart attack don't experience classic chest pain.
✓ Feedback
Correct Answer: B. Shortness of breath, nausea, fatigue, back/jaw pain
Women's Atypical MI Symptoms:
• Shortness of breath (most common atypical symptom)
• Nausea/vomiting
• Unusual fatigue (days or weeks before)
• Back, neck, or jaw pain
• Indigestion or "heartburn" feeling
• Lightheadedness or dizziness
Why This Matters:
• Women are often diagnosed later than men
• Atypical symptoms lead to treatment delays
• Mortality rates are higher in women post-MI
• Diabetes increases atypical presentation in both genders

Adam's Patient Education: "For women, a heart attack might feel more like extreme tiredness, nausea, or pain in the back or jaw rather than classic chest pain."

🩺 Cardiology Clinical Competencies

  • Symptom Recognition: Women often present with atypical MI symptoms (fatigue, nausea, back pain). Classic chest pain is less common in women than men.
  • Diagnostic Procedures: Understand what each test assesses: ECG (electrical), Echo (structural), Stress (functional), Cath (anatomical), Holter (rhythm over time).
  • Medication Safety: Warfarin requires INR monitoring. ACE + ARB combination is usually contraindicated. High-dose diuretics need electrolyte monitoring.
  • Patient Preparation: Different tests require different preparations. Clear explanations reduce anxiety and improve test accuracy.

💬 Patient Communication Scripts

  • "The stress test shows how your heart handles exercise. We'll monitor you closely the entire time."
  • "Women's heart attack symptoms can be different—more like extreme tiredness or nausea than classic chest pain."
  • "Warfarin is a blood thinner that requires regular blood tests to make sure the dose is just right for you."
  • "The swelling in both ankles could mean your heart isn't pumping as efficiently—let's monitor this closely."
