💡 Geriatric Communication Strategies
For hearing-impaired patients: Face patient directly, speak clearly (don't shout), use written supplements, verify understanding with teach-back method.
For arthritis: Offer assistance with clothing, position arm comfortably, use warm compress if needed.
For anxiety: Explain each step before doing it, use distraction techniques, maintain calm demeanor.
🗣️ Communication Challenge: The Patient Forgot to Fast
Patient reveals he ate breakfast 2 hours ago. What is your FIRST response?
A
"That's okay, we'll draw it anyway. The results might just be a little high."
B
"You shouldn't have eaten! Now we have to reschedule. This was clearly explained to you."
C
"I understand forgetting happens. Let me explain why fasting is important and help reschedule."
D
"I'll draw the blood now but mark it as non-fasting. The provider can decide what to do."
🔬 8 Steps of Specimen Collection (Check each as you explain/perform)
1. Patient Identification: Use two identifiers (name & DOB)
2. Explain Procedure: Clear, age-appropriate explanation
3. Prepare Equipment: Tourniquet, correct tubes, needle, gauze, bandage
4. Patient Positioning: Comfortable position, arm supported
5. Site Selection: Assess veins, consider arthritis limitations
6. Perform Venipuncture: Smooth technique, monitor patient comfort
7. Proper Handling: Correct tube order, gentle mixing, no hemolysis
8. Labeling & Documentation: Immediate labeling at bedside, complete requisition
🔗 Chain of Custody (If applicable)
For legal specimens (drug tests, paternity, etc.):
1. Document everyone who handles specimen
2. Maintain continuous physical control
3. Use tamper-evident seals
4. Complete all documentation signatures
5. Store appropriately until transfer
✓ Excellent Geriatric Communication!
Correct Response to Forgotten Fast: C

Why This Approach Works:
• Validates patient's experience: "I understand forgetting happens" reduces shame
• Educational: Explains medical reason for fasting (accurate glucose/HbA1c results)
• Problem-solving: Offers solution (rescheduling) instead of blame
• Maintains therapeutic relationship: Preserves trust for future visits

8 Steps Clinical Pearls for Geriatric Patients:
1. Identification: Elderly may have similar names - always use 2 identifiers
2. Explanation: Use simple language, check hearing aids are working
3. Positioning: Consider arthritis - use pillows for support
4. Vein Selection: Older adults often have fragile, rolling veins
5. Comfort: Warm compress (not too hot) can help with vein visualization
6. Post-procedure: Check bleeding time - may be longer on anticoagulants

Documentation for This Encounter:
• Patient non-fasting status
• Education provided about fasting requirements
• Appointment rescheduled (date/time)
• Any patient concerns or questions addressed
• Communication accommodations used (facing patient, written instructions)

• Medicare insurance
• PMH: Hypertension, Osteoarthritis
• Medications: Lisinopril 10mg daily, Tylenol PRN
Provider Order: "Geriatric Screening Panel"
😴 Increasing fatigue over 3 months
⚖️ Unintentional 12lb weight loss
🏠 Social withdrawal - stopped bridge club
🥗 Reduced appetite & cooking interest
📝 Subjective Note from Daughter
"Mom just isn't herself. She used to love cooking and seeing friends. Now she says she's too tired. Her clothes are getting loose. I'm worried but she says 'it's just old age.'"
📊 Step 1: Select Appropriate Screening Tools
PHQ-9
9-item depression screen. Scores 0-27. ≥10 suggests depression. Geriatric adaptation: May need larger font, assistance reading.
Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS)
15-item screen designed for elderly. Yes/no format. Advantage: Less emphasis on somatic symptoms.
MUST (Malnutrition Universal Screen)
5-step tool: BMI, weight loss, acute disease. For elderly: Consider clothing/fluid changes affecting weight.
Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA)
Comprehensive nutrition screen. Includes appetite, mobility. Gold standard for elderly nutritional assessment.
MMSE
Mini-Mental State Exam. Cognitive screen. Consider: Education level affects scoring.
MoCA
Montreal Cognitive Assessment. More sensitive than MMSE. Better for: Mild cognitive impairment detection.
👵 Step 2: How Would You Administer These Respectfully?
A
Give her the forms to complete alone in the exam room. Check back in 10 minutes.
B
Read questions aloud using normal tone. Assume she can hear/understand everything.
C
Offer choice: "Would you prefer to read this yourself or have me read it to you?" Use large print version, face her, speak clearly.
D
Skip screening - her symptoms clearly indicate depression. Just document clinical impression.

⚠️
Geriatric Syndrome Recognition
Unexplained weight loss + fatigue + social withdrawal = "Geriatric Syndrome" requiring comprehensive evaluation. This pattern suggests depression, malnutrition, or underlying medical condition (cancer, thyroid, anemia). NEVER dismiss as "normal aging" without appropriate assessment.
✓ Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment
Recommended Screening Tools:
• Depression: GDS-15 (preferred for elderly) or PHQ-9
• Nutrition: MNA (Mini Nutritional Assessment)
• Cognition: MoCA (more sensitive than MMSE for mild changes)

Administration Best Practice: C
• Offer choice respects autonomy
• Large print accommodates presbyopia
• Face-to-face aids hearing-impaired
• Clear speech with pauses for processing

Essential Laboratory Workup:
• CBC: Anemia (common cause of fatigue)
• CMP: Electrolytes, kidney/liver function, glucose
• TSH: Hypothyroidism (fatigue, weight changes)
• Vitamin B12 & Folate: Deficiency causes anemia, neuro symptoms
• Albumin/Prealbumin: Nutritional status markers
• CRP/ESR: Inflammation markers (infection, autoimmune)

Clinical Reasoning:
This pattern suggests possible:
1. Depression: Common after loss (widowhood), social withdrawal
2. Malnutrition: Weight loss, reduced cooking interest
3. Medical condition: Cancer, thyroid disorder, anemia
4. Combination: Often multifactorial in elderly

🩺 Clinical Competency Insights

  • Geriatric Communication: Always face patient, speak clearly (don't shout), use teach-back method. Presbycusis affects high-frequency sounds first - consonants (s, f, th) may be missed.
  • Specimen Collection Adaptations: Elderly veins are often fragile. Use smaller gauge needles (23G), apply pressure longer (1-2 minutes minimum), consider butterfly for difficult access.
  • Screening Tools Selection: GDS-15 preferred over PHQ-9 for elderly (less somatic emphasis). MNA better than MUST for comprehensive nutrition assessment. MoCA more sensitive than MMSE for mild cognitive impairment.
  • Lab Interpretation in Elderly: "Normal" ranges may differ. Slight anemia may be tolerated. Creatinine may be normal despite reduced GFR due to low muscle mass. Always consider age-adjusted norms.

📋 Documentation & Follow-up Protocols

  • Communication Accommodations: Document any adaptations used (large print, verbal administration, interpreter). This supports billing and continuity of care.
  • Chain of Custody: For legal specimens, document EVERY transfer: time, date, signatures of both parties. Maintain continuous control. Use tamper-evident seals.
  • Geriatric Screening Results: Document raw scores AND interpretation. Example: "PHQ-9 score: 14/27 (moderate depression). Patient endorsed low mood, anhedonia, fatigue."
  • Critical Value Notification: Elderly may decompensate faster. Document provider notification for abnormal labs including time, method, provider response, and patient instructions given.
