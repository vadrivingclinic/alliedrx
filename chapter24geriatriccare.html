Fall Risk Alert!
Combined nervous system changes (slower reaction time) and musculoskeletal changes (reduced bone density, muscle mass) significantly increase fall risk in older adults. Always assess home safety and recommend assistive devices.

Key Age-Related Changes by System:

Nervous System:
• Decreased reaction time & processing speed
• Mild memory changes (normal aging)
• Reduced taste/smell (olfactory changes)
Clinical Application: Allow extra time for tasks, repeat instructions, ensure adequate lighting.

Sensory Organs:
• Presbyopia (lens stiffening) → need for reading glasses
• Presbycusis (high-frequency hearing loss)
• Reduced tear production (dry eyes)
Clinical Application: Face patient when speaking, provide written instructions, use artificial tears PRN.

Musculoskeletal:
• Decreased bone density (osteopenia/osteoporosis)
• Reduced muscle mass (sarcopenia)
• Joint stiffness & decreased flexibility
Clinical Application: Fall prevention strategies, weight-bearing exercise, calcium/Vitamin D supplementation.

🗣️ Geriatric Communication Scenario

Select the MOST appropriate communication approach for this geriatric patient scenario. Consider sensory changes, cognitive status, and patient dignity.

Patient Scenario
You are medical assistant Bill Novelli. Mr. Henderson, an 82-year-old patient with mild hearing loss, presbyopia, and early-stage osteoarthritis, has arrived for his routine blood pressure check. He seems slightly confused about why he's here and keeps asking you to repeat instructions. His daughter mentioned he's been more forgetful lately.
👨‍🦳
Mr. James Henderson, 82 years old
• Mild bilateral hearing loss (high-frequency)
• Presbyopia (wears reading glasses)
• Early osteoarthritis in knees
• Daughter reports increased forgetfulness
• Takes medications for hypertension & arthritis
A
Speak quickly and efficiently to complete the vital signs check. Give all instructions at once to save time.
B
Speak loudly directly into his ear. Assume he needs help with everything and assist without asking permission.
Face him directly at eye level. Speak clearly at moderate pace. Provide one instruction at a time. Verify understanding using teach-back method.
Speak to his daughter instead, as she seems more competent. Let her explain everything to her father.
🩺 Communication Principles for Older Adults
SPEAK Model:
• Sit or stand at eye level
• Position yourself facing the patient
• Eliminate background noise
• Ask one question at a time
• Keep instructions simple & verify understanding
✓ Effective Geriatric Communication!
Correct Answer: C

Why This Approach Works:
• Face-to-face: Allows lip-reading and visual cues for hearing-impaired patients
• Eye level: Maintains dignity and avoids intimidating posture
• Moderate pace: Accommodates slower processing speed
• One instruction at a time: Prevents cognitive overload
• Teach-back method: "Please tell me in your own words what we're going to do next"

Why Other Options Are Less Effective:
• A (Rushed approach): Increases anxiety and errors
• B (Loud/patronizing): Violates dignity, may startle patient
• D (Excluding patient): Violates autonomy and may miss important information from patient

Documentation Note: Document any cognitive concerns using standardized tools like MMSE or MoCA if provider orders. Note communication strategies used successfully.

🩺 Clinical Competency Insights

  • Age-Related Changes: Normal aging involves gradual decline in all systems. Pathological changes require medical intervention. Always assess functional status rather than chronological age.
  • Communication Adjustments: Presbycusis affects high-frequency sounds first (consonants like s, f, th). Face patients, speak clearly, use written supplements, and verify understanding.
  • Fall Prevention: Multifactorial approach: medication review, vision check, home safety assessment, exercise for balance/strength, proper footwear, and assistive devices.
  • Screening Tools: Use validated tools: MMSE/MoCA for cognition, PHQ-9/GDS for depression, MUST for malnutrition. Document scores and follow protocols.

📋 Documentation & Community Resources

  • Comprehensive Assessment: Document ADLs (Activities of Daily Living) and IADLs (Instrumental ADLs). Changes may indicate need for additional support services.
  • Community Resources: Know local services: Meals on Wheels, senior transportation, adult day care, home health, respite care, and caregiver support groups.
  • Legal Considerations: Document advance directives, healthcare proxy, and POLST forms. Respect patient autonomy while ensuring safety.
  • Medication Management: Older adults average 4-5 prescriptions. Document adherence, use pill organizers, conduct "brown bag" medication reviews, watch for polypharmacy.
