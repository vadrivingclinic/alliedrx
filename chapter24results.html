Work through 4 geriatric-focused lab result stations. At each station: (1) Interpret the result, (2) Identify CLIA category, (3) Draft patient explanation, (4) Propose health recommendation.

💡 Geriatric Lab Interpretation Tips
Age-adjusted norms: Some "normal" ranges differ for older adults (e.g., creatinine may be normal despite reduced kidney function due to low muscle mass).
Polypharmacy effects: Always consider medication interactions when interpreting results.
Functional impact: Ask "How does this result affect the patient's daily life and safety?"

🩺 Clinical Competency Insights

  • Lab Result Interpretation: Always interpret in clinical context. INR 4.2 on warfarin = critical vs. INR 4.2 with liver disease = expected. Consider medications, comorbidities, and functional status.
  • CLIA Categories: Waived tests (glucose, INR, urinalysis dipstick) can be performed in office with minimal training. Moderate/high complexity tests require certified lab personnel. Know what your facility is certified to perform.
  • Patient Education: Use "teach-back" method: "Can you tell me in your own words what we discussed?" Provide written materials in large print (14pt+). Involve family when appropriate.
  • Holistic Assessment: The "Geriatric Giants": Immobility, Instability, Incontinence, Intellectual impairment. Address these systematically with interdisciplinary team.

📋 Documentation & Advocacy Protocols

  • Care Coordination Documentation: Document all referrals (date, agency, contact), follow-up appointments, and patient/family education. Include barriers addressed (transportation, cost, language).
  • Community Resource Navigation: Maintain updated resource directory: Area Agency on Aging, Alzheimer's Association, local senior centers, transportation services, meal programs, support groups.
  • Legal/Ethical Documentation: Document capacity assessments, advance directive discussions, surrogate decision-maker identification. Note any concerns about safety vs. autonomy conflicts.
  • Quality Indicators: Track: fall reduction, medication adherence, depression screening completion, malnutrition identification, advance directive completion rates.

🔗 Interprofessional Collaboration

  • Team Members: Geriatrician, Pharmacist (medication review), Dietitian, Physical/Occupational Therapy, Social Work, Home Health, Community Health Worker.
  • Communication Channels: Regular team meetings, shared EHR notes, warm handoffs between providers, standardized referral forms, family conferences.
  • MA Role: Central coordination point, patient/family education reinforcement, appointment scheduling, resource connection, continuity maintenance.
