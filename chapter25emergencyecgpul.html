Correct Priority Sequence for Chest Pain:
1. Pulse Oximetry - Immediate oxygenation status (SpO₂ 89% indicates hypoxia)
2. Cardiac Monitor - Detect life-threatening arrhythmias
3. 12-Lead ECG - STEMI detection within 10 minutes is critical
4. Blood Pressure - 88/60 indicates hypotension requiring intervention
5. Respiratory Rate - Assess work of breathing

Distractors to Avoid:
• Urinalysis - Not indicated for acute chest pain
• Blood Glucose - Important but not priority with hypotension

First Tool: 12-Lead ECG for chest pain with cardiac history (correct choice)
Incorrect Choices: Peak flow meter for asthma, BP monitor not first priority

Treatment Pathway: O₂ → IV access → Physician notification (correct)
Incorrect Treatments: Oral pain meds contraindicated, waiting delays care

Clinical Reasoning: Chest pain with hypotension (88/60) and low SpO₂ (89%) suggests cardiogenic shock. Immediate ECG and aspirin are critical while maintaining oxygenation. O₂ therapy comes before IV access for hypoxic patients.

💡 Teach-Back Guidance
Correct Response: B
Why it's correct: Holter monitors require continuous wear (except bathing) to capture all heart rhythms. Electrical interference can affect readings. Symptom correlation is critical for diagnosis.

Why others are incorrect:
• A: Removing for exercise misses potentially significant rhythms
• C: Intermittent wear provides incomplete data
• D: Symptom-only monitoring misses asymptomatic arrhythmias

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Rapid Triage: In cardiopulmonary emergencies, prioritize ABCs (Airway, Breathing, Circulation) before diagnostic tests. Obtain ECG within 10 minutes for chest pain patients. Distractors like urinalysis or non-urgent tests should be recognized and deferred.
  • Device Education: Holter monitors require continuous wear (except bathing). Common patient errors include removing during sleep or exercise. Electrical devices (microwaves, cell phones) can interfere with readings.
  • Peak Flow Technique: Proper technique includes standing upright, taking maximal inspiration, sealing lips tightly, and giving a hard, fast blow. Record the best of 3 attempts with 30-second rests between. Common errors include sitting, gentle blowing, and immediate repeats.
  • Teach-Back Method: Always verify patient understanding by having them demonstrate or explain instructions. For Holter monitors, emphasize continuous wear, symptom diary, and avoiding electrical interference. Incorrect responses often involve intermittent wear or removal during activities.
  • Treatment Pathway: For chest pain with hypoxia: O₂ first, then IV access, then physician notification. Avoid delays like waiting or giving oral medications. Immediate interventions take priority over complete diagnostics.

📋 Documentation & Compliance Standards

  • Emergency Documentation: Record exact times of symptom onset, interventions (O₂ start time, ECG time), and responses. Include ECG findings, medications given with times, and patient's response to treatment.
  • Device Documentation: Log Holter monitor placement time, electrode locations, patient instructions given, symptom diary provided, and return appointment scheduled. Document any technical issues or patient concerns.
  • Patient Education Records: Document what was taught, patient's demonstration of understanding, any barriers to learning (language, literacy), adaptations made, and plan for follow-up education. Include teach-back validation.
  • Quality Indicators: Monitor door-to-ECG time (<10 minutes), patient education comprehension rates (>90%), device-related adverse events, and follow-up appointment adherence.
