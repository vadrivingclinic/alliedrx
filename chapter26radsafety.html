ALARA Principles & PPE Selection

Apply ALARA (As Low As Reasonably Achievable) principles by selecting appropriate radiation safety measures and personal protective equipment for different clinical scenarios.

Scenario: Chest X-Ray Preparation
Sara is preparing a 65-year-old patient for a routine PA chest x-ray. The patient has no known allergies and is cooperative. Sara needs to ensure radiation safety for both the patient and herself.
Lead apron (0.5mm Pb equivalent)
Thyroid shield
Lead gloves (if hands in primary beam)
Surgical mask
Position patient 6 feet from source
Sterile gloves
Collimate to area of interest
Use fastest exposure time possible
⚠️
Radiation Safety Violation!
ALARA principles require minimizing time, maximizing distance, and using proper shielding. Never compromise on lead protection when in the radiographic room during exposures.
💡 Pro Tip: Pregnancy Precautions
Always ask female patients of childbearing age about possible pregnancy before any radiographic procedure. Use the "10-day rule" for non-urgent abdominal/pelvic imaging: schedule within 10 days after onset of menstruation when pregnancy is unlikely.
✓ ALARA Principles Applied!
Correct ALARA Measures for Chest X-Ray:
1. Lead apron (0.5mm Pb equivalent) - Required for anyone remaining in room
2. Thyroid shield - Essential for thyroid protection
3. Lead gloves - Only if hands are in primary beam (not typically for chest)
4. Position patient 6 feet from source - Maximize distance when possible
5. Collimate to area of interest - Reduces scatter radiation
6. Use fastest exposure time possible - Minimizes radiation dose

Incorrect Items:
• Surgical mask - Not needed for radiation protection
• Sterile gloves - Not a radiation safety measure

Clinical Insight: The annual occupational dose limit is 5,000 mrem (50 mSv). A single chest x-ray gives about 10 mrem (0.1 mSv) to the patient.

Radiographic Positioning & Projections

Select the correct radiographic position based on clinical indications. Understand the differences between AP, PA, lateral, and oblique projections.

Scenario: Suspected Wrist Fracture
A 28-year-old patient presents with acute wrist pain after a fall. Dr. Swain orders a wrist series to rule out fracture. Select the MOST appropriate initial projection for evaluating a suspected Colles' fracture.
PA Wrist (Posteroanterior)
Patient's palm flat on cassette, beam enters posterior wrist, exits anterior
AP Wrist (Anteroposterior)
Patient's dorsum on cassette, beam enters anterior wrist, exits posterior
Lateral Chest
Patient's side against cassette, beam enters opposite side
Lateral Wrist
Patient's thumb up, ulnar side against cassette, beam enters radial side
Scenario: Pre-employment Chest X-Ray
A 45-year-old applicant requires a pre-employment chest x-ray for hospital credentialing. Select the STANDARD projection that minimizes cardiac magnification and provides best lung visualization.
AP Chest (Anteroposterior)
Patient facing tube, back against cassette; magnifies heart, used for portable exams
PA Chest (Posteroanterior)
Patient facing cassette, back to tube; minimizes heart magnification, standard for routine
Right Anterior Oblique (RAO)
Patient rotated 45°, used for cardiac studies and esophageal visualization
Lateral Decubitus
Patient lying on side, used to detect free air or fluid in pleural space
📋 Documentation Standards
Essential elements for radiographic documentation:
1. Patient identification (name, DOB, medical record number)
2. Date and time of examination
3. Projection/position (e.g., "PA Chest", "Lateral Wrist")
4. Technical factors (kVp, mAs, SID) if required by facility
5. Right/Left marker clearly visible on image
6. Pregnancy status documented for females 12-55 years
7. Clinical indication and ordering provider
✓ Positioning Mastered!
Correct Selections:
1. PA Wrist & Lateral Wrist - Standard wrist series includes both PA and lateral projections
2. PA Chest - Standard for routine chest exams; minimizes cardiac magnification

Key Positioning Principles:
• PA vs AP: PA = beam enters posterior, exits anterior (better detail, less magnification)
• AP Chest is used for portable exams, ICU patients, and trauma
• Lateral projections provide depth information and are always paired with frontal view
• Oblique projections (45° rotation) visualize structures without superimposition

Clinical Application:
For Colles' fracture (distal radius fracture with dorsal displacement), the lateral view is critical to assess displacement and angulation. The PA view shows radial shortening and intra-articular extension.

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • ALARA Principle: As Low As Reasonably Achievable - The fundamental radiation safety principle. Time, Distance, and Shielding are the three key factors.
  • PPE Requirements: Lead apron (0.5mm Pb eq), thyroid shield, and lead gloves when hands are in primary beam. Monitor badges must be worn at collar level outside lead.
  • Patient Education: Explain procedure simply, ensure understanding of breath-hold instructions, address claustrophobia concerns for MRI/CT, verify pregnancy status.
  • Positioning Accuracy: PA chest reduces heart magnification by 15% compared to AP. Proper positioning reduces repeats, minimizing patient radiation exposure.
  • Legal Responsibilities: Maintain radiation exposure records for 5 years (OSHA), ensure pregnancy screening, obtain informed consent for contrast studies.

📋 Documentation & Compliance

  • Image Management: PACS (Picture Archiving and Communication System) stores digital images. HIPAA compliance requires secure transmission and storage.
  • Contrast Media: Document type, amount, route, and lot number. Screen for allergies (iodine, shellfish) and renal function (eGFR >30 for IV contrast).
  • Quality Control: Daily processor checks, weekly viewbox cleaning, monthly repeat analysis. Document equipment malfunctions and service reports.
  • Emergency Procedures: Know location of emergency shut-off, contrast reaction kit (epinephrine, antihistamines), and radiation safety officer contact.
