Sensitive Scenarios: Pregnancy & Drug Testing

Apply professional judgment to sensitive testing situations. Make decisions regarding confidentiality, procedure, and ethics.

🩺 Case A: The Pregnancy Test
Mrs. Carpenter's pregnancy test (an enzymatic reaction test for hCG) is positive. She is tearful and says, "My husband can't know about this."
1. How do you maintain confidentiality?
Document it normally; the provider will handle it
Her results are private under HIPAA; cannot disclose without written consent
Tell her you'll only share with the provider, not the husband
Ask the provider how to handle this sensitive situation
2. What is your role as an MA?
Counsel her on her options and provide resources
Provide the result factually, show empathy, and inform the provider
Ask if she wants to discuss with a social worker
Document her emotional state in the chart
3. Why might a blood test be ordered next?
To confirm the urine test wasn't a false positive
To quantify hCG levels for dating or monitoring
Because blood tests are more accurate than urine tests
To check for ectopic pregnancy risk
🔒 HIPAA Reminder
Patient results are ALWAYS confidential. Even spouses and family members cannot receive results without the patient's written authorization. This includes pregnancy, drug screening, and all other test results.
⚖️ Case B: The Drug Test
A new patient, Mr. Doe, is asked to provide a urine sample for a pre-employment drug screen. He seems nervous and asks multiple questions about the procedure.
1. What are your instructions for collection?
Explain "chain of custody": proper ID, no water in bathroom, temperature strip
Give him the cup and direct him to the restroom
Have him empty pockets and wash hands first
Instruct him to provide at least 30mL in the cup
2. What does "adulteration" mean?
Contamination from improper collection technique
Tampering with the sample to falsify results (adding water, bleach, etc.)
Using someone else's urine for the test
Forgetting to sign the chain of custody form
3. What is a key legal/ethical point?
The test must be ordered by the employer, not the physician
Test must have legitimate reason; follow procedure exactly for legal integrity
Results can be shared with the employer immediately
You should observe the collection for all drug screens
⚖️ Legal Procedure Required
Chain of Custody (CoC) documentation is LEGALLY REQUIRED for forensic drug testing. Every person who handles the specimen must sign and document date/time. Breaks in the chain invalidate results legally.
✓ Professional Scenarios Mastered
Case A - Pregnancy Test Key Points:
• Confidentiality: HIPAA protects all results. No disclosure without written consent.
• MA Role: Report results factually + empathetically → inform provider.
• Blood Test Purpose: Quantifies hCG for dating/monitoring, not just confirmation.

Case B - Drug Screen Key Points:
• Chain of Custody: Legal protocol: ID verification, secured collection, temperature check.
• Adulteration: Tampering (adding substances) to alter results.
• Legal Integrity: Must follow exact procedure; any deviation can invalidate legally.

Professional Practice: Your demeanor matters. Be factual, non-judgmental, and follow protocols precisely regardless of the situation's sensitivity.

✓ Vocabulary & Ethics Applied
Terminology Mastery:
• Anuria: No urine → MEDICAL EMERGENCY (vs. oliguria/scant urine)
• Bacteriuria: Bacteria in urine → indicates possible UTI
• Cast: Kidney tubule mold → suggests renal involvement
• C&S: Culture & Sensitivity → guides antibiotic therapy

Clinical Application:
• Scant urine ≠ anuria (anuria = 0 urine output)
• Positive leukocyte/nitrite → order C&S for targeted treatment
• Hyaline casts → kidney filtration issue, needs provider evaluation

Ethical Standards:
• NO DISCLOSURE without written consent - even to concerned family
• Document all requests for information, even when denied
• Your professional boundary protects patient autonomy and trust

Documentation Principle: "If it wasn't documented, it wasn't done." This applies to test results AND to protecting confidentiality.
