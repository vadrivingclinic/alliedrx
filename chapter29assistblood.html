Key Learning Points:
• Pediatric Draws: Capillary puncture often preferred for children under 2; use distraction techniques and involve parents appropriately
• Capillary vs Venipuncture Decisions:
  1. Newborn bilirubin → Capillary (heel stick) - standard for infants
  2. Burns on both arms → Capillary (finger stick) - no accessible venous sites

• Ethical Duty: Never compromise sample integrity; refusal is mandatory

💉 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Patient Identification: Always use two identifiers (name + DOB) before ANY procedure. This is the #1 error prevention step in phlebotomy.
  • Order of Draw: Remember "Blood Cultures → Light Blue → Red/Gold → Green → Lavender → Gray." Wrong order can contaminate additives.
  • Pediatric Considerations: For children under 2, capillary may be first choice. Use play distraction, involve parents for positioning, and be swift but gentle.
  • Difficult Sticks: Try 1-2 minor adjustments before withdrawing. Consider warming the site, using smaller gauge (23G butterfly), or hand veins.
  • Legal Draws: Chain of custody requires witness observation, tamper-evident sealing, and documentation of every handoff. Never leave legal samples unattended.

📋 Documentation & Safety Protocols

  • Immediate Labeling: Label tubes at bedside with patient name, DOB, date, time, and collector initials. Prevents misidentification errors.
  • Needle Safety: Activate safety mechanism immediately after withdrawal. Never recap used needles. Dispose in sharps container at point of use.
  • Post-Exposure Protocol: If needlestick occurs: 1) Wash area immediately 2) Report to supervisor 3) Complete incident report 4) Follow facility testing/treatment protocol.
  • Specimen Handling: Mix additive tubes gently 8-10 times. Transport within 30 minutes if not refrigerated. Protect from extreme temperatures.
  • Quality Indicators: Track hemolysis rates (<2%), patient complaints, and successful draw rates. Document difficult sticks for future reference.
