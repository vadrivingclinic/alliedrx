⚠️ Safety First!
Remember: For needlesticks, immediate washing with soap and water is critical. Report ALL incidents within 1 hour. Hemolysis (red cell breakage) ruins specimens - avoid small needles forcing blood, shaking tubes, or drawing from hematomas.

Needlestick Response Protocol:
1. WASH IMMEDIATELY with soap and water (reduces viral load)
2. REPORT to supervisor within 1 hour
3. DOCUMENT all details (time, device, source patient)
4. BASELINE TESTING immediately
5. FOLLOW-UP at 6 weeks, 3 months, 6 months

OSHA Requirements:
• Must use safety-engineered devices (shields, retraction, blunting)
• NEVER RECAP contaminated needles
• Annual training on bloodborne pathogens
• Maintain exposure control plan

💡 Clinical Tip: The anxious patient is at highest risk for vasovagal syncope (fainting). Always have them lie down, use a cold compress on forehead, and monitor closely during and after the procedure.

🛡️ Equipment & Safety Mastery

  • Tube Selection: Lavender=EDTA (CBC), Light Blue=citrate (coagulation), Red=clot activator (chemistry), Green=heparin (STAT chem). Order of draw prevents additive contamination.
  • Needle Gauge: 21G standard adult, 23G elderly/fragile veins, 25G pediatric. Smaller gauge = less trauma but slower flow. Butterfly needles provide flexibility for difficult access.
  • Safety Devices: OSHA requires engineered sharps injury protection. Features include retractable needles, self-blunting, or built-in shields. Never recap contaminated needles.
  • Needlestick Protocol: 1) Wash immediately 2) Report to supervisor 3) Document incident 4) Baseline testing 5) Follow-up at 6wk/3mo/6mo 6) Post-exposure prophylaxis if indicated.
  • Specimen Integrity: Label at bedside, mix gently 8-10 inversions, transport within 30 minutes. Hemolysis causes: small needle forcing blood, shaking tubes, drawing from hematoma.

📝 Documentation & Compliance

  • Incident Reporting: Document needlesticks with date, time, location, device type, procedure being performed, and source patient information. Report to supervisor within 1 hour.
  • Specimen Labeling: Must include: patient name, DOB, medical record number, date/time of collection, collector initials. Label at point of collection to prevent errors.
  • Quality Control: Monitor hemolysis rates (<2%), patient complaints, needlestick incidents. Document equipment failures and implement corrective actions.
  • Patient Education: Explain procedure steps, provide aftercare instructions (pressure on site, monitor for bruising), address anxiety with positioning and distraction techniques.
  • Continuing Education: Annual competency validation for phlebotomy skills, biennial bloodborne pathogens training, quarterly safety drills for emergency response.
