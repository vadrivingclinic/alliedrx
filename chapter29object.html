Phlebotomy Mastery

Venipuncture • Capillary Puncture • Safety Protocols

Week 15 focuses on mastering essential phlebotomy skills, one of the most frequently performed and critical procedures in healthcare. You'll learn multiple blood collection techniques, safety protocols, and specialized approaches for different patient populations to ensure accurate diagnostic results while prioritizing patient comfort and safety.

Phlebotomy requires technical precision combined with interpersonal skill. Successful blood collection depends not only on proper technique but also on effective patient communication, anxiety reduction, and strict adherence to safety and quality standards.

💉 Chapter 29 Case Study: Leah Barney, MA (AAMA)
Setting: Health Alliance Medical Clinic (new employee)
Background: Recent graduate with phlebotomy training in MA program
Current Training: Working with experienced phlebotomist for skill refinement
Key Concerns: Tube/needle selection, pediatric/geriatric approaches, capillary puncture indications, patient comfort techniques, handling difficult venipunctures.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Perform venipuncture using both evacuated tube and syringe methods with proper technique and safety protocols.
  • Select appropriate collection tubes and follow correct order of draw based on requested laboratory tests.
  • Perform capillary puncture procedures for pediatric patients and situations where venipuncture is contraindicated.
  • Apply specialized approaches for pediatric and geriatric patients considering their unique physiological and psychological needs.
  • Implement safety protocols including needlestick prevention and proper post-exposure management procedures.
Order of Draw (CLSI Standard): 1) Blood cultures (yellow), 2) Light blue (citrate), 3) Serum tubes (red/gold/tiger), 4) Green (heparin), 5) Lavender (EDTA), 6) Gray (oxalate/fluoride). Remember: "Blood Cultures Like Red Grapes Grow Lavishly" or "Be Careful Little Girls Love Flowers".
Common Venipuncture Sites: Median cubital vein (first choice - large, stable), Cephalic vein (second choice - lateral arm), Basilic vein (third choice - medial, near arteries/nerves). Avoid areas with hematomas, scars, edema, or above IV sites.
Pediatric Phlebotomy Tips: Use distraction techniques (toys, bubbles), involve parents appropriately (hold child if helpful), be honest ("little pinch"), use play therapy for preparation, consider capillary puncture when possible, and always have assistance for safety.
Preventing Hemolysis: Avoid small needle with large tube, rough handling, frothing (syringe), forcing blood through needle, vigorous mixing, and excessive tourniquet time (>1 minute). Hemolyzed specimens may be rejected by labs.
Difficult Venipuncture Strategies: For deep veins: use longer needle, tighten tourniquet, have patient open/close fist. For rolling veins: anchor firmly below puncture site, use shallow angle. For fragile veins: use smaller needle (23g), butterfly set, syringe method.
