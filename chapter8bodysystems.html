Clinical Significance:
• Integumentary: Often the first system examined (skin color, lesions).
• Respiratory/Circulatory: Requires quiet environment for auscultation.
• Digestive: Examined via palpation; patient must be supine with knees flexed.
• Nervous: Reflex hammer and sensory tools needed for this portion.

A. Check personal email and get coffee.

A. Suture removal kit and sterile gauze.
B. Otoscope, ophthalmoscope, tongue depressors, and reflex hammer.
C. Just the patient's chart; the doctor brings their own tools.
10:30 AM - HIPAA. Felicia is logging vitals on a hallway computer. A visitor walks by.
A. Continue typing; they probably aren't looking.
B. Minimize the screen or use a privacy filter immediately.
C. Ask the visitor to leave the hallway.
Role Simulation Complete!
Key Takeaways:
• Preparation: A stocked room prevents delays during the exam.
• Equipment: Anticipating needs (like the otoscope for HEENT) shows competence.
• Privacy: Protecting PHI (Protected Health Information) is a legal requirement under HIPAA.
