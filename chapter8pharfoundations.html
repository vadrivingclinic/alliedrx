⚠️
Controlled Substance Compliance Critical!
Incorrect DEA schedule classification or improper controlled substance handling violates federal law. Schedule II medications require written prescriptions with no refills. All discrepancies must be reported immediately. Documentation must be precise and contemporaneous.
✓ Drug Classification Mastered!
Drug Name Types:
• Chemical: Exact molecular structure (atorvastatin calcium)
• Generic: Non-proprietary name, lowercase (atorvastatin)
• Trade/Brand: Manufacturer's name, registered trademark® (Lipitor®)
• Drug Class: Pharmacologic category (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitor)
• DEA Schedule: Abuse potential classification
DEA Schedule Criteria:
• Schedule I: No accepted medical use, high abuse (heroin, LSD)
• Schedule II: High abuse, Rx with no refills (oxycodone, fentanyl)
• Schedule III: Moderate abuse, Rx with up to 5 refills in 6 months
• Schedule IV: Low abuse (alprazolam, diazepam)
• Schedule V: Lowest abuse, some OTC (cough syrup with codeine)
Controlled Substance Protocol:
1. Count inventory at ALL shift changes
2. Document EVERY dose in controlled substance log
3. Verify patient ID using TWO identifiers
4. Witness waste with second staff member
5. Store securely - double-locked cabinet required
6. Report discrepancies within 24 hours
Legal Requirements:
• Maintain accurate inventory records for 2 years
• Prescribers must have valid DEA registration
• Schedule II prescriptions cannot be phoned in (except emergencies)
• Tamper-resistant prescription pads required in most states

⚠️
Prescription Accuracy is Non-Negotiable!
Transcribing errors can cause serious harm. Always verify: Right patient, right drug, right dose, right route, right time, right documentation. OTC medications are NOT risk-free - they interact with prescription drugs and have contraindications. Elderly patients are particularly vulnerable to anticholinergic side effects from OTC sleep aids and cold medications.
✓ Prescription Safety Achieved!
Prescription Accuracy Standards:
• Verify TWO patient identifiers before transcribing
• Include ALL required elements: patient info, drug, strength, form, quantity, SIG, refills, provider info
• SIG should be clear: "Take 1 tablet by mouth twice daily with meals"
• Electronic prescriptions reduce errors but still require verification
Age-Related Pharmacokinetics:
• Pediatric: Higher water content, immature liver/kidneys → adjust doses, monitor closely
• Geriatric: Reduced kidney function, polypharmacy risk → start low, go slow, monitor
• Adult: Standard pharmacokinetics apply but consider individual factors
OTC Safety Guidelines:
• NSAIDs with anticoagulants: AVOID - bleeding risk
• Antihistamines in elderly: AVOID - sedation, confusion, falls
• Decongestants with hypertension: AVOID - BP elevation
• Antacids: Calcium carbonate safe short-term; avoid aluminum with kidney disease
Patient Education Points:
• Read OTC labels carefully for active ingredients and warnings
• Report ALL medications (Rx and OTC) to all providers
• Don't exceed recommended doses or duration
• Store medications safely, check expiration dates
• Never share prescription medications

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Drug Name Mastery: Always use generic names when possible to avoid confusion. Know that chemical names describe molecular structure, generic names are non-proprietary, and trade names are manufacturer-specific. This knowledge prevents medication errors.
  • DEA Schedule Compliance: Schedule I drugs have NO medical use. Schedule II prescriptions cannot be refilled - new Rx required. Schedule III-V allow refills with limits. ALL controlled substances require precise documentation and secure storage.
  • Prescription Accuracy: The "five rights" plus documentation: Right patient, drug, dose, route, time, and documentation. Double-check every element, especially with look-alike/sound-alike drugs. Use tall man lettering when appropriate (e.g., hydrALAZINE vs. hydrOXYzine).
  • Age-Specific Considerations: Pediatric doses are weight-based. Geriatric patients often need reduced doses due to decreased renal/hepatic function. Always consider developmental stage and comorbidities when assessing medication appropriateness.
  • OTC Medication Safety: OTC does NOT mean risk-free. Many OTC drugs interact with prescriptions (NSAIDs with warfarin, antacids with tetracyclines). Elderly patients are particularly vulnerable to anticholinergic effects from OTC sleep aids and cold medications.

📋 Documentation & Legal Compliance

  • Controlled Substance Logs: Must include date, time, patient name, medication, dose, administering staff, and witness for waste. Maintain records for 2 years minimum. Inventory must be counted at every shift change with outgoing and incoming staff signatures.
  • Prescription Documentation: EHR entries must be complete and accurate. Include indication when known. Document patient education provided about the medication, including potential side effects and when to contact the provider.
  • Error Reporting: Medication errors must be reported immediately per facility policy. Document objectively - what happened, when, who was involved, what actions were taken. Focus on system improvement, not individual blame.
  • Continuing Education: Pharmacology knowledge must be updated regularly. New drugs are constantly approved, and new safety information emerges. Document participation in drug safety trainings and competency validations.
