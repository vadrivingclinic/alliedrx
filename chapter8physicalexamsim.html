Standard Order (IPPA):
1. Inspection: Always look first. Observation yields 90% of the diagnosis.
2. Palpation: Touch follows observation to confirm findings.
3. Percussion: Tapping assesses density of underlying structures.
4. Auscultation: Listening is generally last.

🚨 Clinical Alert (Abdominal Exception): For abdominal exams, the order changes to Inspection, Auscultation, Percussion, Palpation. [cite_start]You must listen before touching, as palpation can alter bowel sounds[cite: 7].

⚖️ Patient Education & Ethics Navigator

Navigate complex clinical scenarios involving patient rights, privacy, and education. Select the most professional response for Felicia.

Scenario 1: Modesty & Compliance 1/3
A female patient is scheduled for a breast exam but refuses to remove her bra, stating she feels too exposed. The provider will be in shortly.
Tell her she must remove it or the doctor cannot perform the exam.
Validate her concern and offer a gown with the opening in the front, explaining she only needs to uncover one side at a time.
Go get the provider immediately to handle the refusal.
Scenario 2: Family & HIPAA 2/3
The patient's adult daughter is in the exam room. The patient asks you, "Did my STD test results come back?"
Answer yes or no since the daughter is present in the room.
State that results are available and ask the patient if she would like to discuss them privately or with her daughter present.
Ask the daughter to leave the room immediately.
Scenario 3: Chaperone Duties 3/3
Dr. Kosto (female) is performing a pelvic exam on a female patient. The patient says, "I don't need a chaperone, I trust the doctor."
Leave the room to respect the patient's wishes.
Explain that for the protection of both the patient and provider, it is office policy to have a chaperone present for all sensitive exams.
Stay in the room but turn your back to give them privacy.
✓ Ethical Standards Met!
Key Takeaways:
• Modesty: Always offer alternatives (draping, partial exposure) to increase comfort.
• Privacy: Presence does not equal consent. Always verify permission before discussing PHI with family.
• Chaperones: Essential for legal protection of all parties during sensitive exams, regardless of patient preference.

🩺 Clinical Proficiency Insights

  • Exam Sequencing: Mastering the standard IPPA sequence ensures efficiency. Remember the "Abdominal Exception" (Auscultate before Palpate) to avoid creating false bowel sounds.
  • Patient Comfort: Preserving dignity through proper draping and communication builds trust and reduces patient anxiety during vulnerable moments.
  • Role of the MA: You are the patient's advocate. If they seem uncomfortable, pause and address their concerns before the provider enters.

📋 Key Learning Points

  • HIPAA Compliance: Always verify who is allowed to hear medical information, even with spouses or parents of adult children present.
  • Chaperone Policy: Standard of care requires a third party for all sensitive examinations (breast, genital, rectal) to protect against liability.
  • Documentation: "If it wasn't documented, it wasn't done." Record the presence of a chaperone and any patient education provided.
