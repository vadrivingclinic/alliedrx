3D Spatial Orientation

Understanding heart anatomy in three dimensions is crucial for clinical applications:

  • Anterior View: Right ventricle most anterior, left ventricle posterior
  • Base vs Apex: Base (superior) receives veins, apex (inferior) points leftward
  • Chamber Relationships: Right atrium is rightmost, left ventricle is leftmost and posterior
  • Axis Orientation: Heart sits at approximately 45-degree angle in chest

Clinical Note: EKG leads view the heart from different 3D perspectives.

🔍

Chamber Walls & Thickness

3D visualization reveals important thickness variations:

  • Left Ventricle: Thickest wall (10-15mm) - pumps against systemic pressure
  • Right Ventricle: Thinner wall (3-5mm) - pumps to low-pressure pulmonary system
  • Atrial Walls: Thinnest chambers - mainly reservoirs
  • Interventricular Septum: Thickens inferiorly, bows into right ventricle

Wall thickness correlates with pressure requirements and can indicate pathology.

🔄

Coronary Artery 3D Course

Coronary arteries wrap around the heart in three dimensions:

  • RCA: Runs in right AV groove, wraps to posterior surface
  • LAD: Descends anteriorly in interventricular groove
  • Circumflex: Travels in left AV groove, may wrap around posteriorly
  • Diagonal Branches: Angle across anterior surface of left ventricle

Understanding 3D courses helps locate blockages on angiography.

Essential 3D Anatomy Concepts

Spatial Relationships
Heart chambers overlap in 3D space. Right ventricle is most anterior, left atrium is most posterior.
EKG Correlation
Different EKG leads view the heart from specific 3D angles (frontal vs horizontal planes).
Valve Positioning
Valves are oriented in different planes: aortic valve is central and superior.
Clinical Imaging
CT and MRI show heart in true anatomical planes (axial, coronal, sagittal).
