The Heart as a Pump

Structure • Electrical Conduction • Circulation

Week 1 establishes the critical foundation for EKG interpretation. Before you can analyze an electrical tracing, you must understand the mechanical pump it powers.

We focus on the flow of blood through the chambers and valves, the layers of the heart that allow for contraction, and the coronary circulation that keeps the heart muscle alive.

🩺 Case Study: Identifying Ischemia
Patient: John Doe, 58M
Complaint: "Heavy" chest pressure radiating to left arm.
Clinical Link: This suggests Angina Pectoris caused by Ischemia (lack of O2) in the myocardium. If the blockage persists, it progresses to Infarction (tissue death).
Key Concept: Understanding coronary artery anatomy helps predict which part of the heart is affected.

Key Physiology Concepts:

  • Systole: Contraction/Ejection phase.
  • Diastole: Relaxation/Filling phase.
  • Atrial Kick: The final 10-30% of ventricular filling volume provided by atrial contraction.
  • Stroke Volume: Amount of blood ejected with one beat.
Critical Formula:
Cardiac Output (CO) = Stroke Volume (SV) × Heart Rate (HR)
The volume of blood pumped by the heart in one minute.
Key Terms - Load:
Preload: Volume of blood in ventricles at end of diastole (stretch).
Afterload: Resistance left ventricle must overcome to circulate blood.
Layers of the Heart:
1. Epicardium: Outer protective layer.
2. Myocardium: Middle muscular layer (does the work).
3. Endocardium: Inner lining, smooth to prevent clots.
