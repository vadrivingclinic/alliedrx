Electricity drives the Pump

Electrophysiology • Waveforms • Mechanics

Week 2 dives deep into the electrical properties of the heart. Understanding how a cell generates an electrical impulse (action potential) and how that impulse travels is key to interpreting what you see on the EKG paper.

We connect the invisible (ion movement) to the visible (PQRST waveforms).

📉 Case Study: Artifact Alert
Scenario: Patient moving during EKG; baseline is wandering and "fuzzy".
Challenge: Is this Atrial Fibrillation or Artifact?
Key Concept: Artifact is non-cardiac electrical activity. Differentiate by looking for distinct QRS complexes marching through the noise. Solution: Check electrodes and patient comfort.

Key Concepts:

  • Depolarization: Electrical activation (Contraction follows).
  • Repolarization: Electrical recovery (Relaxation follows).
  • Refractory Periods: "Resting" phases where cells cannot be stimulated (Absolute vs Relative).
The Conduction Highway:
SA Node → AV Node → Bundle of His → Bundle Branches → Purkinje Fibers
ECG Paper Math:
• Small Box = 0.04 sec
• Large Box = 0.20 sec
• 5 Large Boxes = 1.0 sec
Vocabulary Spotlight:
Automaticity: The unique ability of cardiac pacemaker cells to generate their own electrical impulses spontaneously.
