The SA Node: The Heart's Pacemaker

Structure • Rate Control • Rhythm

Week 3 focuses on the Sinoatrial (SA) Node, the primary pacemaker of the heart. You will learn to identify when the SA node is functioning normally and what happens when it fires too slowly, too quickly, or fails to fire at all.

Correctly identifying sinus mechanisms is the first step in EKG interpretation, as all other rhythms are compared against Normal Sinus Rhythm.

📉 Case Study: Symptomatic Bradycardia
Patient: 72M, endurance athlete, presenting with dizziness.
ECG Findings: Regular rhythm, P wave before every QRS, Rate = 42 bpm.
Interpretation: Sinus Bradycardia.
Critical Thinking: Is the rate physiological (athlete) or pathological (symptoms)? Since he is dizzy, it is symptomatic and requires evaluation/treatment (e.g., Atropine or pacing).

Key Rhythm Concepts:

  • NSR: Regular, 60-100 bpm, P waves upright in Lead II.
  • Arrhythmia: Often respiratory; rate increases with inspiration, decreases with expiration.
  • Block vs. Arrest: Block is a delay/pause where the next beat resumes on time (multiple of P-P). Arrest is a failure to fire with a pause of undetermined length.
Management Tip:
Sinus Tachycardia is usually a symptom of an underlying cause (fever, pain, anxiety, hypovolemia). Treat the cause, not just the rhythm.
Vocabulary Spotlight:
Sinus Arrhythmia: A dysrhythmia originating in the SA node that occurs when the SA node discharges irregularly; often associated with phases of breathing.
