Atrial Irregularities

Ectopic Foci • Supraventricular • Reentry

Week 4 moves beyond the SA node to examine rhythms originating from ectopic foci within the atria. When cells other than the SA node take over pacing, distinct P-wave morphological changes occur.

You will learn to differentiate between irritability (PACs) and sustained tachycardias (SVT, Flutter, Fibrillation), which are critical for patient management.

🩺 Case Study: Palpitations
Patient: 32F, complaining of sudden "racing heart" and dizziness.
ECG Findings: Regular rhythm, Rate 180 bpm, QRS narrow (0.08s). P waves are buried.
Diagnosis: SVT (Supraventricular Tachycardia).
Treatment: Vagal maneuvers first (bearing down), followed by Adenosine if unsuccessful.

Key Rhythm Identifiers:

  • PAC: Early beat, different P wave shape, pause follows.
  • A-Flutter: "Sawtooth" F waves, usually regular conduction (e.g., 2:1).
  • A-Fib: "Irregularly irregular," chaotic baseline (f waves), no distinct P waves.
SVT vs. Sinus Tach:
Sinus Tach generally has visible P waves and a rate < 160 bpm (in adults). SVT often exceeds 150-160 bpm and P waves are hidden in the T wave.
Critical Risk - AFib:
Loss of atrial kick reduces cardiac output by 20-30%. Stagnant blood in the atria can form clots, leading to stroke (CVA) or pulmonary embolism.
Vocabulary Spotlight:
Vagal Maneuver: Physical actions (like coughing or carotid massage) that stimulate the vagus nerve to slow conduction through the AV node.
