The AV Junction: Backup Pacemaker

Retrograde Conduction • Escape • Inverted P Waves

Week 5 explores the AV Junction (AV node + Bundle of His). Normally the "gatekeeper" delaying impulses, it serves as a critical backup pacemaker if the SA node fails.

A key concept this week is Retrograde Conduction. Because junctional impulses start in the middle of the heart, they must travel backward to depolarize the atria, creating distinct inverted P waves.

💊 Case Study: Digitalis Toxicity?
Patient: 82M with Heart Failure on Digoxin.
ECG Findings: Regular rhythm, Rate 50 bpm, P waves are inverted and occur immediately before the QRS.
Interpretation: Junctional Escape Rhythm.
Clinical Link: Digoxin slows AV conduction. Toxicity can suppress the SA node, forcing the Junction to take over (Escape).

Key Rhythm Concepts:

  • PJC vs. Escape: A PJC comes early (irritability). An Escape beat comes late (safety mechanism because the SA node failed).
  • Inverted P Waves: Can appear before, during (hidden), or after the QRS in leads II, III, and aVF.
Identifying Rates:
• Escape: 40-60 bpm (Intrinsic)
• Accelerated: 61-100 bpm
• Tachycardia: >100 bpm
Vocabulary Spotlight:
Retrograde: Moving backward; in the opposite direction to normal conduction (Atria ← Junction → Ventricles).
