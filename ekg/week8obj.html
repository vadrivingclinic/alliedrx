Artificial Pacing

Pulse Generator • Capture • Sensing

Week 8 focuses on what happens when the heart's natural pacemakers fail and technology steps in. You must be able to identify artificial pacemaker spikes and determine if the device is functioning correctly.

Pacemakers are programmed to "watch" (sense) the patient's intrinsic rhythm and only "fire" (pace) when needed (demand).

🩺 Case Study: Dizziness
Patient: 85M with a pacemaker.
ECG Findings: Regular pacemaker spikes are visible at 70 bpm, but several spikes are NOT followed by QRS complexes.
Diagnosis: Failure to Capture.
Implication: The electrical stimulus was delivered but failed to depolarize the heart. The patient has a lower heart rate than programmed, causing symptoms.

Key Rhythm Concepts:

  • The Spike: A sharp, thin vertical line indicating electrical discharge.
  • Capture: The depolarization (P wave or QRS) that immediately follows a spike.
  • Sensing: The pacemaker's ability to see intrinsic activity and inhibit firing to avoid competition.
Malfunction Terms:
• Failure to Capture: Spike present, no complex.
• Failure to Pace (Fire): No spike when one should be there.
• Oversensing: Pacemaker thinks artifact is a heart beat and inhibits firing (pauses).
Vocabulary Spotlight:
AV Interval: In dual-chamber pacing, the time between the atrial event and the ventricular pace (artificial PR interval).
