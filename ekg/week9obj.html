The Diagnostic Standard

Axis • Hypertrophy • Ischemia

Week 9 transitions from simple rhythm identification to comprehensive **12-lead analysis**. You will learn to use the electrical "view" from different leads to localize problems in the heart.

Key skills include calculating the **Electrical Axis** to determine if the heart's position or conduction is shifted, and recognizing the signs of structural changes like **Hypertrophy** (thickening) vs **Dilatation** (stretching).

🩺 Case Study: Left Axis Deviation
Patient: 65M with history of chronic uncontrolled hypertension.
ECG Findings: Lead I is Positive (Up). Lead aVF is Negative (Down).
Axis Analysis: "Left Thumbs Up" method = Left Axis Deviation (LAD).
Clinical Link: High blood pressure forces the left ventricle to work harder, thickening the muscle (LVH), pulling the electrical vector to the left.

Key Concepts:

  • Axis Quadrants: Normal, Left, Right, Extreme.
  • R-Wave Progression: R waves should grow larger from V1 to V5/V6. Poor progression suggests prior MI.
  • The Three I's: Ischemia (reversible), Injury (acute), Infarction (permanent).
The "Two Thumbs" Rule for Axis:
• Both Up: Normal Axis
• Left Up, Right Down: Left Axis Deviation
• Left Down, Right Up: Right Axis Deviation
• Both Down: Extreme Axis (rare)
Vocabulary Spotlight:
Hypertrophy vs. Dilatation: Hypertrophy is increased wall thickness (like a muscle). Dilatation is increased chamber diameter (like a stretched balloon). Both are types of "Enlargement".
