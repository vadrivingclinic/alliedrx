Compartment Syndrome Emergency Response

Sequence the correct emergency response for suspected compartment syndrome. This is a surgical emergency where minutes matter - proper sequence can prevent limb loss.

🚨 SURGICAL EMERGENCY
Compartment Syndrome Timeline: Irreversible muscle damage begins at 4-6 hours, nerve damage at 6-8 hours. Fasciotomy must be performed within 6 hours for optimal outcomes. Every minute counts.
💡 Clinical Assessment Tips
Pain Assessment: Pain out of proportion to injury is the earliest sign. Pain with passive stretch of affected muscles is pathognomonic. Use numeric pain scale (0-10) and document changes over time.
✓ Emergency Response Sequence Correct!
🔍 Correct Compartment Syndrome Emergency Response Sequence:

1. Recognize signs/symptoms (6 P's assessment)
• Pain: Out of proportion, unrelieved by elevation/analgesics
• Paresthesia: Numbness/tingling in affected nerve distribution
• Pallor: Pale, mottled, or cyanotic skin
• Paralysis: Inability to move digits (late sign)
• Pulselessness: Absent distal pulses (very late sign)
• Poikilothermia: Cool extremity compared to contralateral side
• Pain with passive stretch: Most reliable early sign

2. Remove all constrictive dressings/casts
• Cut cast/bivalve immediately - do not wait for orders
• Remove all layers including underlying padding
• Document pre- and post-removal CMS findings
• Never delay for X-ray or specialist consultation

3. Elevate extremity to heart level ONLY
• NOT above heart level (decreases arterial pressure)
• Maintain neutral position to optimize perfusion
• Use pillows for support - avoid pressure points
• Document positioning and any changes in symptoms

4. Notify provider/surgeon immediately
• Use rapid communication: "This is an emergency"
• Provide concise SBAR report (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation)
• Document time of notification and provider response
• Request immediate orthopedic/surgical consultation

5. Measure compartment pressures if trained
• Normal: <10-12 mmHg
• Concerning: 20-30 mmHg
• Critical: >30-35 mmHg (emergency fasciotomy needed)
• Delta P (diastolic BP - compartment pressure) <30 mmHg = surgical emergency
• Document pressures, technique, and location of measurements

6. Prepare for emergency fasciotomy
• Notify OR staff immediately
• Ensure informed consent process initiated
• Prepare emergency surgery paperwork
• Coordinate patient transfer if needed
• Document all preparations and communications

7. Document EVERYTHING in real-time
• Time sequence of events
• All assessments and interventions
• Provider notifications and responses
• Patient/family education provided
• Disposition and follow-up plan

⚠️ CRITICAL TIME POINTS:
• 0-2 hours: Optimal window for intervention
• 4-6 hours: Irreversible muscle damage begins
• 6-8 hours: Permanent nerve damage
• >8 hours: High risk of amputation

📋 Legal Documentation Requirements:
• Time of symptom onset (patient report)
• Time of first provider notification
• All CMS checks with exact times
• Response to interventions
• Informed consent discussions
• Transfer of care documentation

🔍 Comprehensive SOAP Note Documentation Guide:

S: SUBJECTIVE (Patient's story in their own words)
• Chief Complaint (CC): "Left ankle pain after fall" - Use patient's exact words in quotes
• History of Present Illness (HPI): OLDCARTS (Onset, Location, Duration, Characteristics, Aggravating/Alleviating factors, Radiation, Timing, Severity)
• Patient's description of mechanism of injury: "I twisted my ankle stepping off the curb"
• Patient-reported symptoms: Pain level (0-10 scale), swelling, clicking, instability
• Relevant medical/surgical history: Previous injuries, surgeries, chronic conditions
• Medications/allergies: Complete list with dosages and allergy reactions
• Social history: Smoking, alcohol, occupation, recreational activities
• Review of systems: Constitutional, musculoskeletal, neurological, vascular

O: OBJECTIVE (Measurable, observable data)
• Vital signs: BP, HR, RR, Temp, SpO2, pain score
• Physical exam findings: Inspection (deformity, swelling, ecchymosis), palpation (tenderness, warmth), ROM (active/passive), strength (MMT 0-5), special tests
• Neurovascular assessment (CMS): Circulation (capillary refill, pulses, color), Motor (MMT), Sensation (light touch, pinprick)
• Diagnostic test results: X-ray findings (fracture type, displacement, alignment), MRI/CT results if available, lab values
• Measurements: Girth measurements (cm), goniometer readings (degrees), limb length discrepancy
• Wound assessment if present: Size, depth, drainage, odor, surrounding tissue

A: ASSESSMENT (Clinical reasoning and diagnosis)
• Diagnosis/differential diagnosis: Primary diagnosis first, followed by differentials
• Problem list: Numbered list of active problems with status (acute/chronic)
• Clinical reasoning: Why this diagnosis fits the presentation
• Risk assessment: Compartment syndrome risk, nonunion risk, infection risk
• Progress assessment: Improving, worsening, or unchanged from previous visit
• Complication assessment: Any identified or potential complications
• Patient understanding: Assessment of patient's comprehension of condition

P: PLAN (Management and follow-up)
• Diagnostic plan: Additional tests needed (repeat X-ray, MRI, labs)
• Therapeutic plan: Treatment interventions (casting, surgery, PT, medications)
• Patient education provided: Cast care, warning signs, activity restrictions
• Follow-up plan: Appointment scheduling, expected timeline
• Referrals/consultations: Orthopedics, PT, OT, pain management
• Medication management: New prescriptions, changes, refills
• Discharge instructions if applicable: ED discharge or post-op instructions

⚠️ LEGAL DOCUMENTATION ESSENTIALS:
• Timeliness: Document in real-time or as soon as possible after encounter
• Accuracy: Use objective language, avoid opinions and assumptions
• Completeness: Address all components of SOAP note
• Chronology: Document events in correct time sequence
• Authentication: Sign with name, credentials, date, and time
• Corrections: Single line through error, initial, date, write correction
• Abbreviations: Use only approved abbreviations from facility list

🎯 QUALITY INDICATORS FOR ORTHOPEDIC DOCUMENTATION:
• 100% documentation of CMS checks pre/post procedure
• 100% documentation of informed consent
• 100% documentation of medication reconciliation
• 100% documentation of fall risk assessment
• 100% documentation of discharge instructions
• Timely completion (within 24 hours for outpatient, end of shift for inpatient)
• Patient identification on every page
• Allergies documented in prominent location

🚨 Clinical Insights: Emergency Response

  • Compartment Syndrome Timing: The 6-hour rule: Fasciotomy within 6 hours has 90% good outcomes, 6-12 hours has 50% good outcomes, >12 hours has poor outcomes with high amputation risk. Document exact time of symptom onset and all interventions.
  • Pain Assessment: Pain out of proportion is subjective but critical. Use standardized scales (0-10) and document response to interventions. Pain unrelieved by opioids is a red flag. Document character (burning, throbbing, sharp), location, radiation.
  • Neurovascular Checks: CMS (Circulation, Motor, Sensation) must be documented q1-2h for high-risk injuries. Use consistent methodology: capillary refill <2 seconds, motor function specific to nerve distribution, sensation to light touch and pinprick.
  • Communication: Use SBAR (Situation, Background, Assessment, Recommendation) for provider communication. Document time of notification, provider name, exact message, and response. Repeat back critical orders for verification.
  • Legal Documentation: "If it wasn't documented, it wasn't done." Document in real-time, use objective language, avoid opinions. Include patient quotes in subjective section. Document informed consent discussions and patient understanding.

📋 Documentation & Legal Considerations

  • SOAP Note Structure: S: Patient's story (quotes), O: Measurable data, A: Clinical reasoning, P: Specific actions. Each section must flow logically to the next. Assessment should explain how objective findings support subjective complaints.
  • Risk Management: Document discussions of risks/benefits, alternative treatments, and patient questions/concerns. Include "informed refusal" if patient declines recommended treatment. Document shared decision-making process.
  • Medication Documentation: Include indication, dose, route, frequency, duration. Document patient education about side effects, interactions, administration instructions. Include monitoring parameters and follow-up plans.
  • Quality Indicators: Monitor documentation completeness (CMS checks, informed consent, discharge instructions), timeliness (within 24 hours), accuracy (correct diagnosis codes), and patient identification (2 identifiers on every page).
