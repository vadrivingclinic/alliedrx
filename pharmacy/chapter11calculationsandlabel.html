Temperature

Affects physical stability: melting, crystallization, and phase separation in creams and ointments.

Humidity

Causes chemical degradation through hydrolysis reactions, especially in tablets and capsules.

Light Exposure

Leads to photolysis - breakdown of active ingredients by UV/visible light. Requires amber containers.

Oxygen Exposure

Causes oxidation reactions with active ingredients. Nitrogen flushing can help preserve stability.

pH Level

Affects hydrolysis rates. Optimal pH buffers improve stability in liquid formulations.

Water Activity

High water activity promotes microbial growth. Critical for aqueous preparations and creams.

Stability Management Strategies:
1. Temperature Control: Store according to manufacturer specifications
2. Moisture Protection: Use desiccants for hygroscopic medications
3. Light Protection: Use amber containers for light-sensitive drugs
4. Oxygen Exclusion: Nitrogen flush or oxygen scavengers
5. pH Optimization: Buffering systems for stability
6. Water Activity Control: Humectants or drying agents

💊 Pharmaceutical Insights

  • BUD Calculations: The most critical factor in BUD determination is water content. Aqueous preparations have shorter BUDs due to microbial growth risk, while non-aqueous preparations can have BUDs up to 180 days.
  • Label Compliance: Missing lot numbers or incorrect BUDs are among the most common labeling errors found during pharmacy audits. These errors can lead to medication recalls and regulatory citations.
  • Stability Factors: Understanding how temperature, humidity, light, and other factors affect medication stability is essential for proper storage and handling instructions.
