⚖️ Precision Tip
Always use the appropriate class of balance. Use an analytical balance (0.1mg precision) for weights less than 100mg, and a torsion or electronic balance (1mg precision) for larger quantities.
Calculation Challenge: Prepare 60mL of a 2.5% w/v solution.
How many grams of active ingredient do you need?

Instructions: Remember that % w/v means "weight/volume". 2.5% w/v means 2.5 grams of solute per 100 mL of solution. Use this formula: (Percentage ÷ 100) × Volume = Grams needed
📝 Calculation Help
Step-by-step solution:
1. 2.5% w/v = 2.5g per 100mL
2. For 60mL: (2.5g ÷ 100mL) × 60mL
3. = 0.025 × 60
4. = 1.5 grams
Always double-check your calculations!

USP <795> Compliance Checklist:

🐕
Veterinary Compounding Case - Detailed Instructions
Golden Retriever, 65 lbs (29.5 kg)
Prescription: Carprofen 50mg/mL oral suspension
Dose: 2.2 mg/kg twice daily
Quantity: 30-day supply

Instructions:
1. First, convert the dog's weight from pounds to kilograms (1 kg = 2.2 lbs)
2. Calculate the dose per administration: Weight (kg) × Dose (mg/kg)
3. Calculate volume per dose: Dose (mg) ÷ Concentration (mg/mL)
4. Calculate total carprofen needed: Dose (mg) × Frequency (2× daily) × Days (30)
5. Enter your answers below and check your calculations
🐾 Veterinary Compounding Tip
Important considerations for veterinary compounding:
• Animals metabolize medications differently than humans
• Palatability is crucial - animals won't take bad-tasting medicine
• Accurate weight measurement is essential for dosing
• Some human medications are toxic to animals
• Always verify with a veterinarian when compounding for animals
