Clinical Application:
• Low-risk CSPs (e.g., simple IV admixtures) have BUD of up to 48 hours at room temp
• Medium-risk CSPs (batch compounding) require more stringent testing and shorter BUD
• High-risk CSPs (non-sterile ingredients) require sterilization and validation

Pro Tip: Always verify risk level before assigning BUD. Incorrect classification can compromise sterility and patient safety.

Correct Sequence:
1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Don PPE in anteroom
3. Clean hood surface with 70% IPA
4. Gather and clean supplies
5. Place items in hood without blocking airflow
6. Maintain first air protection during compounding

Critical Safety Points:
• First air = air exiting HEPA filter before encountering obstacles
• Critical sites = vial tops, needle hubs, injection ports must remain sterile
• Work zone = 6 inches inside hood edges for optimal airflow

Common Errors to Avoid:
1. Reaching over sterile field (breaks first air)
2. Blocking hood grilles with supplies
3. Using expired sterile supplies
4. Failing to disinfect vial tops before entry

🏥 Clinical Application Insights

  • Risk Level Determines BUD: Low-risk CSPs = up to 48 hours; Medium-risk = up to 30 hours; High-risk = up to 24 hours (when stored at room temperature). Always verify storage conditions.
  • First Air Protection: Critical sites must remain in the path of first air. Any break in technique (reaching over, talking directly over hood) compromises sterility.
  • USP <800> for Hazardous Drugs: Requires closed-system transfer devices, specialized PPE, and separate compounding area. Technician competency must be documented annually.

💊 Pharmacy Technician Practice Points

  • Documentation is Critical: Every CSP requires complete labeling including patient ID, ingredients, concentration, BUD, storage requirements, and preparer's initials.
  • Quality Assurance: Regular media-fill testing and gloved fingertip sampling are required by USP <797> to verify aseptic technique competency.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Know procedures for spills (especially hazardous drugs), power failures affecting hoods, and product recalls affecting CSPs.
