Correct ISO Classifications:
• ISO Class 5 → Laminar airflow hood surface
• ISO Class 7 → Clean room (buffer area)
• ISO Class 8 → Anteroom (ante-area)

Correct Hood Applications:
• Vancomycin IV → Horizontal Laminar Flow Hood
• Chemotherapy → Biological Safety Cabinet (Vertical)
• TPN with lipids → Horizontal Laminar Flow Hood
• Hazardous powder → Containment Aseptic Isolator (CACI)

Critical Rationale:
1. Horizontal vs Vertical: Horizontal protects product, Vertical protects operator AND product
2. TPN in Horizontal: Lipids can coat HEPA in vertical, causing airflow issues
3. CACI for powders: Complete containment for hazardous powder reconstitution
4. ISO Class 5 = Hood: Where actual compounding occurs, requires cleanest air

Airflow Visualization:
• Horizontal: Air flows from back to front, parallel to work surface
• Vertical: Air flows top to bottom, creating air curtain at opening
• First Air: Always keep critical sites in undisturbed first air from HEPA

Compliance Requirements:
• Hood certification: Every 6 months
• ISO Class 7 room: <352,000 particles ≥0.5μm per cubic meter
• ISO Class 5 hood: <3,520 particles ≥0.5μm per cubic meter

🛡️ Sterile Compounding Insights

  • Contamination Control: Every step in PPE donning has a purpose. Hand hygiene first removes transient flora. Mask before shoes prevents floor contamination of mask ties. The sequence isn't arbitrary—it's engineered to minimize contamination risk.
  • First Air Principle: In horizontal hoods, "first air" is the undisturbed air coming directly from the HEPA filter. Critical sites (vial stoppers, syringe tips) must remain in this zone. Placing items behind others creates turbulence and contamination risk.
  • ISO Classification Logic: ISO Class 5 (hood), 7 (clean room), 8 (anteroom) represent exponential increases in allowable particles. Understanding these helps you appreciate why certain activities are restricted to specific areas.

📋 Pharmacy Technician Application

  • Anteroom Vigilance: As a technician, the anteroom is YOUR responsibility. Proper wiping prevents clean room contamination. Remember: if it touches medications or hood surface, it gets wiped with 70% IPA.
  • Hood Selection: Technician must verify correct hood type before compounding. Horizontal for TPN (lipid issue), Vertical BSC for chemo (operator protection), CACI for hazardous powders. Wrong hood = patient AND operator risk.
  • Documentation Chain: From hood certifications (every 6 months) to cleaning logs, technicians maintain the documentation proving USP <797> compliance. Missing documentation = failed inspection.
