Correct USP <800> Sequence:
1. Verify drug is on hazardous list
2. Prepare in designated hazardous area
3. Don specialized PPE (double gloves, gown, eye protection)
4. Use closed-system transfer devices (CSTDs)
5. Compound in biological safety cabinet or CACI
6. Label with hazardous drug warning
7. Dispose waste in designated containers
8. Remove PPE properly, wash hands

Critical Safety Points:
• CSTDs are REQUIRED by USP <800> for hazardous drug transfers
• Double gloving with chemo-rated gloves (tested for permeation)
• Separate waste streams for hazardous vs. regular waste
• Negative pressure in hazardous compounding area

Spill Management:
1. Evacuate area, alert others
2. Don spill kit PPE
3. Contain spill with absorbent
4. Clean with designated solutions
5. Dispose as hazardous waste
6. Document incident

⚠️ USP <800> Compliance Insights

  • Closed-System Transfer Devices (CSTDs): REQUIRED for all hazardous drug transfers. These devices prevent vapor and aerosol escape during compounding. Common brands include PhaSeal® and Equashield®.
  • PPE Hierarchy: Double chemotherapy-rated gloves (changed every 30 minutes), disposable impermeable gown with tight cuffs, eye/face protection. All PPE must be donned and doffed in specific sequence.
  • Environmental Controls: Hazardous drugs require separate negative-pressure room, dedicated biological safety cabinet or CACI, and HEPA-filtered exhaust. Regular environmental wipe testing is required.

💉 Syringe & Needle Selection Guidelines

  • Needle Gauge Selection: 18-20G for viscous drugs or large volume withdrawal; 21-23G for standard IV medications; 25-27G for insulin, heparin, or subcutaneous injections. Larger gauge = smaller needle diameter.
  • Filter Needle Protocol: MUST use new filter needle when withdrawing from ampules to remove glass particles. NEVER use same needle to withdraw and administer. Filter needles are single-use only.
  • Syringe Size Matters: Use smallest syringe that accommodates volume needed (improves accuracy). Insulin syringes are U-100 calibrated (100 units = 1 mL). Tuberculin syringes for small volumes (<1 mL).
