Key Terminology Mastery

Select each card to reveal definitions from Chapter 1. Practice explaining terms in your own words as if teaching a new AlliedRx pharmacy technician student.

Apothecary
Historical term for pharmacist; also referred to the shop where medicines were prepared and sold. The predecessor to modern pharmacies.
Bloodletting
Ancient medical practice of withdrawing blood to treat illness, based on the humor theory of balancing bodily fluids.
Laudanum
A tincture of opium (approximately 10% opium powder in alcohol) widely used as a painkiller and "cure-all" in the 18th-19th centuries.
Opiate vs. Opioid
Opiate: Natural alkaloids from opium poppy (morphine, codeine). Opioid: All substances (natural, semi-synthetic, synthetic) that bind to opioid receptors.
Pharmacy Technician
Healthcare professional who assists pharmacists with prescription preparation, inventory management, and patient service under pharmacist supervision.
PTCB
Pharmacy Technician Certification Board - National organization that develops and administers the PTCE certification exam for pharmacy technicians.

Historical Context

Medicine & Pharmacy Evolution

Key milestones that shaped modern healthcare and pharmacy practice:

Ancient Times (Pre-1700s): Trephining, bloodletting, herbal remedies. Hippocrates establishes medical ethics.
1700s-1800s: Laudanum widespread. Scientific method applied to medicine. First pharmacies established in colonial America.
1928: Alexander Fleming discovers penicillin, revolutionizing infection treatment.
Mid-20th Century: Formalization of pharmacy education. Development of synthetic drugs. Hospital pharmacy expansion.
1970s-Present: Clinical pharmacy emerges. Technician roles formalized. Computerization of pharmacy practice. Specialty pharmacies develop.
