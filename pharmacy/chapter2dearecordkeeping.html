DEA Numbers

Understanding Drug Enforcement Administration identifiers for controlled substance handlers

What is a DEA Number?

A DEA (Drug Enforcement Administration) number is a unique identifier assigned to individuals and organizations that handle controlled substances. Pharmacy technicians working in facilities that store and dispense controlled substances must understand DEA number validation.

DEA Number Format & Validation

Format Structure
DEA numbers follow this format: 2 letters + 7 digits
Example: AB1234567
  • First letter: Registrant type (B = Hospital/Clinic, F = Pharmacy, etc.)
  • Second letter: First letter of registrant's last name
  • Seven digits: Unique identifier with check digit
Validation Formula
Check Digit Calculation:
  1. Add first, third, and fifth digits
  2. Add second, fourth, and sixth digits and multiply by 2
  3. Sum both results
  4. The last digit of sum must match the seventh digit

Record Keeping Requirements

Essential documentation for controlled substance handling and compliance

Critical Documentation

Prescription Information
Must include:
  • Patient's full name and DOB
  • Prescriber's DEA number
  • Medication name, strength, quantity
  • Dispensing date and pharmacy information
  • Refill authorization and limits
Inventory Records
Must track:
  • Initial counts and periodic inventories
  • Purchase orders and receipts
  • Dispensing records by day
  • Returns and adjustments
  • Discrepancy reports (DEA Form 106)
Patient Profiles
Maintain for each patient:
  • Contact information and demographics
  • Medical history and conditions
  • Known allergies and sensitivities
  • Current medication list
  • Insurance and payment information
📄

DEA Form 222

Ordering Schedule II Substances

Purpose: Used to order Schedule II controlled substances
  • Triplicate carbonless form
  • Copy 1: Sent to supplier
  • Copy 2: Filed by pharmacy
  • Copy 3: Retained by supplier
  • Must be stored in secure location for 2 years
📄

DEA Form 41

Registrant's Inventory of Drugs Surrendered

Purpose: Documentation for destruction of controlled substances
  • Used when disposing of controlled substances
  • Requires witness signatures
  • Must be submitted to DEA within 30 days
  • Retain copy for 2 years
  • Includes drug name, strength, quantity, and disposal method

Destruction of Controlled Substances

Safe and compliant disposal procedures for expired or unwanted medications

1
Identify & Segregate
  • Regularly review inventory for expired medications
  • Separate controlled substances from non-controlled
  • Store in secure area pending destruction
  • Document inventory of items to be destroyed
2
Prepare Documentation
  • Complete DEA Form 41 for each destruction event
  • Include drug name, strength, quantity, and lot numbers
  • Obtain required witness signatures (usually two)
  • Make copies for pharmacy records
3
Choose Disposal Method
  • Authorized Reverse Distributor: Most common method
  • DEA Take-Back Events: Community disposal events
  • On-Site Destruction: Using DEA-approved methods
  • Mail-Back Programs: Pre-paid return packages
4
Execute & Document
  • Follow chosen disposal method procedures
  • Ensure witness observes actual destruction
  • Complete all sections of DEA Form 41
  • Submit original to DEA within 30 days
  • Retain copy for 2 years minimum

✅ Destruction Compliance Checklist

Inventory Review Completed
Monthly review of controlled substance inventory for expired items
Proper Segregation
Controlled substances separated from regular waste in secure storage
DEA Form 41 Prepared
Complete documentation with all required information
Witness Signatures Obtained
Two authorized witnesses present during destruction
Environmental Compliance
Disposal method follows EPA and state environmental regulations
Records Retention
All documentation retained for minimum 2 years as required by DEA
⚠️

CRITICAL: Diversion Prevention

Controlled substance diversion is a federal crime. Always maintain chain of custody from receipt through destruction. Document every transfer and disposal. Report any discrepancies immediately using DEA Form 106. Failure to properly document destruction can result in DEA registration revocation, fines up to $25,000 per violation, and imprisonment.

🛡️ Key Compliance Takeaways

  • DEA Number Validation: Always verify DEA numbers using the check digit formula to prevent fraudulent prescriptions.
  • Documentation Retention: Maintain all controlled substance records for minimum 2 years. DEA can audit records at any time.
  • Proper Disposal: Use DEA Form 41 for all controlled substance destructions. Never dispose in regular trash or sewer systems.
  • Inventory Control: Conduct regular inventories and investigate any discrepancies immediately using DEA Form 106.
  • Ongoing Education: DEA regulations update frequently. Complete annual compliance training to stay current with requirements.
