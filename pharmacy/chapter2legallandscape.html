DEA Schedule Overview:
• Schedule I: No accepted medical use, high abuse (Heroin, LSD, Marijuana*)
• Schedule II: High abuse, severe dependence (Oxycodone, Fentanyl, Adderall)
• Schedule III: Moderate abuse (Testosterone, Ketamine, Tylenol with Codeine)
• Schedule IV: Low abuse (Xanax, Ambien, Valium)
• Schedule V: Limited abuse (Lyrica, Lomotil, some cough preparations)

Key Regulations:
• C-II: No refills, written Rx required (except emergencies)
• C-III-V: Max 5 refills within 6 months
• C-V: Some may be OTC depending on state law
*Note: Marijuana remains Schedule I federally despite state laws.

Identify which agency—OSHA or The Joint Commission (TJC)—is primarily responsible for various pharmacy safety and quality standards.

Requires a Safety Data Sheet (SDS) for hazardous chemicals.
Conducts unannounced surveys for hospital accreditation.
Mandates a written Exposure Control Plan for bloodborne pathogens.
Sets standards for medication storage and security.
🩺 Clinical Connection
OSHA protects employee safety. TJC ensures patient safety and quality of care. Both are essential! Proper hazardous drug handling, for example, satisfies both OSHA's employee protection standards and TJC's patient safety requirements.
✓ Safety Agencies Clarified!
OSHA (Occupational Safety & Health Administration):
• Focus: Employee workplace safety
• Key Requirements: SDS sheets, Exposure Control Plans, PPE, hazard communication
• Enforcement: Fines and citations for violations

TJC (The Joint Commission):
• Focus: Patient safety & quality of care
• Key Requirements: Medication management, infection control, patient rights
• Enforcement: Accreditation status (required for Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement)

Practical Tip: While their focuses differ, compliance with both is essential for safe, effective, and legally compliant pharmacy practice.

DEA Number Verification Formula:
1. Add: 1st + 3rd + 5th digits
2. Add: 2nd + 4th + 6th digits, then multiply by 2
3. Add both sums together
4. Last digit of total must match check digit (7th digit)
For AB1234563:
(1+3+5) + 2*(2+4+6) = 9 + 2*12 = 9 + 24 = 33
Last digit is 3 ✓ Check digit is 3 ✓
🤔 Did You Know?
A prescriber's DEA number follows a specific format: First letter = license type (A, B, or M), Second letter = first letter of last name. For example, AB1234563 indicates the registrant's last name starts with "B".
✓ DEA Procedures Mastered!
Key DEA Forms:
• Form 222: Required for ordering Schedule II substances (now largely electronic as CSOS)
• Form 106: Report lost/stolen controlled substances
• Form 41: Destroy unwanted controlled substances

DEA Number Verification:
• Always verify prescriber DEA numbers - fake numbers are common in fraud
• Formula: (1st+3rd+5th) + 2*(2nd+4th+6th). Last digit of sum = check digit
• Example: AB1234563 → (1+3+5) + 2*(2+4+6) = 9 + 24 = 33 → check digit = 3 ✓

Critical Safety Step: Validating DEA numbers prevents diversion and protects your pharmacy's license.

⚖️ Legal & Regulatory Insights

  • Historical Context: Each drug law was a response to public health crises. The 1906 Act addressed patent medicines, the 1938 Act followed the Elixir Sulfanilamide tragedy, and the 1970 CSA addressed drug abuse epidemics.
  • Agency Jurisdiction: FDA = safety/efficacy, DEA = controlled substances, OSHA = employee safety, TJC = patient care quality. Know which agency governs each aspect of pharmacy practice.
  • Controlled Substance Hierarchy: Schedule I (no medical use) to V (limited abuse). C-II has the strictest controls: no refills, written Rx required (except emergencies), and special inventory requirements.
  • Recall Protocols: Class I (remove immediately), Class II (remove promptly), Class III (may not require removal). Always follow manufacturer and FDA guidance exactly.
  • Verification Culture: Always verify DEA numbers, check recall lists daily, and double-check controlled substance counts. These verifications protect patients and your license.

📋 Compliance Documentation

  • Record Retention: Controlled substance records must be kept for 2 years (federal minimum), but many states require longer. Know your state's specific requirements.
  • Training Documentation: Document all staff training on controlled substances, HIPAA, and safety procedures. This is evidence of due diligence during inspections.
  • Inspection Preparedness: DEA inspections can be unannounced. Maintain organized logs of all C-II transactions, discrepancy reports, and destruction records.
  • Quality Indicators: Monitor controlled substance discrepancy rates, recall response times, and training completion rates as quality metrics.
