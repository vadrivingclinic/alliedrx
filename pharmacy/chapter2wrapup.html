Key Agencies

Regulatory Agencies & Functions

Understand the roles and responsibilities of major pharmacy regulatory bodies:

FDA (Food & Drug Admin)
Ensures safety, efficacy of drugs/devices; approves new medications; monitors drug recalls
DEA (Drug Enforcement Admin)
Enforces controlled substance laws; regulates Schedule I-V drugs; combats drug diversion
State Board of Pharmacy
Licenses pharmacists/technicians; regulates pharmacy practice within the state
OSHA (Occupational Safety)
Ensures workplace safety; requires SDS documentation; regulates hazardous material handling
The Joint Commission
Accredits healthcare organizations; sets quality/safety standards for hospitals
USP (United States Pharmacopeia)
Sets medication quality standards; publishes USP-NF with drug monographs

Safety Protocols

FDA Drug Recall Classification

Understanding recall classifications is essential for pharmacy technicians:

Class I Recall
Most Serious: Reasonable probability that use will cause serious adverse health consequences or death
Class II Recall
Intermediate: Use may cause temporary or medically reversible adverse health consequences
Class III Recall
Least Serious: Use is not likely to cause adverse health consequences

Technician Responsibility: Immediately remove recalled medications from inventory and follow pharmacy protocol.

Historical Context

Federal Drug Law Timeline

Key Legislation in Chronological Order

1906: Pure Food and Drug Act - First federal law regulating drugs
1938: Food, Drug & Cosmetic Act - Required safety proof before marketing
1951: Durham-Humphrey Amendment - Created prescription drug category
1970: Controlled Substances Act - Established Schedules I-V
1990: OBRA '90 - Required patient counseling, DUE programs
1996: HIPAA - Protected patient privacy/health information
2010: Affordable Care Act - Expanded healthcare coverage
2013: DQSA - Enhanced drug supply chain security

Memorization Tip: Use the acronym "PFD-DC-OH-D" to remember major acts in order.

Step 2

Essential Terminology

Select each term to reveal its definition:

HIPAA
Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act - Federal law protecting patient health information privacy and security.
PHI
Protected Health Information - Any identifiable patient health data protected under HIPAA regulations.
DEA Number
Unique identifier for prescribers authorized to prescribe controlled substances; follows specific format with verification formula.
Boxed Warning
FDA's strongest warning for serious or life-threatening risks; appears in black box on drug labeling.
NDC Number
National Drug Code - 10-digit identifier specifying manufacturer, product, and package size.
Drug Diversion
Illegal channeling of regulated drugs from legal sources to illegal marketplace; serious federal offense.
