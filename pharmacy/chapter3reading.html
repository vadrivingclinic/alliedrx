Key Career & Professional Terms
Nondiscretionary Duties
Tasks requiring no professional judgment: inventory, billing, repackaging
CPhT
Certified Pharmacy Technician - Passed national exam, uses CPhT credential
ASHP Model Curriculum
Standardized training guidelines for pharmacy technician education
Professionalism
Conforming to ethical principles and work standards of the profession
Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)
IV nutrition for patients unable to eat; requires sterile compounding
Competency
Capability to perform specific functions proficiently in pharmacy settings
Confidentiality
Protecting patient information from unauthorized disclosure (HIPAA)
Continuing Education (CE)
Required education for maintaining certification and license renewal
Professional Associations for Technicians
AAPT (1979)
First pharmacy technician association, advocacy and education
NPTA (1999)
National Pharmacy Technician Association, primarily for technicians
ASHP (1942)
American Society of Health-System Pharmacists, sets Model Curriculum
APhA (1852)
Oldest pharmacy association, represents all pharmacy professionals
National Certification Comparison
PTCB Certification
  • Pharmacy Technician Certification Board
  • Most widely recognized nationally
  • Required in many states
  • 900-question exam pool
  • 20 CE hours every 2 years
NHA/ExCPT Certification
  • National Healthcareer Association
  • ExCPT exam (Exam for Certification)
  • Accepted in many states
  • Focus on practical skills
  • 20 CE hours every 2 years

Career Development Activities

  • □ Research and compare state requirements for technician registration, licensure, and certification in your state and three neighboring states.
  • □ Create a list of nondiscretionary duties for each pharmacy setting: inpatient, outpatient, and closed-door.
  • □ Visit the websites of AAPT, NPTA, and ASHP to compare membership benefits and costs for students.
  • □ Develop a 2-year professional development plan including certification goals and association memberships.
  • □ Research specialty certifications available (like sterile compounding or chemotherapy) and their requirements.
  • □ Create a sample resume highlighting competencies learned from the ASHP Model Curriculum.

Career Development

Building Your Pharmacy Technician Career

Chapter 3 provides the roadmap for your pharmacy technician career development. Understanding competencies, certifications, and professional associations will help you navigate career advancement opportunities and maximize your professional potential.

Career Pathway Decisions:

  • Certification Choice: PTCB vs. NHA certification based on state requirements and career goals.
  • Setting Selection: Hospital, retail, closed-door, or specialty pharmacy based on interests.
  • Association Membership: Professional networking and continuing education opportunities.
  • Specialization Path: Advanced certifications in sterile compounding, chemotherapy, or inventory management.

Essential Technician Competencies

  • Communication: Clear patient counseling, professional interactions with healthcare team
  • Technical Skills: Prescription processing, inventory management, sterile compounding
  • Legal Compliance: HIPAA, controlled substance handling, state regulations
  • Professionalism: Ethical conduct, confidentiality, continuous learning
Nondiscretionary Duties by Setting:
  • Inpatient: Unit dose packaging, IV admixture, automated dispensing
  • Outpatient: Prescription filling, patient intake, inventory management
  • Closed-door: Bulk packaging, delivery preparation, facility billing
  • All Settings: Stocking, cleaning, equipment maintenance

Certification & Career Advancement

  • Salary Impact: Certified technicians earn 10-20% more on average
  • Job Mobility: Certification facilitates state-to-state job transfers
  • Specialization: Advanced roles in chemotherapy, TPN, nuclear pharmacy
  • Leadership: Opportunities for supervisory and training positions
Continuing Education Sources:
  • Pharmacy Technician Certification Board (PTCB) website
  • National Healthcareer Association (NHA) resources
  • Professional association conferences and webinars
  • Employer-provided training programs
  • Accredited online CE providers
