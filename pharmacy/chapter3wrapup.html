Career Pathways

Pharmacy Practice Settings

Understanding different practice environments for pharmacy technicians:

R Retail/Community

Serves walk-in patients, processes prescriptions, provides OTC recommendations, patient counseling support.

Technician Duties: Prescription processing, inventory, customer service, insurance billing.

H Hospital/Inpatient

Serves hospitalized patients, prepares IV medications, unit dose packaging, operates automated dispensing systems.

Technician Duties: IV preparation, TPN compounding, medication cart filling, sterile compounding.

C Closed-Door

Serves long-term care facilities, nursing homes; medications delivered, not open to public walk-in.

Technician Duties: Bulk medication packaging, facility deliveries, medication cart preparation.

S Specialty Pharmacy

Focuses on complex, high-cost medications for chronic conditions (oncology, HIV, transplant).

Technician Duties: Prior authorization, patient assistance programs, specialty medication handling.

Essential Skills

Core Technician Competencies

Successful pharmacy technicians demonstrate these key competencies across all settings:

1
Technical Knowledge: Drug names, classifications, dosage forms, calculations, pharmacy software systems.
2
Communication Skills: Clear patient interaction, professional team communication, accurate documentation.
3
Attention to Detail: Accuracy in prescription processing, labeling, inventory management, data entry.
4
Ethical Practice: Maintain confidentiality, follow regulations, demonstrate honesty and integrity.
5
Problem-Solving: Identify and resolve medication issues, insurance problems, workflow challenges.
6
Time Management: Prioritize tasks, manage workflow efficiently, meet deadlines in fast-paced environment.

Professional Credentials

National Certification Options

PTCB vs. NHA/ExCPT Certification

PTCB Certification
  • Administered by Pharmacy Technician Certification Board
  • 90 multiple-choice questions, 1 hour 50 minutes
  • CPhT credential after passing
  • Requires 20 CE hours every 2 years (including 1 hour pharmacy law, 1 hour patient safety)
  • Recognized in all 50 states
NHA/ExCPT Certification
  • Administered by National Healthcareer Association
  • 100 multiple-choice questions, 2 hours 10 minutes
  • CPhT credential after passing
  • Requires 20 CE hours every 2 years
  • Also recognized nationally

AlliedRx Recommendation: Research your state requirements and employer preferences when choosing certification.

Essential Terminology

Select each term to reveal its definition:

Nondiscretionary Duties
Tasks that do not require professional judgment, such as repackaging medications, inventory management, and filling automated dispensing machines.
CPhT
Certified Pharmacy Technician - Credential earned after passing national certification exam; indicates validated knowledge and skills.
TPN
Total Parenteral Nutrition - Intravenous nutrition administered through central vein for patients unable to eat solids or liquids.
ASHP Model Curriculum
Guidelines for pharmacy technician training programs to ensure comprehensive education meeting professional standards.
Professionalism
Conforming to accepted principles of conduct in the pharmacy profession, including ethics, appearance, communication, and work habits.
Closed-Door Pharmacy
Pharmacy serving institutions like nursing homes; medications delivered, not open to public walk-in traffic.
