💡 Did You Know?
Mistakes with abbreviations cause over 7,000 medication errors annually. The Joint Commission's "Do Not Use" list was created after fatal errors involving abbreviations like "U" (misread as "0") and "QD" (misread as "QID").
⚠️ Safety Alert
Trailing zeros are dangerous! "1.0 mg" can be misread as "10 mg" leading to a 10x overdose. Always write "1 mg" without the zero. Leading zeros ARE required: "0.1 mg" not ".1 mg" to avoid misreading as "1 mg".

Sort these abbreviations into "Safe to Use" vs. "Do Not Use" according to The Joint Commission's dangerous abbreviation list. Hover over abbreviations to see why they're dangerous.

✓ Abbreviation Safety Analysis
Correct Classification:
Do Not Use: U (unit), QD, µg, 1.0 mg (trailing zero), HS
Safe to Use: IU, daily (write out), mcg, 1 mg (no trailing zero), bedtime (write out)

Key Safety Points:
• U vs IU: "U" can look like a zero or "cc" - always write "unit" or use "IU"
• QD vs daily: QD is easily misread as QID (4x daily) - write "daily"
• µg vs mcg: The µg symbol can look like mg when handwritten
• Trailing zeros: Never use after decimal point (1.0 → 1)
• Leading zeros: Always use before decimal point (.1 → 0.1)

💡 Quick Tip: Suspensions
Always shake well before use! Suspensions settle over time. Instruct patients to shake for 10-15 seconds. Examples: Amoxicillin suspension, antacid suspensions.
💡 Quick Tip: Tablets vs Capsules
Tablets: Can be scored (split), often coated for swallowing ease.
Capsules: Contain powder or beads, should NOT be opened unless specifically instructed (e.g., sprinkle on applesauce).
✓ Dosage Forms Mastered
Correct Matches:
• Suspension → Susp → PO (oral)
• Tablet → Tab → PO (oral)
• Cream → Crm → Top (topical)
• Capsule → Cap → PO (oral)
• Injection → Inj → IV (intravenous)

Why This Matters for Mary:
1. Correct Processing: Each form has specific handling requirements
2. Patient Instructions: Different forms require different administration instructions
3. Storage: Forms affect storage requirements (e.g., suspensions often need refrigeration)

Common Errors to Avoid:
• Don't confuse "Susp" (suspension) with "Syrup" (liquid already dissolved)
• "Crm" is for cream (water-based), "Oint" is for ointment (oil-based)
• "PO" means by mouth, "NG" means nasogastric tube (different absorption)
