💡 Did You Know?
First-pass metabolism can eliminate up to 90% of some oral drugs! That's why nitroglycerin is given sublingually - bypassing the liver allows more drug to reach the bloodstream. Understanding pharmacokinetics explains why different routes are used for different drugs.

Key Concepts Learned:
Absorption:
• First-pass effect: Oral drugs metabolized by liver before reaching systemic circulation
• Bioavailability: IV = 100%, Oral = varies (5-100%)
• Routes matter: Sublingual, rectal, transdermal bypass first-pass

Distribution:
• Blood-brain barrier: Lipid-soluble drugs cross easily
• Protein binding: Only free drug is active (warfarin 99% protein bound) • Volume of distribution: High Vd = tissue binding Metabolism:
• Phase I: Oxidation, reduction, hydrolysis (CYP450 enzymes)
• Phase II: Conjugation (makes drugs more water-soluble)
• Enzyme induction/inhibition: Causes drug interactions

Elimination:
• Renal excretion: Affected by kidney function, urine pH
• Biliary excretion: Enterohepatic recycling extends drug effect
• Half-life: Time for drug concentration to reduce by 50%

Orange Book Bioequivalence Challenge

Compare brand vs. generic drug profiles. Determine if generic meets FDA bioequivalence standards (80-125% rule). Learn why bioequivalence matters for patient safety.

📊 FDA Bioequivalence Standard
80-125% Rule: Generic drug must have 90% confidence interval of AUC (area under curve) and Cmax (peak concentration) between 80-125% of brand name. This ensures therapeutic equivalence for most patients.

• Some patients may still need brand for consistency

Key Excipient Functions Learned:

Stabilizers:
• Citrate buffer: Maintains pH stability (amoxicillin degrades in acid)
• EDTA: Chelates metals that catalyze degradation
• Antioxidants: Prevent oxidation (ascorbic acid, BHT)

Absorption Modifiers:
• Surfactants: Improve wetting and dissolution (polysorbate 80)
• Permeation enhancers: Increase membrane permeability (DMSO)
• Cyclodextrins: Form inclusion complexes with poorly soluble drugs

Patient Compliance Enhancers:
• Flavors: Mask bitter tastes (cherry, bubblegum, grape)
• Sweeteners: Improve palatability (sucralose, sorbitol)
• Colors: Visual identification and appeal

Storage-Related Excipients:
• Preservatives: Prevent microbial growth (benzyl alcohol, parabens)
• Humectants: Prevent drying (glycerin, propylene glycol)
• Antimicrobials: Required for multi-dose containers
