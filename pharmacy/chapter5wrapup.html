Core Concept

Pharmacokinetics: ADME Process

Understanding how drugs move through the body (Pharmacokinetics = "What the body does to the drug"):

A
Absorption
Drug enters bloodstream from site of administration
D
Distribution
Drug circulates to tissues & target sites
M
Metabolism
Liver converts drug (usually to inactive form)
E
Elimination
Kidneys remove drug from body (urine)
B
Bioavailability
% of drug that reaches systemic circulation
First-Pass Effect: Oral drugs absorbed from GI tract → portal vein → liver metabolism → reduced bioavailability. IV drugs bypass first-pass metabolism (100% bioavailability).

Drug Delivery

Common Dosage Forms

Pharmacy technicians must recognize various dosage formulations:

T Tablets
Compressed powder: regular, chewable, sublingual, buccal, effervescent
Examples: Aspirin, Lisinopril, Nitroglycerin SL
C Capsules
Gelatin shell containing powder/liquid: hard-shell, soft-gel, sustained-release
Examples: Amoxicillin, Omeprazole, Fish oil
L Liquids
Solutions, suspensions, syrups, elixirs, tinctures, emulsions
Examples: Amoxicillin suspension, Cough syrup, Liquid Tylenol
I Injections
Sterile solutions/suspensions: IV, IM, SC, intradermal
Examples: Insulin, Vaccines, IV antibiotics
T Topicals
Creams, ointments, gels, lotions, patches, sprays
Examples: Hydrocortisone cream, Nitroglycerin patch, Lidocaine gel
S Suppositories
Rectal, vaginal, urethral; melt at body temperature
Examples: Acetaminophen rectal, Antifungal vaginal

Drug Delivery

Routes of Administration

Enteral vs. Parenteral vs. Other Routes

Enteral Routes
• Oral (PO) - by mouth
• Sublingual (SL) - under tongue
• Buccal - between cheek/gum
• Rectal (PR) - suppositories
Parenteral Routes
• Intravenous (IV) - into vein
• Intramuscular (IM) - into muscle
• Subcutaneous (SC) - under skin
• Intradermal - into skin layer
Topical Routes
• Transdermal - through skin
• Ophthalmic - eye drops/ointment
• Otic - ear drops
• Nasal - nasal spray
Other Routes
• Inhalation - lungs (aerosol)
• Vaginal - creams/suppositories
• Intrathecal - spinal fluid
• Intra-articular - joint

Key Distinction: Enteral = through GI tract; Parenteral = bypasses GI tract (usually injections).

Safety Alert

Dangerous Abbreviations

The Joint Commission "Do Not Use" List (Selected Examples)

U or u
Misinterpreted as: 0 (zero), 4 (four), or cc
Use Instead: "units"
IU
Misinterpreted as: IV (intravenous) or 10 (ten)
Use Instead: "International Units"
Q.D., QD, q.d., qd
Misinterpreted as: QID (four times daily)
Use Instead: "daily"
Q.O.D., QOD, q.o.d, qod
Misinterpreted as: QD (daily) or QID
Use Instead: "every other day"
Trailing zero
(X.0 mg)
Misinterpreted as: 10 mg if decimal not seen
Use Instead: X mg
Lack of leading zero
(.X mg)
Misinterpreted as: X mg if decimal not seen
Use Instead: 0.X mg

Pharmacy Technician Responsibility: Always use clear, unambiguous terminology and question unclear abbreviations.

Practical Application

Medication Storage Requirements

Common drugs with specific storage needs that pharmacy technicians must know:

Insulin
Unopened: Refrigerate (2-8°C/36-46°F)
In Use: Room temperature (≤30°C/86°F) for 28 days
Avoid: Freezing, direct sunlight, excessive heat
Nitroglycerin
Tablets: Tight, light-resistant container
Protect from: Light, moisture, heat
Shelf life: 6 months after opening bottle
Amoxicillin Suspension
Dry powder: Room temperature
Reconstituted: Refrigerate (2-8°C)
Discard: After 10-14 days (check label)
Vaccines
Most require: Refrigeration (2-8°C)
Monitor: Temperature logs
Some require: Frozen storage (-15 to -50°C)

General Rule: When in doubt, check package insert, USP standards, or consult pharmacist.

Essential Terminology

Select each term to reveal its definition:

Pharmacokinetics
The study of what the body does to a drug: Absorption, Distribution, Metabolism, and Elimination (ADME).
First-Pass Effect
Metabolism of oral drugs by the liver before reaching systemic circulation, reducing bioavailability.
Half-Life (t½)
Time required for drug concentration in plasma to decrease by 50%; determines dosing frequency.
Bioequivalence
When two drugs with same dosage form have similar bioavailability and produce same therapeutic effect.
Parenteral
Route of administration that bypasses gastrointestinal tract (injections: IV, IM, SC).
Pro-drug
Inactive medication that converts to active form in the body through metabolism.
