Correct Answers:
1. systematic review - A comprehensive literature review method
2. primary outcome - The main result the study is designed to measure
3. secondary outcomes - Additional results of interest
4. PubMed - Primary database for medical literature
5. Cochrane Risk - Standard tool for assessing study quality
6. meta-analysis - Statistical combination of multiple study results
7. major bleeding - Primary safety endpoint for anticoagulant studies
8. number needed to treat - How many patients need treatment to benefit one
9. clinical implications - How findings apply to patient care
10. limitations - Study weaknesses that affect interpretation

Journal Article Structure:
• Abstract: Summary of entire article
• Introduction: Background and study question
• Methods: How the study was conducted
• Results: What was found (data)
• Discussion: Interpretation and implications
• Conclusion: Main takeaway points

CE Relevance: Pharmacy technicians should understand:
• How to locate relevant articles (PubMed, journal websites)
• Basic study designs (RCT, cohort, case-control)
• Common statistical terms (RR, CI, p-value)
• How to identify practice-changing findings

Application to DOAC Monitoring: This study suggests routine lab monitoring may not be necessary for DOACs, unlike Warfarin. Technicians should understand this when preparing patient education materials.

💻 Electronic Resource Insights

  • Database Hierarchy: Micromedex/Clinical Pharmacology > PubMed > General Google. For clinical decisions, use professional databases. For background research, PubMed. Never use general search engines for clinical questions.
  • URL Evaluation: .gov and .edu domains are most reliable for medical information. .org domains vary in quality. .com sites often have commercial bias. Check "About Us" sections for author credentials and funding sources.
  • Update Frequency: Drug information should be updated at least annually. Look for "last reviewed/updated" dates. For rapidly changing fields (oncology, infectious diseases), information should be no more than 6 months old.

📚 Continuing Education & Professional Development

  • Journal Reading Strategy: Start with abstract and conclusion. If relevant, read methods and results. Focus on practice implications. Share key findings with pharmacy team during huddles or meetings.
  • CE Requirements: Most states require 15-20 hours of CE annually for technicians. Electronic resources count when from approved providers. Document all CE activities with certificates for renewal.
  • Research Skills: Technicians who can efficiently research drug information save pharmacists 30-60 minutes daily. This allows pharmacists more time for clinical services and patient counseling.
