📋 Common Prescription Abbreviations:
• b.i.d. = Twice daily (bis in die)
• t.i.d. = Three times daily (ter in die)
• q.i.d. = Four times daily (quater in die)
• p.o. = By mouth (per os)
• p.r.n. = As needed (pro re nata)
• a.c. = Before meals (ante cibum)

⚠️ Safety Note: Always write out abbreviations in plain English on prescription labels to prevent medication errors!

💻 E-Prescribing Advantages:
• Error Reduction: 55% fewer errors than handwritten prescriptions
• Legibility: Eliminates misinterpretation of handwriting
• Efficiency: Direct transmission saves 15-20 minutes per prescription
• Safety: Built-in drug interaction and allergy checks
• Patient Convenience: No need to transport paper prescriptions

📄 Paper Prescription Considerations:
• Still used in some rural areas with limited technology
• Requires careful verification of handwriting
• Physical copy can be lost or damaged
• Must verify DEA number for controlled substances

👤 Required Patient Information:
1. Full Name: For accurate patient identification and matching with insurance
2. Date of Birth: Age verification and pediatric dosing calculations
3. Phone Number: Prescription readiness notifications and follow-up calls
4. Allergies: CRITICAL for preventing adverse reactions - check at EVERY visit
5. Current Medications: Essential for drug interaction screening

⚠️ Safety First: Always ask "Do you have any medication allergies?" even if the patient has visited before.

💳 Insurance Processing Basics:
• BIN (Bank Identification Number): 6-digit number that routes claims to the correct payer
• Member ID: Unique patient identifier within the insurance plan
• Group Number: Employer or plan identifier
• NDC Number: 10-11 digit National Drug Code identifying the specific drug, strength, and package size

🔍 Prescriber Verification Best Practices:
• DEA Number Format: Two letters + seven numbers (e.g., BS1234567)
• First letter: B = individual practitioner, F = manufacturer, etc.
• Second letter: First letter of prescriber's last name
• Always verify for controlled substance prescriptions - it's a legal requirement

💡 Pro Tip: When entering NDC numbers, always double-check: 1. Correct drug identification 2. Correct strength 3. Correct package size 4. Active/inactive status

🏪 Pharmacy Environment Insights

  • Chain vs. Independent: Chain pharmacies offer standardized procedures and purchasing power, while independent pharmacies provide personalized care and local community relationships. Understanding these differences helps technicians adapt to different work environments.
  • Pharmacy Layout Efficiency: Well-organized pharmacy layouts separate patient-facing areas (Rx counter, consultation) from workflow areas (dispensing, inventory). This organization minimizes distractions and maximizes efficiency while maintaining patient privacy.
  • First Impressions Matter: The patient intake process sets the tone for the entire pharmacy experience. Accurate information gathering and professional communication build trust and prevent errors downstream in the prescription process.

📋 Prescription Processing Foundation

  • Prescription Anatomy: Understanding Rx components (superscription, inscription, subscription, signatura) enables technicians to accurately interpret prescriptions and identify potential errors before they reach the dispensing stage.
  • E-Prescribing Revolution: Electronic prescribing reduces medication errors by 55% compared to handwritten prescriptions. Technicians must understand both paper and electronic systems to work effectively in any pharmacy setting.
  • Abbreviation Accuracy: Misinterpretation of Latin abbreviations (b.i.d., t.i.d., p.r.n.) can lead to dosing errors. Consistent use of plain English directions improves patient understanding and medication adherence.

👥 Patient Interaction & Verification

  • Information Gathering: Complete and accurate patient profiles (allergies, current medications) are essential for drug interaction screening. Technicians serve as the first line of defense in medication safety.
  • Prescriber Verification: Validating DEA numbers and prescriber information prevents fraudulent prescriptions and ensures controlled substances are properly authorized. This verification is a legal requirement and patient safety measure.
  • Insurance Fundamentals: Understanding BIN numbers, member IDs, and basic claim submission prepares technicians for the administrative aspects of pharmacy practice that directly impact patient access to medications.
