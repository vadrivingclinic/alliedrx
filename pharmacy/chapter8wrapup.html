Practice Settings

Types of Community Pharmacies

Different community pharmacy settings where technicians may work (based on Andrew's orientation scenario):

🏪 Chain Pharmacy
Corporate-owned (CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid); standardized procedures, central purchasing, brand recognition.
Examples: CVS, Walgreens, Rite Aid
👨‍⚕️ Independent Pharmacy
Locally owned (sole proprietorship); personalized service, may offer specialized services like compounding.
Examples: Family-owned pharmacies, compounding pharmacies
🛒 Grocer Pharmacy
Located inside supermarkets (Kroger, Safeway, Publix); convenient one-stop shopping, may offer clinic services.
Examples: Kroger, Safeway, Publix pharmacies
🏬 Mass Merchandiser
Inside big-box stores (Walmart, Target, Costco); competitive pricing, high volume, membership requirements possible.
Examples: Walmart, Target, Costco pharmacies
📦 Mail-Order Pharmacy
90-day supplies mailed to patients; often used by insurance plans for maintenance medications.
Examples: Express Scripts, OptumRx, CVS Caremark Mail
💊 Specialty Pharmacy
Focuses on complex, high-cost medications (oncology, HIV, MS); requires special handling and patient support.
Examples: Accredo, BriovaRx, OncoMed

Core Knowledge

Prescription Components

Understanding the key parts of a prescription (based on Andrew's observation of prescription acceptance):

Superscription (℞)
The "Rx" symbol meaning "take thou" - indicates this is a prescription order
Inscription
Drug name, dosage form, strength, and quantity (e.g., "Lisinopril 10mg tablets #30")
Subscription
Directions to pharmacist (e.g., "Dispense as written," "No substitutions")
Signatura (Sig)
Patient directions (e.g., "Take 1 tablet by mouth daily") - what appears on label
Prescriber Info
Name, address, phone, DEA number (for controlled substances), NPI number, signature
Patient Info
Name, address, date of birth, allergies, insurance information
Refill Info
Number of refills authorized, date written, expiration date

Workflow Process

Prescription Processing Steps

The Medication Use Process (Andrew's observed workflow)

1
Prescription Drop-off
Accept Rx, verify patient info, check completeness
2
Data Entry
Enter Rx into computer, verify insurance
3
Adjudication
Submit claim to insurance, resolve rejections
4
Filling
Count/measure medication, apply labels
5
Pharmacist Check
Final verification, clinical review

Technician's Role at Each Step

  • Step 1: Collect complete information, verify prescriber DEA if controlled substance
  • Step 2: Accurate data entry, select correct NDC, enter correct SIG codes
  • Step 3: Troubleshoot insurance rejections, contact prescriber if needed
  • Step 4: Accurate counting/measuring, proper labeling, auxiliary labels
  • Step 5: Prepare prescription for pharmacist review, flag any concerns

Work Environment

Community Pharmacy Layout

Key areas in a typical community pharmacy (where Andrew will be working):

Drop-off/Consultation
Patient interaction area, privacy for counseling, new Rx acceptance
Workstation/Data Entry
Computer stations for prescription entry, insurance processing
Filling Station
Counting trays, tablets/capsules, liquid measuring, labeling equipment
Pharmacist Verification
Final check area, drug information resources, counseling preparation
Pick-up/Cashier
Payment processing, prescription pickup, OTC recommendations
Inventory Storage
Shelving, refrigerators, controlled substance safe, OTC products

Behind-the-Counter (BTC): Medications requiring pharmacist consultation before sale (pseudoephedrine, plan B).

Insurance Processing

Prescription Adjudication Terms

Key insurance and reimbursement terminology for pharmacy technicians:

Adjudication
Electronic submission of prescription claim to insurance for reimbursement approval
BIN Number
6-digit Bank Identification Number for routing insurance claims
NDC Number
10- or 11-digit National Drug Code identifying manufacturer, product, package
NPI Number
10-digit National Provider Identifier for health care providers
DAW Codes
Dispense As Written codes affecting reimbursement (0=generic OK, 1=brand mandated)
Help Desk
Insurance company support line for pharmacy claim questions and issues

Common Rejections: Prior authorization needed, refill too soon, non-formulary drug, invalid prescriber ID.

Patient Care

Communicating with Special Needs Patients

Strategies for effective communication with diverse patient populations:

Non-English Speakers
• Use translation services
• Speak slowly and clearly
• Use visual aids
• Avoid medical jargon
Hearing Impaired
• Face patient directly
• Ensure good lighting
• Use written notes
• Consider TTY services
Visually Impaired
• Offer large print labels
• Use tactile markers
• Describe medication appearance
• Offer Braille if available
Low Literacy
• Use simple language
• Demonstrate when possible
• Use teach-back method
• Provide written instructions
Aphasia/Dysarthria
• Be patient and allow time
• Use yes/no questions
• Offer writing materials
• Confirm understanding
Elderly Patients
• Speak clearly
• Allow extra time
• Check comprehension
• Address polypharmacy concerns

Therapeutic Alliance: Building trust relationships with patients through respectful communication and mutual goal-setting.

Essential Terminology

Select each term to reveal its definition:

Adjudication
Electronic submission of prescription claim to insurance for reimbursement approval; process of claim processing.
DAW Codes
Dispense As Written codes (0-9) that indicate whether brand or generic should be dispensed, affecting reimbursement.
Signatura (Sig)
The directions on a prescription explaining how the patient should take the medication; appears on prescription label.
Auxiliary Label
Supplementary label providing additional safety/administration information (e.g., "Take with food," "Refrigerate").
BTC Medications
Behind-the-Counter medications requiring pharmacist consultation before sale but not a prescription.
Therapeutic Alliance
Trust relationship between healthcare professional and patient with mutually agreed upon treatment goals.
