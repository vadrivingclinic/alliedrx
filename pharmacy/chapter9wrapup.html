Practice Environments

Hospital Pharmacy Settings

Different types of institutional pharmacy environments where technicians may work:

🏥 Central Pharmacy
Main pharmacy serving entire hospital; processes most medication orders, IV preparations, maintains main inventory.
🔄 Satellite Pharmacy
Specialty pharmacy located in specific departments (OR, ED, ICU); provides immediate access to critical medications.
💉 IV Room/Clean Room
Sterile compounding area for IV medications, TPN, chemotherapy; requires aseptic technique certification.
📦 Decentralized Pharmacy
Pharmacists/technicians stationed on patient care units; provides clinical services and immediate order processing.
🏢 Government Hospitals
VA hospitals, Indian Health Service, Bureau of Prisons; follow federal regulations and specific formularies.
👵 Long-Term Care
Nursing homes, extended-living facilities; unit-dose packaging, medication cart preparation.

Order Processing

Hospital Medication Orders

Understanding Order Priorities and Processing Systems

STAT Order
Immediate: Life-threatening situation
Timeframe: 10-15 minutes
Examples: Cardiac arrest, anaphylaxis, severe pain
ASAP Order
Urgent: As soon as possible
Timeframe: 30-60 minutes
Examples: Antibiotics for infection, pain management
Standing Order
Protocol-based: Pre-approved for specific situations
Timeframe: Routine processing
Examples: Post-op pain meds, vaccination protocols

Order Entry Systems

  • CPOE: Computerized Physician Order Entry - reduces errors, improves efficiency
  • E-MAR: Electronic Medication Administration Record - documents medication administration
  • BPOE: Bedside Physician Order Entry - entered at patient bedside
  • CADM: Computer-Aided Dispensing Machines - automated medication dispensing

Compliance

Regulatory Agencies & Standards

Key organizations governing hospital pharmacy operations (Marcus's Joint Commission scenario):

The Joint Commission
Accredits hospitals; required for Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement; conducts unannounced surveys
CMS
Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services; sets Conditions of Participation for hospitals
FDA
Regulates drug safety, IND process, medication approvals and recalls
DEA
Controls scheduled medications; requires strict inventory and documentation
USP
Sets standards for sterile compounding (USP Chapter 797), medication quality
State BOP
State Board of Pharmacy; licenses facilities and personnel; sets state-specific regulations

Joint Commission Visit Preparation: Review policies, ensure documentation complete, verify staff competencies, check medication storage, audit controlled substances.

Sterile Compounding

IV Preparation: Hazardous vs. Nonhazardous

Critical Differences and Safety Protocols

Hazardous Drugs
  • Examples: Chemotherapy, some antivirals, immunosuppressants
  • Preparation: Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC) Class II
  • PPE Required: Double gloves, gown, eye protection, respiratory protection
  • Disposal: Special hazardous waste containers
  • Standards: USP Chapter 800
Nonhazardous CSPs
  • Examples: IV antibiotics, TPN, pain medications
  • Preparation: Laminar Airflow Hood (LAF)
  • PPE Required: Single gloves, gown, hair cover, shoe covers
  • Disposal: Regular pharmaceutical waste
  • Standards: USP Chapter 797
Aseptic Technique Essentials: Hand hygiene, proper garbing, sterile field maintenance, no-touch technique, environmental monitoring, beyond-use dating, quality assurance checks.

Hospital Departments

Specialty Areas Requiring Pharmacy Support

Different hospital departments with unique medication needs:

Emergency Dept (ED)
Crash carts, emergency medications, rapid sequence intubation kits
Operating Room (OR)
Anesthetics, neuromuscular blockers, reversal agents, irrigation solutions
Intensive Care Unit (ICU)
Vasopressors, sedatives, paralytics, IV antibiotics, TPN
Oncology
Chemotherapy, antiemetics, growth factors, supportive care medications
Pediatrics/NICU
Weight-based dosing, specialized formulations, neonatal medications
Psychiatry
Psychotropics, mood stabilizers, antipsychotics, PRN medications

Floor Stock: Non-patient-specific medications kept at nursing stations (emergency drugs, commonly used medications).

Essential Terminology

Select each term to reveal its definition:

Formulary
List of drugs approved for hospital use by Pharmacy & Therapeutics Committee; standard stock medications.
PAR Level
Periodic Automatic Replenishment - minimum quantity of medications maintained on nursing units.
Aseptic Technique
Procedures to minimize contamination during sterile compounding; required for IV preparation.
Automated Dispensing System
Computerized cabinets (Pyxis, Omnicell) that control medication access on nursing units.
Investigational Drug
FDA-unapproved drug in clinical trials; requires special handling and documentation.
Unit Dose
Individualized medication packaging for single administration; standard in institutional practice.
