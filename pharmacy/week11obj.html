Patient Safety First

Error Prevention • Safety Systems • Quality Assurance • Reporting Protocols

Week 11 focuses on medication safety and error prevention, the most critical responsibility for pharmacy technicians. You'll learn about patient rights, error types, prevention strategies, and quality systems that protect patients from medication-related harm.

Medication errors cause 7,000-9,000 deaths annually in the United States. Pharmacy technicians are frontline defenders against these preventable tragedies through diligent adherence to safety protocols and continuous quality improvement.

✅ Five Patient Rights of Medication Safety
1. Right Patient
Verify patient identity using two identifiers before administration
2. Right Drug
Confirm correct medication selection and labeling accuracy
3. Right Dose
Calculate and verify appropriate dosage strength and amount
4. Right Route
Ensure proper administration method (oral, IV, topical, etc.)
5. Right Time
Administer at correct frequency and scheduled intervals
⚠️ Common Medication Error Types
Wrong Drug Errors
Incorrect medication selection due to look-alike/sound-alike names
Wrong Dose Errors
Incorrect dosage calculations or decimal point placement errors
Wrong Route Errors
Medication administered via incorrect method (oral vs IV)
Wrong Patient Errors
Medication given to incorrect patient due to identification failures
🏛️ Safety Organizations & Reporting Systems
ISMP
Institute for Safe Medication Practices - collects error data and publishes safety alerts
MedWatch
FDA's safety information and adverse event reporting program
MedMARx
National database for tracking adverse drug reactions and medication errors
NCCMERP
National Coordinating Council for Medication Error Reporting and Prevention

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Apply the Five Rights of medication safety to every dispensing and preparation task.
  • Identify and categorize different types of medication errors and their root causes.
  • Navigate error reporting systems and understand mandatory vs voluntary reporting requirements.
  • Implement barcode scanning, automated dispensing systems, and other technology to prevent errors.
  • Participate effectively in medication safety committees and quality improvement initiatives.
  • Apply USP <797> regulations and risk management principles to sterile compounding safety.
Error Prevention: Barcode scanning systems reduce medication errors by 85% when implemented correctly. The combination of barcode scanning at dispensing, administration, and verification points creates a closed-loop medication system that prevents most preventable errors.
Look-Alike Sound-Alike (LASA) Drugs: Medications like hydralazine/hydroxyzine or Lamictal/Lamisil cause frequent errors. Technicians must use TALLman lettering (hydrALAZINE/hydroXYzine), separate storage, and double verification for all LASA medications to prevent dangerous mix-ups.
Safety Committee Participation: Like Jonathan, technicians on safety committees should prepare by studying error data, understanding human factors, and learning quality improvement methodologies. Technician insights into workflow challenges are invaluable for developing practical, effective safety solutions.
📚 Key Medication Safety Terms
Medication Error
Any preventable event that may cause inappropriate medication use or patient harm
Institute for Safe Medication Practices (ISMP)
Nonprofit organization dedicated to preventing medication errors and promoting safety
MedWatch
FDA program for reporting drug safety alerts and adverse events
Automated Dispensing System (ADS)
Electronic system that controls medication dispensing to prevent errors
Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy (REMS)
Program to manage known or potential serious risks associated with medications
National Coordinating Council (NCCMERP)
Multidisciplinary council addressing causes of medication errors and prevention strategies
Medication Safety Leader
Designated individual responsible for overseeing medication safety programs and initiatives
Quality Assurance
Systematic approach to ensuring medications meet established standards of safety and efficacy
