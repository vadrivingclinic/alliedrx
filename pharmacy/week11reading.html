🛡️ The Five Rights of Medication Safety

1
Right Patient
Verify patient identity using two identifiers before medication administration
2
Right Drug
Confirm medication name, strength, and formulation matches the order
3
Right Dose
Verify dosage calculation and measurement accuracy
4
Right Route
Confirm administration method (oral, IV, IM, etc.)
5
Right Time
Administer medication at correct time and frequency

Prescribing Errors Incorrect drug, dose, frequency, or route ordered Illegible handwriting, incorrect calculations, knowledge gaps
Dispensing Errors Wrong drug, strength, or quantity provided Similar packaging, sound-alike drugs, interruptions

Patient Safety

Protecting Patients from Preventable Harm

Chapter 14 (Medication Safety & Error Prevention) addresses the most critical responsibility in healthcare: preventing harm to patients. This chapter transforms knowledge into life-saving practices through systems, verification, and continuous improvement.

Why Error Prevention Matters:

  • Patient Survival: Medication errors cause 7,000-9,000 deaths annually in the US
  • Professional Responsibility: Every healthcare worker shares accountability for patient safety
  • Systemic Protection: Safety is built through systems, not individual perfection
  • Continuous Improvement: Error reporting drives system enhancements
Jonathan's Committee Role Preparation:
  • Review Historical Data: Analyze past error reports for patterns and trends
  • Study Safety Frameworks: Learn ISMP guidelines and NCCMERP error categories
  • Understand Systems: Familiarize with ADS, barcoding, and CPOE safety features
  • Practice RCA: Master root cause analysis methodology
  • Prepare Contributions: Document frontline observations and improvement ideas
Key Safety Organizations & Their Roles:
  • ISMP: Develops error prevention guidelines and alerts
  • FDA MedWatch: National adverse event reporting system
  • NCCMERP: Standardizes error categorization and prevention
  • ASHP: Establishes best practices for medication use
  • USP: Sets quality and safety standards for medications

Automated Safety Systems (Objective 7):

  • Computerized Provider Order Entry (CPOE): Eliminates handwriting errors, includes dose checking
  • Bar Code Medication Administration (BCMA): Verifies patient and medication at bedside
  • Automated Dispensing Cabinets (ADCs): Controls medication access, tracks inventory
  • Smart Infusion Pumps: Prevents IV dosing errors with hard/soft limits
  • Clinical Decision Support (CDS): Alerts for allergies, interactions, duplications
Common Error Causes (Objective 4):
  • Communication Failures: Incomplete orders, unclear verbal orders
  • Work Environment: Interruptions, distractions, fatigue
  • Knowledge Deficits: Unfamiliar drugs, incorrect calculations
  • System Issues: Poor labeling, similar packaging, inadequate staffing
  • Human Factors: Complacency, confirmation bias, rushing

Error Prevention Strategies (Objective 6):

  • Verification Systems: Independent double-checks for high-risk medications
  • Standardization: Tall man lettering, standardized concentrations
  • Error Reporting Culture: Non-punitive reporting, focus on system improvement
  • Patient Engagement: Teach-back method, medication reconciliation
  • Continuous Education: Regular safety training, competency assessments
⚠️ High-Risk Medication Categories:
  • Anticoagulants (warfarin, heparin, direct oral anticoagulants)
  • Insulins and other hypoglycemic agents
  • Opioids and other controlled substances
  • Chemotherapy and hazardous drugs
  • Concentrated electrolytes (potassium, magnesium)
  • Pediatric and neonatal medications
