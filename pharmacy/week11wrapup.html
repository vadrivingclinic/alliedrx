Core Concept

The Five Rights of Medication Safety

Memorize and apply these fundamental rights every time you handle medications. Each right must be verified before medication administration.

1 Right Patient

Verify patient identity using two identifiers (name, date of birth, medical record number).

2 Right Drug

Confirm the medication name matches the prescription order exactly, checking for look-alike/sound-alike drugs.

3 Right Dose

Verify the prescribed dosage is appropriate and matches available strength; calculate accurately if needed.

4 Right Route

Ensure medication is prepared for correct administration method (oral, IV, IM, topical, etc.).

5 Right Time

Administer medication at the correct time according to schedule and pharmacokinetic considerations.

Step 2

Safety Organizations & Systems

Select each card to learn about key safety organizations and systems. Understand their roles in error prevention and reporting.

ISMP
Institute for Safe Medication Practices: A nonprofit dedicated entirely to medication error prevention through data collection, analysis, and safety standard recommendations.
MedWatch
FDA's safety information and adverse event reporting program for medications, medical products, and devices.
Automated Dispensing System
Electronic systems that control medication dispensing, often with barcode scanning and inventory tracking to reduce errors.
NCCMERP
National Coordinating Council for Medication Error Reporting and Prevention: Interdisciplinary council addressing causes and prevention of medication errors.
REMS
Risk Evaluation and Mitigation Strategy: FDA-required plans for drugs with serious risks to ensure benefits outweigh risks.
Medication Error
Any preventable event that may cause or lead to inappropriate medication use or patient harm, whether or not harm occurs.

Quick Reference

Common Medication Error Types

Know These Categories

  • Prescribing Errors: Wrong drug, dose, route, frequency, or contraindicated therapy
  • Dispensing Errors: Wrong drug, strength, dosage form, or labeling errors
  • Administration Errors: Wrong patient, route, time, or technique
  • Monitoring Errors: Failure to review therapy, assess response, or check labs
  • Documentation Errors: Incomplete or inaccurate medication records

Remember: Near-misses (errors caught before reaching patient) are critical learning opportunities and should always be reported.
