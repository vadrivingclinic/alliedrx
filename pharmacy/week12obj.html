Mastering Drug Information Resources

References • Research • Resources

Week 12 focuses on drug information mastery through print and electronic references. You'll learn to navigate essential pharmacy resources, evaluate their content, and apply research skills to support medication safety and patient care.

In today’s digital age, knowing where to find accurate information is as important as knowing the information itself. A technician must quickly distinguish between reliable references and outdated or unverified sources—whether using a pocket guide, a printed formulary, or an online database.

💼 Pharmacy Scenario: Robert, CPhT
Role: Certified Pharmacy Technician with 6 years of experience
Setting: Busy retail pharmacy
Task: Updates the Drug Facts and Comparisons loose-leaf binder monthly
Challenge: Finds the process tedious and wonders about switching to an online database
Key Question: How should Robert approach his pharmacist about transitioning to digital resources? What are the pros and cons?

Key Terms & Definitions

Term Definition
Brand name Trademark of a drug held by the originating manufacturer
Generic name Nonproprietary name of a drug
Formulary List of approved drugs stocked by the pharmacy or covered by insurance
Monograph Comprehensive information on a medication’s class, uses, side effects, and interactions
Package insert Official prescribing information provided by the manufacturer
Nonformulary Drugs not included in the preferred/reimbursable drug list

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Navigate 10+ core drug references efficiently in both print and digital formats.
  • Evaluate the reliability of online drug information sources for clinical use.
  • Recommend appropriate references based on practice setting and information need.
  • Apply research skills to answer drug-related questions accurately and quickly.
  • Discuss the ethical and legal importance of using current, evidence-based references.
Digital Transition Tip: When suggesting a switch to online references, prepare a cost-benefit analysis that includes time savings, update frequency, and search capabilities. Highlight how real-time updates improve patient safety.
Reference Selection: For quick dosing checks, use pocket guides or mobile apps. For in-depth therapeutic reviews, turn to AHFS DI or Clinical Pharmacology. Always verify with at least two sources for critical information.
Tech-Patient Education: Use MedlinePlus or FDA resources to provide patients with reliable, easy-to-understand drug information. Avoid unverified online forums or commercial sites with promotional bias.
