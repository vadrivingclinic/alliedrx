Key Terms & Definitions

Brand Name
Trademark of a drug or device held by the originating manufacturing company.
Generic Name
Nonproprietary name assigned to a medication.
Formulary
List of approved drugs to be stocked by the pharmacy or covered by insurance.
Monograph
Comprehensive information on a medication's actions, side effects, dosages, and interactions.
Package Insert
Official prescribing information provided by the drug manufacturer.
Drug Classification
Categorization based on chemical structure, action, or therapeutic use.

Clinical Application

Mastering Drug Information Resources

Chapter 7 (Drug Information References) equips you with the skills to efficiently locate accurate medication information—a critical competency for pharmacy technicians in any setting.

Essential Reference Comparison:

  • Physicians' Desk Reference (PDR): Manufacturer-provided prescribing information, ideal for FDA-approved uses
  • Orange Book (Approved Drug Products): Therapeutic equivalence evaluations for generic substitutions
  • Drug Facts & Comparisons: Comprehensive drug monographs organized by therapeutic class
  • USP-NF: Official standards for drug strength, quality, and purity
  • Micromedex/Clinical Pharmacology: Electronic databases with up-to-date drug information
When to Use Which Reference:
  • Patient asks about side effects: Drug Facts & Comparisons or package insert
  • Checking generic equivalence: Orange Book
  • Compounding verification: USP-NF or Trissel's
  • Drug interaction check: Micromedex or Clinical Pharmacology
  • Pediatric dosing: Pediatric Dosage Handbook
Electronic vs. Print Considerations:
  • Electronic Pros: Instant updates, searchability, portability, interaction checking
  • Electronic Cons: Subscription costs, internet dependency, training required
  • Print Pros: No power/internet needed, standardized format, one-time purchase
  • Print Cons: Outdated quickly, physical storage, manual searching

Robert's Scenario Analysis:

  • Yes, Robert should suggest electronic resources—they save time, reduce errors, and provide current information
  • Approach professionally: Present cost-benefit analysis, demonstrate efficiency gains, suggest trial period
  • Implementation strategy: Start with one electronic resource, train staff, maintain print as backup
