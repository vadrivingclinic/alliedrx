💡 Pharmacy Tech Tip
Always verify drug names carefully. Many drugs have similar names but treat completely different conditions. For example, Lamictal (lamotrigine) for seizures vs. Lamisil (terbinafine) for fungal infections. When in doubt, ask the pharmacist or check reliable drug references.

Common Confusions:
• Don't confuse Dilantin (phenytoin) with Ditropan (oxybutynin)
• Don't confuse Neurontin (gabapentin) with Noroxin (norfloxacin)
• Always verify spelling: Amiodarone vs. Amlodipine (different classes)

🧠 Neuro/Cardiac Drug Knowledge

  • Seizure Medications: All require gradual titration and should never be discontinued abruptly. Common side effects include dizziness, drowsiness, and potential for drug interactions.
  • ED Medications: PDE5 inhibitors are contraindicated with nitrates. Duration of action varies: Sildenafil (4-6 hours), Tadalafil (up to 36 hours).
  • Anti-Arrhythmics: Many have narrow therapeutic indices requiring blood monitoring. Amiodarone has the longest half-life (up to 100 days) and requires multiple organ monitoring.

🏥 Clinical Application & Safety

  • Therapeutic Monitoring: Phenytoin, digoxin, and valproic acid require regular blood level monitoring. Document levels and adjust doses per protocol.
  • Patient Education: Seizure medications must be taken consistently. ED medications should be taken as directed before activity. Anti-arrhythmics may require regular EKGs and blood tests.
  • Safety Checks: Always verify indication, check for contraindications, review drug interactions, and confirm appropriate dosing for age and renal/hepatic function.
