Key Points Remembered:
• Prilosec = Omeprazole - First PPI approved by FDA
• Nexium = Esomeprazole - Isomer of omeprazole
• Zantac = Ranitidine - Note: Many ranitidine products were recalled in 2020
• Tagamet = Cimetidine - First H2 blocker developed

Common Confusions:
• Don't confuse Protonix (pantoprazole) with Prilosec (omeprazole)
• Don't confuse Aciphex (rabeprazole) with Accupril (quinapril)
• Always double-check drug suffixes: -prazole for PPIs, -tidine for H2 blockers

💊 Drug Knowledge Insights

  • PPI Recognition: All proton pump inhibitors end in -prazole. Remember: Omeprazole (Prilosec) was the first, Esomeprazole (Nexium) is its isomer.
  • H2 Blocker Patterns: All H2 receptor antagonists end in -tidine. Note: Ranitidine (Zantac) recall means famotidine (Pepcid) is now most common H2 blocker.
  • Indication Memory: GERD = gastroesophageal reflux disease. PUD = peptic ulcer disease. PPIs are stronger than H2 blockers for both conditions.

🏥 Clinical Application & Safety

  • Dispensing Accuracy: Always verify both brand and generic names. Many sound-alike medications exist (e.g., Lamisil vs. Lamictal). Use TALLman lettering when appropriate.
  • Patient Education Points: PPIs work best when taken 30-60 minutes before meals. H2 blockers can be taken as needed. Both may interact with clopidogrel (Plavix).
  • Regulatory Compliance: Document all brand-generic conversions accurately. Maintain current knowledge of FDA recalls and black box warnings for all dispensed medications.
