Gastrointestinal Medications Overview

Understanding acid-suppressing drugs for GERD and ulcer treatment

📋 Key Medical Abbreviations
  • GERD: Gastroesophageal Reflux Disease - Chronic acid reflux
  • PUD: Peptic Ulcer Disease - Ulcers in stomach or duodenum
  • PPI: Proton Pump Inhibitor - Strongest acid suppression
  • H2B: H2 Blockers - Moderate acid reduction

PPI vs H2 Blocker Comparison

Feature Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs) H2 Blockers
Mechanism Irreversibly block proton pumps (H+/K+ ATPase) Reversibly block histamine H2 receptors
Onset of Action 3-5 days for full effect 1-2 hours
Duration 24+ hours 6-12 hours
Strength Strongest acid suppression Moderate acid suppression
Common Use Severe GERD, healing erosive esophagitis Mild GERD, nighttime symptoms
Administration 30-60 minutes before breakfast With or without food

Aciphex Rabeprazole Fastest onset of action among PPIs
💡 Clinical Pearls for PPIs
  • Timing is critical: Take 30-60 minutes before first meal of the day for maximum effectiveness
  • Enteric coating: Do not crush or chew delayed-release formulations
  • Long-term use: Monitor for B12 deficiency, magnesium levels, and increased fracture risk
  • Drug interactions: Can reduce absorption of drugs requiring acidic environment (ketoconazole, iron, calcium)

H2 Blockers (H2 Receptor Antagonists)

Moderate acid suppression for mild GERD and ulcer prevention

🧪

H2 Blockers Drug List

🎯 Indications for H2 Blockers:
  • Mild GERD
  • Duodenal Ulcers
  • Gastric Ulcers
  • Nighttime Heartburn
  • Stress Ulcer Prophylaxis
Brand Name Generic Name Key Information
Zantac Ranitidine Withdrawn from market due to NDMA contamination (2020)
Pepcid Famotidine Currently preferred H2 blocker, OTC available
Tagamet Cimetidine Multiple drug interactions, inhibits CYP450
Axid Nizatidine Good renal excretion, fewer CNS side effects
💡 Clinical Pearls for H2 Blockers
  • Rapid onset: Effects begin within 1-2 hours, useful for PRN dosing
  • Tolerance: Tachyphylaxis can occur with continuous use over 2 weeks
  • Cimetidine warnings: Significant CYP450 inhibition - monitor drug interactions
  • Renal dosing: Adjust dose for patients with renal impairment

Knowledge Check Quiz

1. A patient needs strong acid suppression for severe erosive esophagitis. Which class of medication would be most appropriate?

Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)
H2 Blockers
Antacids
Prokinetic agents
Correct Answer: Proton Pump Inhibitors (PPIs)
PPIs provide the strongest acid suppression and are first-line for severe GERD and erosive esophagitis healing. H2 blockers are better for mild symptoms or maintenance therapy.

2. Which medication pairing is correct?

Prilosec - Esomeprazole
Protonix - Pantoprazole
Nexium - Omeprazole
Prevacid - Rabeprazole
Correct Answer: Protonix - Pantoprazole
Correct pairings are: Prilosec-Omeprazole, Nexium-Esomeprazole, Protonix-Pantoprazole, Prevacid-Lansoprazole, Aciphex-Rabeprazole.

3. Why was Zantac (ranitidine) removed from the market?

Ineffective for GERD treatment
NDMA (probable carcinogen) contamination
Severe liver toxicity
Cardiac arrhythmia risk
Correct Answer: NDMA (probable carcinogen) contamination
In 2020, the FDA requested removal of all ranitidine products due to unacceptable levels of NDMA, a probable human carcinogen. Famotidine (Pepcid) is now the preferred H2 blocker.
🎯 Essential Pharmacy Technician Knowledge
  • Patient counseling: Teach patients to take PPIs 30-60 minutes before breakfast for maximum effectiveness
  • Formulation awareness: Do not crush or chew enteric-coated PPI tablets/capsules
  • Safety monitoring: Watch for long-term PPI side effects - increased fracture risk, B12 deficiency, C. diff infection
  • Drug interactions: PPIs reduce absorption of drugs requiring acidic environment (ketoconazole, iron, calcium carbonate)
  • Current guidelines: Use lowest effective dose for shortest duration needed; consider stepping down to H2 blockers for maintenance
💼 Professional Practice Tip:

Always verify the indication and duration of therapy when dispensing PPIs. For long-term use (>8 weeks), recommend monitoring with healthcare provider and consider periodic attempts to discontinue or reduce dose.
