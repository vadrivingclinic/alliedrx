Unit Conversion Practice

Practice essential unit conversions used daily in pharmacy practice. Accurate conversions are critical for proper medication preparation.

📏 Conversion Example
1 gram (g) = 1000 milligrams (mg)
1 milligram (mg) = 1000 micrograms (mcg)
1 liter (L) = 1000 milliliters (mL)
  1. Convert 2.5 grams to milligrams: 2.5 g × 1000 = 2500 mg
  2. Convert 750 milligrams to grams: 750 mg ÷ 1000 = 0.75 g
  3. Convert 0.5 liters to milliliters: 0.5 L × 1000 = 500 mL
💡 Practice Problems
Problem 1: Convert 1.2 grams to milligrams.
Problem 2: A medication is dosed at 5 mg/kg. For a 70 kg patient, what is the total dose?
Problem 3: Convert 1500 milliliters to liters.

Dosage & Concentration Calculations

Master percentage calculations, ratio strengths, and medication concentration problems.

🧪 Concentration Example
Concentration (%) = (Mass of solute ÷ Total volume) × 100
For 1% solution: 1 g of drug in 100 mL of solution
  1. 5% solution means 5 g of drug in 100 mL of solution
  2. To make 250 mL of 2% solution: 2 g × (250/100) = 5 g needed
  3. 1:1000 solution means 1 g in 1000 mL = 0.1% concentration
💡 Practice Problems
Problem 1: How many grams of drug are needed to prepare 500 mL of a 3% solution?
Problem 2: A medication is available as 250 mg/5 mL. How many mL are needed for a 500 mg dose?
Problem 3: If 10 g of drug is dissolved in 200 mL of solution, what is the percentage strength?
