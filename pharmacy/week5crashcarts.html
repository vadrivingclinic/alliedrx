⚠️ CRITICAL SAFETY WARNING
NEVER assume that unused drugs in a crash cart are correct!

• Pediatric vs. Adult Strengths: Common error with lidocaine and epinephrine
• Epinephrine: Pediatric (1:1000, 1 mg/mL) vs. Adult (1:10,000, 0.1 mg/mL)
• Fatal Error: Adult-dose epinephrine in pediatric trays can cause death
• Proper Procedure: Always remove all medications and start anew with prepared list
• Double Check: Verify all strengths and expiration dates

⚠️ HIGH-RISK MEDICATION PAIRS
• Epinephrine: Vasopressor - Critical dose distinction between pediatric and adult
• Lidocaine: Antiarrhythmic - Also used as local anesthetic, different concentrations
• Digoxin: Cardiac glycoside - Narrow therapeutic index, requires monitoring
• Heparin: Anticoagulant - Weight-based dosing, frequent monitoring required
• Insulin: (Not listed but often stocked) - Unit confusion (U-100 vs U-500)
✓ All Matches Complete!
Classification Patterns:
• -olone endings: Often corticosteroids, but careful - Naloxone is different
• -amine endings: Often vasopressors (Dopamine, Dobutamine)
• -dipine endings: Calcium channel blockers (but Verapamil is exception)
• -lol endings: Beta-blockers (Metoprolol, Propranolol)

Critical Emergency Drugs:
• Cardiac Arrest: Epinephrine, Amiodarone, Lidocaine
• Anaphylaxis: Epinephrine, Diphenhydramine (not always on cart)
• Hypoglycemia: Dextrose 50%, Glucagon
• Opioid Overdose: Naloxone

🚑 Emergency Medication Mastery

  • High-Risk Medications: Epinephrine and Lidocaine require special attention to pediatric vs adult concentrations. A 10-fold dosing error can be fatal. Always verify concentrations before restocking.
  • Antiarrhythmics: Amiodarone, Lidocaine, Procainamide, and Adenosine are critical for cardiac emergencies. Each has specific indications and administration requirements.
  • Vasopressors: Dopamine, Dobutamine, Epinephrine, and Norepinephrine support blood pressure during shock. Understand their different receptor activities and indications.

🏥 Crash Cart Management Protocol

  • Restocking Procedure: Always start with empty cart. Use facility-specific checklist. Two-person verification for all medications. Document restocking date and verifiers.
  • Pediatric Considerations: Maintain separate pediatric crash carts. Clearly label all pediatric medications. Double-check concentrations before placement.
  • Quality Assurance: Monthly expiration date checks. Regular staff training on crash cart locations and contents. Mock code participation to maintain proficiency.
