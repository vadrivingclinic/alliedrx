Hospital Practice

Why Institutional Pharmacy Practice Matters

Chapter 9 prepares you for one of the most challenging and rewarding pharmacy settings. Hospital pharmacy requires advanced technical skills, strict attention to detail, and comprehensive knowledge of regulations to ensure patient safety in critical care environments.

Critical Hospital Pharmacy Concepts:

  • Patient Safety: Multiple checks prevent medication errors in high-stakes environment.
  • Regulatory Compliance: TJC accreditation essential for hospital funding and operation.
  • Team Coordination: Pharmacy-nursing collaboration ensures timely medication administration.
  • Technical Expertise: Sterile compounding skills critical for IV medications and chemotherapy.

The Joint Commission Visit Preparation:
  • Documentation review: Policies, procedures, training records
  • Facility inspection: Cleanliness, organization, safety measures
  • Process observation: Medication preparation, dispensing, documentation
  • Staff interviews: Knowledge assessment, competency verification
  • Medication storage: Temperature monitoring, security, expiration dating
  • Error prevention: Double-check systems, labeling standards

Sterile Compounding & Aseptic Technique

  • USP 797: Standards for compounded sterile preparations (CSPs)
  • USP 800: Hazardous drug handling requirements
  • Laminar Flow Hoods: Maintain sterile environment for preparation
  • Garbing Requirements: Hair covers, masks, gowns, sterile gloves
  • Beyond-Use Dating: Shorter expiration for compounded preparations
Hazardous vs. Non-Hazardous IV Preparation:
  • Hazardous: Chemotherapy, some antivirals - requires biological safety cabinet
  • Non-Hazardous: Antibiotics, electrolytes, TPN - prepared in laminar flow hood
  • Personal Protective Equipment: Double gloves, gowns, eye protection for hazardous drugs
  • Containment: Closed system transfer devices for hazardous drug administration
  • Documentation: Special labeling, waste disposal procedures for hazardous drugs

Automated Dispensing & Inventory Management

  • Pyxis/SureMed Systems: Computerized medication access with tracking
  • PAR Level Management: Minimum/maximum stock levels for each medication
  • First In, First Out (FIFO): Rotate stock to use oldest medications first
  • Controlled Substance Security: Double-locked storage, perpetual inventory
  • Crash Cart Maintenance: Scheduled checks, expiration date monitoring
Specialty Hospital Areas Pharmacy Serves:
  • Operating Room (OR): Anesthetics, emergency medications, irrigation solutions
  • Emergency Department (ED): STAT medications, trauma drugs, resuscitation supplies
  • Intensive Care Unit (ICU): Vasoactive drugs, sedatives, paralytics, antibiotics
  • Oncology Unit: Chemotherapy, antiemetics, growth factors
  • Pediatric Unit: Weight-based dosing, liquid formulations, specialized packaging
  • Labor & Delivery: Oxytocin, analgesics, emergency medications

Hospital Technician Career Advancement

  • Certification Levels: Technician I (entry), Technician II (certified), Technician III (lead)
  • Specialization: Sterile compounding, chemotherapy, inventory management
  • Professional Organizations: ASHP, state pharmacy associations
  • Continuing Education: Required for certification maintenance, hospital credentialing
  • Career Pathways: Supervisor roles, informatics, purchasing, education
