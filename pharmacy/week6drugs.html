Key Points Remembered:
• Avandia = Rosiglitazone - Thiazolidinedione class
• Actos = Pioglitazone - Also thiazolidinedione, fewer cardiac concerns than Avandia
• Lantus = Insulin Glargine - Long-acting basal insulin
• Singulair = Montelukast - Leukotriene receptor antagonist for asthma/allergies
• Advair Diskus = Fluticasone + Salmeterol - Combination inhaler (steroid + LABA)
• Combivent = Albuterol + Ipratropium - Combination bronchodilator

Common Confusions:
• Don't confuse Glucotrol (glipizide) with Glucophage (metformin)
• Don't confuse Avandia (rosiglitazone) with Amaryl (glimepiride)
• Nasonex (mometasone) vs. Nasacort (triamcinolone) - both nasal steroids
• Clarinex (desloratadine) vs. Claritin (loratadine) - both antihistamines

⚠️ Critical Safety Points
• Insulin products: Never interchange without provider approval. Lantus is long-acting, others may be rapid or short-acting
• Sulfonylureas (Diabeta/Glyburide): Risk of hypoglycemia, especially in elderly
• Thiazolidinediones (Avandia/Actos): May cause fluid retention and heart failure exacerbation
• Inhaled corticosteroids (Advair/Flonase): Rinse mouth after use to prevent oral thrush
• Leukotriene inhibitors (Singulair): Monitor for mood changes and neuropsychiatric effects

• Diabetes drugs: Different mechanisms - know which are insulin secretagogues vs sensitizers
• Asthma drugs: Rescue (albuterol) vs controller (steroids, montelukast) medications
• Allergy medications: Sedating (older antihistamines) vs non-sedating (newer ones)
• Drug interactions: Many diabetes and asthma drugs have significant interactions

🩸 Glycemic Control Drug Knowledge

  • Insulin Products: Lantus (insulin glargine) is a long-acting basal insulin administered once daily. Never interchange with rapid-acting insulins like Humalog or Novolog without provider approval.
  • Oral Hypoglycemics: Sulfonylureas (Diabeta/glyburide, Glucotrol/glipizide) stimulate insulin secretion. Thiazolidinediones (Avandia/rosiglitazone, Actos/pioglitazone) improve insulin sensitivity.
  • Safety Considerations: Sulfonylureas carry highest hypoglycemia risk. Thiazolidinediones may cause fluid retention and are contraindicated in heart failure. Always check renal function before prescribing.

🌬️ Asthma & Allergy Drug Knowledge

  • Bronchodilators: Proventil (albuterol) is a short-acting beta agonist for acute relief. Combination products like Combivent (albuterol + ipratropium) provide both bronchodilation and anticholinergic effects.
  • Controller Medications: Advair Diskus (fluticasone + salmeterol) combines corticosteroid with long-acting bronchodilator. Singulair (montelukast) is a leukotriene receptor antagonist for maintenance therapy.
  • Allergy Treatments: Second-generation antihistamines (Zyrtec/cetirizine, Allegra/fexofenadine) are less sedating. Nasal steroids (Flonase/fluticasone, Nasonex/mometasone) are first-line for allergic rhinitis.

🏥 Clinical Application & Safety

  • Patient Education: Teach proper inhaler technique, insulin injection rotation, and medication timing. Emphasize rescue vs controller medications for asthma patients.
  • Drug Interactions: Monitor for corticosteroid-induced hyperglycemia in diabetic patients. Watch for beta-blocker interactions with asthma medications. Check for QT prolongation with certain combinations.
  • Special Populations: Adjust diabetes medications in renal impairment. Use caution with asthma drugs in cardiac patients. Consider sedation risk with antihistamines in elderly.
