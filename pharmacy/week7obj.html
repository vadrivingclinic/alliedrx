Compounding & Repackaging Excellence

BULK Repackaging • USP 795 • Quality Control • Hands-On Training

Week 7 delves into the specialized skills of bulk repackaging and non-sterile compounding. You'll learn procedures for preparing unit doses, compounding customized medications, and maintaining quality control according to USP 795 standards.

These skills are critical for long-term care, compounding, and hospital pharmacies where customized medications and unit-dose packaging improve patient safety, medication adherence, and operational efficiency.

✅ Compounding Safety & Quality Control
  • Beyond-Use Dates (BUD): Calculate using stability data from USP or literature
  • Documentation: Complete Formulation Records (FR) and Compounding Records (CR)
  • Calibration: Regular equipment calibration according to manufacturer specifications
  • Labeling: Complete labeling with BUD, storage conditions, and patient instructions

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Perform bulk repackaging procedures with proper handling and documentation according to GMP standards.
  • Calculate beyond-use dates for repackaged and compounded medications using appropriate stability data.
  • Prepare non-sterile compounded products following USP 795 guidelines and formulation records.
  • Operate compounding equipment including balances, mortars/pestles, and measuring devices accurately.
  • Maintain complete documentation including repackaging logs, formulation records, and compounding records.
  • Apply quality control procedures to ensure compounded products meet safety and efficacy standards.
Unit Dose Advantages: Repackaging into unit doses reduces medication errors by 50% in institutional settings. It improves medication adherence, reduces waste, and enhances medication tracking and inventory control.
USP 795 Compliance: Non-sterile compounding must follow USP Chapter 795 guidelines which address stability, beyond-use dating, ingredient quality, and documentation requirements. These standards ensure patient safety and product efficacy.
Blister Card Packaging: Used in long-term care facilities, blister cards (bubble packs) with 28-, 30-, or 31-day compartments improve medication administration accuracy and simplify medication pass procedures for nursing staff.
📚 Key Compounding & Repackaging Terms
Beyond-Use Date (BUD)
Date after which compounded preparation should not be used; determined from preparation date
USP 795
General chapter defining practices and guidelines for non-sterile compounding
Bubble Pack (Blister Card)
Preformed card with daily compartments for long-term care medication packaging
Formulation Record (FR)
Document similar to recipe used in preparation of non-sterile compounds
Compounding Record (CR)
Form that documents a non-sterile compounding process
Good Manufacturing Practices (GMP)
Federal guidelines for all entities preparing and packaging medication or devices
Triturate
To grind or crush powder, such as a tablet, into fine particles
Troches
Flat, disklike tablets that dissolve between gum and cheek
Reconstitution
Mixing liquid and powder to form suspension or solution
