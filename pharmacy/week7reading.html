Clinical Application

Mastering Medication Preparation Skills

Chapter 11 (Bulk Repackaging & Non-Sterile Compounding) provides essential skills for medication preparation beyond standard dispensing. These specialized skills are crucial in long-term care, compounding pharmacies, and institutional settings where customized medications are required.

Critical Competencies Developed:

  • Accuracy: Precise measurements and calculations for patient safety
  • Documentation: Complete and accurate record-keeping for quality control
  • Regulatory Compliance: Adherence to USP 795 and Good Manufacturing Practices
  • Problem-solving: Creating customized medications for unique patient needs
Judy's Training Advantages:
  • Hands-on Experience: Practical skills development in a controlled lab environment
  • Muscle Memory: Developing proper techniques for weighing, measuring, and mixing
  • Error Recognition: Learning to identify and prevent common compounding mistakes
  • Confidence Building: Gaining competence before working with actual patient medications
  • Quality Assurance: Understanding the importance of every step in the process
Common Non-Sterile Compounding Applications:
  • Pediatric: Creating liquid formulations from adult tablets
  • Geriatric: Adjusting dosages or removing allergens
  • Dermatological: Customizing creams and ointments for specific skin conditions
  • Veterinary: Compounding medications for animal patients
  • Allergy/Sensitivity: Removing dyes, preservatives, or allergens

Five Reasons for Unit Dose Packaging:

  • Medication Safety: Reduces medication errors by providing single, identifiable doses
  • Contamination Prevention: Maintains medication integrity by protecting individual doses
  • Inventory Control: Simplifies tracking and reduces waste in institutional settings
  • Dosing Accuracy: Ensures precise administration, especially for high-alert medications
  • Time Efficiency: Streamlines medication administration in busy healthcare settings
Key USP 795 Guidelines:
  • Beyond-Use Dating: Specific timelines for different dosage forms
  • Formulation Records: Detailed "recipes" for each compounded preparation
  • Compounding Records: Documentation of each batch prepared
  • Quality Control: Procedures for verifying accuracy and stability
  • Facility Requirements: Appropriate environment and equipment standards
  • Personnel Qualifications: Training and competency requirements

Dosage Forms in Non-Sterile Compounding:

  • Creams: Hydrophilic bases for topical application
  • Ointments: Hydrophobic bases like petroleum jelly
  • Solutions: Water bases with dissolved ingredients
  • Suspensions: Undissolved particles in liquid base
  • Troches: Lozenge-like tablets for buccal absorption
  • Elixirs: Alcohol-water mixtures for oral use
  • Syrups: Sugar-based liquids for palatable medications
  • Capsules: Powdered medications in gelatin shells
