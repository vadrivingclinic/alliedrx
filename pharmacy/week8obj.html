Sterile Compounding Excellence

Aseptic Technique • USP Regulations • Hazardous Drugs • IV Preparation

Week 8 focuses on sterile compounding and aseptic technique, the most critical skills for pharmacy technicians working in hospital and compounding settings. You'll learn USP regulations, proper technique, and safety protocols for preparing life-saving intravenous medications.

Sterile compounding requires absolute precision and strict adherence to protocols. Contaminated preparations can cause serious infections or fatalities, making proper training and technique essential for patient safety and regulatory compliance.

📊 USP <797> Risk Levels
Low Risk Level
Simple aseptic transfers of sterile products using ≤3 commercial containers
Medium Risk Level
Multiple aseptic transfers or manipulations; complex compounding procedures
High Risk Level
Non-sterile ingredients used, prolonged preparation time, complex manipulations

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Apply USP <797> and <800> regulations to daily sterile compounding activities.
  • Perform proper aseptic technique to prevent contamination of compounded sterile preparations.
  • Differentiate between horizontal and vertical laminar flow hoods and their appropriate uses.
  • Prepare medications from ampules and vials using correct technique to maintain sterility.
  • Identify and apply appropriate risk levels (low, medium, high) to various compounding scenarios.
  • Implement proper disposal procedures for hazardous materials and chemotherapy waste.
Critical Safety: First air is the air exiting the HEPA filter that must contact critical sites (vial tops, needle hubs) first. Never block first air flow as contamination can occur within seconds, potentially causing life-threatening infections in immunocompromised patients.
USP <800> Hazardous Drugs: Biological Safety Cabinets (BSCs) must be used for hazardous drug compounding. Proper PPE includes double gloves, gowns, respiratory protection, and eye protection to prevent exposure to carcinogenic and teratogenic substances.
Beyond-Use Dating: BUDs are determined by USP <797> based on risk level and storage conditions. Low-risk CSPs stored at room temperature have 48-hour BUDs, while high-risk preparations may have only 24-hour BUDs. Accurate labeling prevents administration of expired medications.
📚 Key Sterile Compounding Terms
Aseptic Technique
Procedures to eliminate possibility of drug contamination with microbes or particles
Critical Site
Any surface exposed to first air or at risk for touch contamination
First Air
Air exiting HEPA filter in unidirectional stream that must contact critical sites first
Beyond-Use Date (BUD)
Date after which compounded sterile preparation cannot be administered
Clean Room
Controlled environment with low pollutants for preparing sterile medications
Biological Safety Cabinet (BSC)
Vertical flow hood for hazardous sterile preparations
Total Parenteral Nutrition (TPN)
Large volume IV nutrition administered through central vein
Hazardous Drug
Drug proven to have dangerous effects during testing (carcinogenic, teratogenic)
Compounded Sterile Preparations (CSPs)
Preparations made in sterile environment using non-sterile ingredients/devices
