Correct Matches:
• Multiskilling → Being proficient at more than one job; cross-training
• Point-of-Care Testing (POCT) → Testing performed at or near the patient for immediate results
• Quality Control → Procedures to ensure test accuracy before patient testing
• Waived Testing → Simple tests with low risk of error, often performed as POCT

Additional Key Terms:
• Glucometer: Device for measuring blood glucose levels
• Electrocardiography (ECG): Recording of heart's electrical activity
• Calibration: Adjusting equipment to match known standards
• Clinical Assistant: Multiskilled technician working under supervision

Certification Importance: These terms frequently appear on phlebotomy certification exams. Understanding them demonstrates comprehensive knowledge.

Chapter 10 Key Concepts Review:

1. Multiskilling Benefits:
• Increased job security in changing healthcare environments
• Better marketability for diverse roles (PCT, Clinical Assistant, etc.)
• Potential for higher pay with additional responsibilities
• Adaptation to hospital patient care team models

2. Common POCT Tests:
• Blood glucose monitoring
• Rapid strep tests
• Urinalysis dipsticks
• Hemoglobin by cuvette
• Pregnancy tests
• INR/coagulation monitoring

3. Quality Control Non-Negotiables:
• Perform QC BEFORE patient testing
• Document all QC results
• Remove faulty equipment from service
• Understand consequences of skipping QC

4. Career Applications:
• Multiskilling opens doors to supervisory roles
• POCT skills are in high demand
• Quality assurance knowledge is valuable in all settings
• Continuing education maintains certification and skills
