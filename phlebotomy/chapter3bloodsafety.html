Correct Donning Sequence for Airborne Precautions:
1. Perform hand hygiene
2. Put on gown
3. Put on N95 respirator (perform seal check)
4. Put on goggles or face shield
5. Put on gloves (extend over gown cuffs)

Correct Doffing Sequence:
1. Remove gloves (inside out, one glove at a time)
2. Perform hand hygiene
3. Remove gown (unfasten ties, pull away from body)
4. Perform hand hygiene
5. Remove goggles/face shield (by headband)
6. Remove N95 (by straps, don't touch front)
7. Perform hand hygiene

Critical Errors to Avoid:
• Adjusting goggles after gloving (contaminates gloves)
• Touching front of gown during removal
• Failing N95 seal check (allows airborne entry)
• Removing PPE in wrong order (self-contamination risk)

🦺 PPE & Infection Control Insights

  • Sequence Matters: Donning/doffing sequence is critical. Wrong order (e.g., adjusting goggles after gloving) leads to self-contamination. Airborne precautions require N95 seal check—a failed check is as effective as no mask.
  • Time-Critical Actions: Immediate washing reduces bloodborne pathogen transmission by 50%. PEP for HIV must begin within 2 hours (ideal), up to 72 hours. Delayed reporting increases legal liability and health risks.
  • Documentation is Protection: OSHA 300 logs are legal documents maintained for 30+ years. Incomplete documentation can void workers' compensation claims and lead to regulatory citations.

💊 Pharmacy Technician Connection

  • Sterile Compounding PPE: Beyond standard precautions, USP <797> requires specific PPE for hazardous drug compounding. Glove thickness matters—chemotherapy requires thicker gloves than standard procedures.
  • Sharps in Pharmacy: Technicians handle insulin pens, lancets, and other sharps from returns. These carry the same bloodborne pathogen risks as clinical sharps. Proper disposal and immediate incident reporting are equally critical.
  • Emergency Preparedness: Pharmacy technicians may be first responders to spills or exposures in medication areas. Knowing immediate response protocols protects both staff and medication integrity.
