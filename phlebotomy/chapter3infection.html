Correct Transmission Order (Most to Least Contagious):
1. Airborne (remains in air for hours, travels long distances)
2. Droplet (travels 3-6 feet, settles quickly)
3. Contact (requires direct/indirect contact)
4. Vector-Borne (requires specific insect vector)

PPE Requirements:
• Standard Precautions: Gloves, hand hygiene (for all patient contact)
• Contact Precautions: Gown, gloves, hand hygiene (for MRSA, C. diff)
• Droplet Precautions: Surgical mask, eye protection (for flu, pertussis)
• Airborne Precautions: N95 respirator, negative pressure room (for TB, measles)

Key Distinction: Surgical masks protect patients from your respiratory droplets; N95 respirators protect you from airborne pathogens.

✓ Pathogen Protocol Complete
Highest Concern Pathogen: Hepatitis B (HBV)
Reason: HBV is the most infectious bloodborne pathogen, with up to 30% transmission risk from a needlestick. HCV has 1.8% risk, HIV has 0.3% risk.

Correct Post-Exposure Protocol:
1. Immediately wash site with soap and water (do NOT squeeze wound)
2. Report incident to supervisor and occupational health
3. Complete incident report and source patient testing
4. Begin Post-Exposure Prophylaxis (PEP) if indicated
5. Follow up testing at 6 weeks, 3 months, and 6 months

Critical Timeframes:
• HBV PEP should begin within 24 hours (up to 7 days)
• HIV PEP should begin within 2 hours (up to 72 hours)
• Immediate action (washing) reduces infection risk by 50%

🦠 Infection Control Insights

  • Chain Breaking Strategy: The most effective infection control targets the weakest link. For airborne diseases like TB, breaking the transmission link (N95, negative pressure) is more effective than targeting the susceptible host.
  • PPE Hierarchy: Different precautions require specific PPE. Confusing droplet (surgical mask) with airborne (N95) precautions can lead to serious exposure events. Always verify the transmission route.
  • Time-Critical Responses: Immediate washing reduces bloodborne pathogen transmission risk by 50%. PEP timeframes are critical: HBV within 24 hours, HIV within 2 hours. Delayed reporting increases legal and health risks.

💉 Pharmacy Technician Connection

  • Medication Preparation Safety: Technicians preparing IV medications in negative pressure rooms for TB patients require the same airborne precautions as direct caregivers. Understanding transmission routes protects both staff and sterile products.
  • Sharps Safety: Pharmacy technicians handling used insulin pens, lancets, or other sharps have bloodborne pathogen exposure risks. Proper disposal and immediate incident reporting are legally required by OSHA.
  • PPE in Sterile Compounding: Beyond standard precautions, USP <797> requires specific PPE for hazardous drug compounding. Understanding these requirements prevents workplace exposure to chemotherapy and other hazardous agents.
