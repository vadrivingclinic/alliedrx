Key Rules for Combining Vowels:
1. Usually "o": Most common (hematology, liposuction, phlebotomy)
2. Sometimes "i": With certain roots (venipuncture)
3. No vowel: When suffix begins with a vowel (carditis, not cardioitis)

Correct Answers:
• ven + i + puncture = venipuncture ("i" is used with "ven")
• lip + o + suction = liposuction ("o" is most common)

Pronunciation Practice: Say "phle-bot-o-my" (3 syllables with "o") vs "phleb-tomy" (harder to pronounce). The combining vowel makes medical terms easier to say and understand.

🏥 Phlebotomy Practice Applications

  • Order Interpretation: When you see "STAT CBC for patient with thrombocytopenia," you now understand "thrombo-cyto-penia" means low platelets. This alerts you to bleeding risk precautions.
  • Procedure Documentation: Accurate use of "venipuncture" vs "phlebotomy" in documentation shows professional competency. Venipuncture = vein puncture; Phlebotomy = vein cutting procedure.
  • Patient Education: You can explain "hematoma" as "hema" (blood) + "oma" (swelling) = blood swelling under skin. This helps patients understand common complications.
  • Lab Test Understanding: "Hematocrit" = hema (blood) + crit (to separate). Knowing this helps explain this common blood test to patients.

📚 Essential Phlebotomy Terms Mastered

  • Venipuncture: ven (vein) + i (combining vowel) + puncture (to pierce) = Your primary procedure
  • Hematoma: hemat (blood) + oma (tumor/swelling) = Common complication to recognize and document
  • Anticoagulant: anti (against) + coagul (clotting) + ant (agent) = Critical for proper tube selection
  • Phlebitis: phleb (vein) + itis (inflammation) = Complication to monitor for and report
