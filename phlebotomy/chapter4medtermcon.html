Correct Answers:
• hyperglycemia: hyper + glyc + emia (no combining vowel needed because "glyc" ends with "c" and "emia" starts with vowel)
• phlebitis: phleb + itis (no combining vowel needed because "itis" starts with vowel)

Combining Vowel Rules:
1. Use "o" when next component starts with consonant (hemat-o-logy)
2. No vowel needed when suffix starts with vowel (phleb-itis)
3. Some roots use specific vowels (ven-i-puncture)

Clinical Terms:
• Hyperglycemia = high blood sugar (common in diabetic patients)
• Phlebitis = vein inflammation (complication to monitor for)

Key Takeaways:
• Combining vowels (usually "o") make medical terms easier to pronounce
• Prefixes modify meaning from the beginning
• Word roots give the primary meaning
• Suffixes modify meaning from the end

Phlebotomy Examples:
• Phleb-itis = vein inflammation (complication)
• Hemat-oma = blood swelling (bruise)
• Anti-coagulant = against clotting (tube additive)

🏥 Clinical Application Mastery

  • Lab Order Comprehension: When you see "STAT hematology panel for thrombocytopenia monitoring," you now understand this means immediate blood tests to check platelet levels in a patient with low platelets.
  • Procedure Documentation: Accurate use of "phlebitis" vs "hematoma" in incident reports shows professional competency and ensures proper follow-up care.
  • Patient Communication: You can explain "hyperglycemia" as "hyper" (high) + "glyc" (sugar) + "emia" (blood condition) = high blood sugar, helping diabetic patients understand their condition.
  • Specimen Handling: Understanding "anticoagulant" helps you explain why certain tubes need to be inverted and why timing matters for coagulation studies.

🔬 Advanced Phlebotomy Terminology

  • Hemolysis: hemo (blood) + lysis (destruction) = broken blood cells. Understanding this helps prevent specimen rejection by using proper technique.
  • Leukocytosis: leuko (white) + cyt (cell) + osis (condition) = high white blood cell count. Often indicates infection or inflammation.
  • Polycythemia: poly (many) + cyt (cell) + hemia (blood condition) = too many red blood cells. Affects blood viscosity and draw difficulty.
  • Hypovolemia: hypo (low) + vol (volume) + emia (blood condition) = low blood volume. Important for assessing dehydration in patients.
