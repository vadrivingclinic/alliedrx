✓ Sharps Safety Analysis
OSHA-Required Sharps Disposal Procedure:
1. Immediate disposal: After use, before any other task
2. One-handed technique: Use scoop method or mechanical device
3. Never recap: Except using approved one-handed method
4. Proper container: FDA-approved, leak-proof, puncture-resistant
5. Fill level: Do not fill above ¾ full (or manufacturer's line)

Engineering Controls:
• Safety-engineered devices (self-sheathing needles)
• Needleless systems for IV access
• Sharps with engineered sharps injury protections (ESIPs)

Pharmacy Technician Role:
Technicians must be trained in proper sharps handling when:
• Preparing chemotherapy drugs
• Compounding sterile preparations
• Handling any medication administration devices

💉 Venipuncture Technique Insights

  • Needle Gauge Selection: 21G is standard for most adults, 23G butterflies for fragile veins, and 25G for difficult pediatric access. Larger gauges (20G) provide faster flow but increase hematoma risk in fragile patients.
  • Vein Assessment: Elderly patients often have fragile, rolling veins requiring smaller gauges and tourniquet alternatives. Athletic patients have prominent veins but may be deeper. Always assess before selecting equipment.
  • Consequence Management: 20G on fragile veins causes hematomas. 23G on deep veins causes slow fill. Butterfly systems reduce vein trauma but increase cost. Understanding these trade-offs is essential.

🏥 Pharmacy Technician Connection

  • Medication Administration: Technicians assisting with injections must understand needle selection for different medications (subcutaneous vs intramuscular) and patient populations.
  • Inventory Management: Knowing which needle gauges are used for which procedures helps technicians maintain appropriate stock levels and anticipate clinic needs.
  • Patient Education: Technicians can explain why certain needle sizes are chosen, reducing patient anxiety and improving cooperation during procedures.
