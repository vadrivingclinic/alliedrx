Why Arthritis Makes Phlebotomy Difficult:
1. Positioning Challenges: Cannot fully extend elbow for antecubital access
2. Pain Response: Sudden movements increase risk of needle stick injury
3. Alternative Sites: May need to use hand veins, which are smaller and more painful
4. Skin Integrity: Thinner skin over joints increases hematoma risk

👴👵 Geriatric-Specific Insights

  • Polypharmacy Awareness: The average older adult takes 4-5 prescription medications daily. Pharmacy technicians must be vigilant about drug interactions, especially with anticoagulants commonly used in this population.
  • Skin Changes Matter: Age-related loss of skin elasticity and subcutaneous fat makes veins more fragile and prone to rolling. Using smaller gauge needles (23G instead of 21G) and minimizing tourniquet time reduces hematoma risk.
  • Cognitive Considerations: Up to 15% of adults over 65 have some cognitive impairment. Simplifying instructions and allowing extra time improves compliance and reduces anxiety during procedures.

💊 Pharmacy Technician Role in Geriatric Care

  • Medication Reconciliation: Technicians play a key role in ensuring accurate medication lists, crucial for older adults who may see multiple specialists and use OTC medications that interact with prescriptions.
  • Supply Chain Adaptation: As the population ages, pharmacy inventories must include more geriatric-friendly items: easy-open caps, large-print labels, and medications for age-related conditions (osteoporosis, dementia, arthritis).
  • Communication Bridge: Technicians often serve as the link between elderly patients, caregivers, and pharmacists. Understanding geriatric challenges helps explain medication instructions clearly and check for comprehension using teach-back methods.

⚠️ Safety Alerts for Geriatric Phlebotomy

  • Fall Prevention: Older adults have slower reaction times and balance issues. Always ensure they are seated securely during draws, and provide assistance when standing up.
  • Bleeding Risk: Many elderly patients are on anticoagulants (warfarin, DOACs). Apply pressure for 3-5 minutes and check the site before dismissing the patient.
  • Dehydration Detection: Older adults are prone to dehydration, which makes veins difficult to locate. Encourage hydration before elective draws when possible.
