✓ Procedure Analysis Complete
Correct Heel Stick Sequence:
1. Warm the heel with a warm compress for 3-5 minutes to increase blood flow
2. Clean the site with 70% isopropyl alcohol and allow to air dry
3. Use a sterile, automatic lancet on the outer aspect of the heel
4. Gently wipe away the first drop of blood with sterile gauze
5. Collect blood in appropriate micro-containers without squeezing
6. Apply gentle pressure with sterile gauze until bleeding stops

Key Rationales:
• Warming: Increases blood flow up to 7x, reducing need for squeezing (which causes hemolysis)
• Outer heel: Avoids the posterior curvature (risk of nerve/bone damage)
• First drop: Contains tissue fluid, alcohol residue, and skin cells
• No squeezing: Prevents hemolysis and dilution with tissue fluid

Correct MCQ Answer: D (All of the above)
The first drop contains tissue fluid (dilution), alcohol residue (interferes with tests), and skin cells (contaminates cultures).

Preventing Iatrogenic Anemia:
• Coordinate tests to minimize draws
• Use micro-methods when available
• Document total blood volume drawn
• Monitor infant's hemoglobin levels

👶 Neonatal Care Insights

  • Blood Volume Matters: A term newborn has only 85-100 mL/kg blood volume. Removing 5 mL represents 5-10% of total volume. Pharmacy technicians must understand this when preparing blood collection supplies.
  • Test Prioritization: In NICU settings, tests are batched to minimize draws. Pharmacy plays a key role in ensuring appropriate tubes and volumes are available for combined testing.
  • Hemolysis Prevention: Neonatal blood has higher hematocrit and is more fragile. Proper technique prevents hemolysis that could lead to unnecessary repeat draws and increased iatrogenic anemia risk.

💊 Pharmacy Technician Connection

  • Supply Chain Role: Techs ensure appropriate inventory of micro-collection tubes, lancets, and warm compresses for neonatal units. Understanding test requirements prevents stockouts.
  • Medication Implications: Iatrogenic anemia may require iron supplements or erythropoietin. Techs preparing these medications must understand their indication in this context.
  • Quality Assurance: Documenting blood draw volumes helps track iatrogenic blood loss. Pharmacy techs supporting laboratory services contribute to this monitoring system.
