The phlebotomist prepares for a routine blood draw. After greeting the patient, they check the patient's armband against the requisition form and begin setting up.

They apply the tourniquet directly over the intended puncture site and wait while searching for a vein. After leaving the tourniquet on for 3 minutes, they find a suitable vein.

The site is cleaned with an alcohol swab using a circular motion, but the phlebotomist immediately proceeds with venipuncture before the alcohol dries.

After successful puncture and blood flow, they fill a light blue top tube only halfway full before switching to a lavender top tube.

Once collection is complete, they vigorously shake the serum tubes to mix them and apply a cotton ball with minimal pressure before sending the patient on their way.

The specimens are delivered to the lab 2 hours later without any special handling.

🧪 Specimen Quality Insights

  • Hemolysis Recognition: Pink/red serum indicates red cell rupture. Potassium elevates because intracellular K+ leaks into serum. Prevent by using proper needle size, avoiding frothing, and allowing alcohol to dry.
  • Proper Fill Volumes: Citrate tubes require exact 9:1 blood-to-anticoagulant ratio. Underfilling prolongs coagulation times; overfilling shortens them. EDTA tubes tolerate 80-120% fill for CBC.
  • Order of Draw Rationale: Blood culture tubes first to prevent contamination. Citrate tubes before clot activators to prevent false coagulation results. EDTA tubes before glycolytic inhibitors for glucose accuracy.

📋 Technician Responsibilities

  • Rejection Prevention: Recognize when a specimen should not be sent to lab. Short draws, clots, improper tubes, or gross hemolysis require recollection, not submission.
  • Timely Processing: STAT specimens within 15 minutes, routine within 45 minutes. Some tests (ammonia, blood gases) require immediate ice slurry transport.
  • Documentation Accuracy: Document all deviations from standard procedure: difficult draws, patient reactions, collection times, and any specimen condition concerns.
