Step 2

Key Vocabulary Flashcards

Select each card to reveal the definition. Then try to explain each term in your own words—as if you were teaching a new team member in the clinical setting.

Phlebotomy
The practice of drawing blood from patients for clinical testing, transfusions, donations, or research.
Antecubital Fossa
The triangular area on the anterior portion of the elbow where the median cubital, cephalic, and basilic veins are located; the primary site for venipuncture.
Homeostasis
The body's ability to maintain a stable internal environment despite external changes; crucial for proper bodily function and health.
Hemostasis
The process that stops bleeding at the site of an injury while maintaining blood flow elsewhere; involves vasoconstriction, platelet plug formation, and coagulation.
Plasma vs. Serum
Plasma is the liquid portion of blood containing clotting factors; serum is plasma without clotting factors, obtained after blood has clotted.
Code of Ethics
A set of principles and standards that guide professional behavior, emphasizing patient welfare, confidentiality, and professional competence in phlebotomy practice.

Chapter 2

Anatomy & Physiology: Objectives

Theodor Herzl on the Human Body

"[The body is] a marvelous machine … a chemical laboratory, a power-house. Every movement, voluntary or involuntary, full of secrets and marvels!"

Learning Objectives

At the conclusion of this chapter, you should be able to:

  • Define all key terms included in this chapter
  • Identify body planes and describe directional terms
  • Classify the four types of tissues based on structure and function
  • Define homeostasis
  • Describe the organs, major structures, and functions of each body system
  • List diagnostic tests and disorders related to each body system
  • Describe how blood circulates within the heart to the lungs and tissues
  • Describe how to take a blood pressure and factors that influence readings
  • Identify the three types of blood vessels and describe their structure and function
  • Name and identify the three common veins used for venipuncture
  • List the major components of blood and describe the function of each formed element
  • Differentiate between whole blood, serum, and plasma
  • Briefly explain clot formation, fibrinolysis, and the principles of blood typing

Clinical Connection

Scenario

You're observing in an outpatient clinic. A patient needs multiple blood tests, but has difficult veins and is anxious about the procedure. The phlebotomist must choose the best approach while keeping the patient comfortable.

Reflection prompts

  • How would your knowledge of vein anatomy help you select the best site for venipuncture?
  • What patient factors (age, hydration, medical conditions) might affect blood collection?
  • How would you apply ethical principles when dealing with an anxious patient?
  • Which body systems are most directly involved in the phlebotomy process?
