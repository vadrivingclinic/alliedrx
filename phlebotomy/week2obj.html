Safety Protocols & Equipment Mastery

Infection Control • Blood Collection Tools • Professional Practice

Week 2 combines critical safety knowledge with practical equipment skills. You'll learn how to protect yourself and patients from infectious diseases while mastering the tools and techniques for successful blood collection.

These chapters are interconnected: proper equipment use depends on infection control knowledge. Knowing which PPE to wear, how to handle contaminated sharps, and which collection tubes to use are all essential skills that keep everyone safe.

⚠️ Critical Bloodborne Pathogens
Hepatitis B (HBV): Vaccine recommended for all healthcare workers. Can survive on surfaces for 7+ days.

Hepatitis C (HCV): No vaccine available. Most common chronic bloodborne infection in the US.

HIV: Weaker virus outside the body, but serious consequences if transmitted. Standard Precautions prevent transmission.
🧪 Essential Phlebotomy Equipment
Needle Selection: 21-23 gauge for most adults, 23-25 gauge for pediatric/geriatric patients

Tube Holders: Must be single-use only to prevent cross-contamination

Sharps Containers: Must be within arm's reach, never overfilled, and disposed of according to facility policy

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Select appropriate PPE for different isolation precautions and clinical situations.
  • Identify correct blood collection tubes for common laboratory tests.
  • Properly don and doff PPE following infection control protocols.
  • Safely handle and dispose of needles and other sharps equipment.
  • Apply Standard Precautions in all patient encounters.
PPE Tip: Always perform hand hygiene before donning PPE and immediately after doffing. Gloves are not a substitute for hand washing—they can have microscopic tears that allow pathogen transmission.
Tube Order of Draw: Remember the sequence: Blood Cultures → Blue (Coagulation) → Serum Tubes → Heparin → EDTA → Glycolytic Inhibitor. Incorrect order can cause additive carryover and affect test results.
Needle Safety: Never recap used needles. Activate safety devices immediately after use. Dispose of sharps in puncture-resistant containers at the point of use. Report all needlestick injuries immediately.
