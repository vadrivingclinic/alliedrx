Week 2 Learning Activities

  • □ Create flashcards for all bloodborne pathogens (HBV, HCV, HIV) with transmission routes, symptoms, and prevention methods.
  • □ Practice the complete donning and doffing sequence for PPE with proper technique and order of removal.
  • □ Memorize all blood collection tube colors, additives, and common tests (focus on Lavender, Red, Blue, Green, Gray).
  • □ Create a comparison chart of Standard Precautions vs. Transmission-Based Precautions (Contact, Droplet, Airborne).
  • □ Practice identifying vein locations using anatomical landmarks and explaining the three common venipuncture sites.
  • □ Research and document the proper disposal methods for different needle types (21g, 22g, 23g) and safety devices.

Clinical Application

The Safety-Equipment Connection

Chapter 3 (Infection Control) and Chapter 5 (Equipment) are inseparable in practice. Every piece of equipment you'll learn about must be used with proper infection control knowledge to ensure safety.

How These Chapters Connect in Practice:

  • PPE (Ch3) + Needles (Ch5): Proper glove selection impacts your ability to safely handle different needle gauges.
  • Bloodborne Pathogens (Ch3) + Tube Handling (Ch5): Understanding transmission routes informs how you handle contaminated tubes.
  • Standard Precautions (Ch3) + Vein Location (Ch5): Proper skin prep and draping combine safety with effective technique.
  • Needle Disposal (Ch5) + MDROs (Ch3): Proper sharps disposal prevents transmission of multidrug-resistant organisms.
  • Training Aids (Ch5) + Respiratory Etiquette (Ch3): Using practice arms safely requires understanding droplet precautions.
Critical Safety-Equipment Pairings:
  • Gloves (PPE) + Needle Gauge Selection: Thicker gloves may reduce tactile sensitivity for smaller gauge needles
  • Face Protection + Tube Capping: Splash protection is essential when removing needles from tube holders
  • Gown Selection + Microcollection Devices: Proper gowning prevents contamination during capillary puncture
  • Eye Protection + Centrifugation: Essential when processing tubes that may break during spinning
Blood Tube Priority Guide:
  • Lavender (EDTA): CBC, WBC differential - MUST be inverted 8-10 times
  • Light Blue (Citrate): Coagulation studies (PT, aPTT) - fill to exact line
  • Red (No additive): Serum tests - let clot 30 minutes before spinning
  • Green (Heparin): Chemistry STAT tests - inverts 8 times
  • Gray (Fluoride): Glucose, alcohol - prevents glycolysis
Needle Safety Protocol:
  • 21g: Standard adult venipuncture, good blood flow
  • 22g: Most common, good for most patients
  • 23g: Pediatric/elderly, fragile veins
  • Safety Feature: NEVER recap needles, activate safety immediately after use
  • Disposal: Place in sharps container when ¾ full
