Key Vocabulary Flashcards

Select each card to reveal the definition. Then try to explain each term in your own words—as if you were teaching a new team member in the office.

Bloodborne Pathogens
Microorganisms present in human blood that can cause disease, including Hepatitis B, Hepatitis C, and HIV. Phlebotomists are at high risk of exposure.
Standard Precautions
Infection prevention practices that apply to all patients, regardless of diagnosis. Include hand hygiene, PPE use, safe injection practices, and proper handling of contaminated equipment.
EDTA (Purple Tube)
Ethylenediaminetetraacetate - an anticoagulant that binds calcium, preventing clotting. Used for CBC, WBC differential, and other hematology tests.
MDRO (MRSA/VRE)
Multidrug-Resistant Organisms like Methicillin-Resistant Staphylococcus aureus. These bacteria are resistant to multiple antibiotics, requiring Contact Precautions.
Needle Gauge
The diameter of a needle. Lower gauge numbers indicate larger diameters (e.g., 21-gauge is larger than 23-gauge). Important for choosing the right needle for different patients and tests.
Chain of Infection
The process by which an infection spreads: infectious agent → reservoir → portal of exit → mode of transmission → portal of entry → susceptible host. Breaking any link prevents infection.

Quick Reference

Common Blood Collection Tubes

Review these essential tube types, their additives, and common uses:

Tube Color Additive Common Tests
Lavender/Purple EDTA CBC, WBC differential, hemoglobin, hematocrit
Light Blue Sodium citrate Coagulation tests (PT, aPTT)
Red None (clot activator) Chemistry tests, serology, blood bank
Green Heparin Chemistry tests (STAT), ammonia, electrolytes
Gray Sodium fluoride/potassium oxalate Glucose, lactate, alcohol levels
Yellow SPS or ACD Blood cultures, HLA typing

Remember: Always follow the correct order of draw to prevent cross-contamination between tubes.

Chapter Summary

Key Learning Objectives

"To study the phenomenon of disease without books is to sail an uncharted sea."

Chapter 3: Infectious Diseases

  • Discuss bloodborne pathogens (HBV, HCV, HIV) and their significance
  • Describe routes of transmission and chain of infection
  • Identify symptoms of common viral/bacterial diseases
  • Understand importance of MDROs in hospital settings
  • Properly use and don/doff PPE
  • Apply Standard and Transmission-Based Precautions

Chapter 5: Equipment

  • Identify blood collection tubes and their additives
  • Understand needle types and gauge selection
  • Practice proper needle disposal (OSHA standards)
  • Use tube holders and vein location equipment
  • Properly use gloves and eye protection
  • Maintain essential phlebotomy equipment

Clinical Scenario: Marcus's First Day

Scenario

Marcus is a new phlebotomy student on his first clinical rotation. He's assigned to draw blood from a patient in Contact Precautions for MRSA, and needs to collect tubes for CBC, coagulation studies, and chemistry panels.

Reflection prompts

  • What PPE should Marcus wear, and in what order should he put it on?
  • Which blood collection tubes (colors) should he prepare, and in what order should they be drawn?
  • How should Marcus safely dispose of the needle and contaminated materials after the draw?
  • What infection control measures should he take when leaving the isolation room?
