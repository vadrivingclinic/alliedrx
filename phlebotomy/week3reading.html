  • □ Practice breaking down 15 medical terms into their components (prefix + root + suffix).
  • □ Memorize the plural formations for medical terms (e.g., -a to -ae, -um to -a, -us to -i).

  • □ Explain the difference between word roots and combining forms to a study partner.

Medical terminology is the universal language of healthcare. Mastering these basics will help you understand orders, communicate with healthcare team members, and document procedures accurately.

Clinical Application

Why Medical Terminology is Essential for Phlebotomists

Chapter 4 (Medical Terminology) teaches you to decode the language of healthcare. While phlebotomists aren't expected to be fluent, understanding common terms is critical for safe practice and professional communication.

Common Phlebotomy Terms You MUST Know:

  • Venipuncture: veni (vein) + puncture (to puncture) = to puncture a vein
  • Phlebotomy: phleb (vein) + o (combining vowel) + tomy (to cut) = to cut into a vein
  • Hematoma: hemat (blood) + oma (tumor/swelling) = swelling of blood
  • Antecubital: ante (before) + cubitum (elbow) = in front of the elbow
  • Hemolysis: hemo (blood) + lysis (destruction) = destruction of red blood cells

Why This Matters in Practice:

  • Understanding orders: "Collect CBC, PT, PTT STAT"
  • Documenting complications: "Small hematoma at antecubital site"
  • Communicating with nurses: "Possible hemolysis in lavender top tube"
  • Reading patient charts: "History of thrombocytopenia"
  • Following protocols: "Use aseptic technique for all venipunctures"
