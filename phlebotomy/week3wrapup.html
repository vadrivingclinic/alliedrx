Step 2

Key Vocabulary Flashcards

Select each card to reveal the definition. Then try to explain each term in your own words—as if you were teaching a new phlebotomy student.

Word Root
The main component of a medical term that establishes the initial meaning. Nearly all word roots point toward an organ, body system, structure, tissue, condition, or substance.
Prefix
A word element placed in front of a word root that changes its meaning by adding information about the context before its root meaning. Example: "hyper-" means above or excessive.
Suffix
A word element placed at the end of a word that changes the meaning of the word root or adds information about the context after its root meaning. Example: "-itis" means inflammation.
Combining Vowel
A vowel (usually "o") added between two word roots or between a word root and a suffix to make pronunciation easier. Example: "lip" + "o" + "suction" = liposuction.
Venipuncture
A medical term meaning to puncture a vein. Breakdown: "veni" (vein) + "puncture" (to puncture). The combining vowel "i" connects the word parts.
Phlebotomy
The practice of drawing blood from patients. Breakdown: "phleb" (vein) + "o" (combining vowel) + "tomy" (to cut). Literally means "to cut a vein."

Apply it

Applying Medical Terminology in Practice

Real-World Scenario

You're preparing to draw blood from a patient whose chart indicates they have thrombocytopenia and are taking an anticoagulant. The order is for a CBC and PTT tests.

Reflection Prompts

  • Break down the term "thrombocytopenia" into its components. How does understanding this term affect your approach to this draw?
  • What does the abbreviation "PTT" stand for? Why is this test ordered for patients on anticoagulants?
  • How would you explain to a patient what a "CBC" test measures, using simple language?
