Procedural Excellence in Phlebotomy

Routine Techniques • Special Collections • Clinical Applications

Week 4 focuses on hands-on procedural skills that form the core of phlebotomy practice. From standard venipuncture to specialized collection techniques, you'll develop the expertise needed to handle diverse patient needs and clinical situations.

These chapters build upon each other: mastering routine procedures prepares you for special collections. A skilled phlebotomist must adapt techniques for different tests, patient conditions, and clinical requirements while maintaining safety and accuracy.

🔄 Standard Venipuncture Procedure
1
Patient Identification: Verify patient using two identifiers (name, DOB, ID)
2
Preparation: Wash hands, apply gloves, assemble equipment
3
Vein Selection: Apply tourniquet, palpate for appropriate vein
4
Cleansing: Cleanse site with 70% isopropyl alcohol, allow to dry
5
Collection: Perform venipuncture, fill tubes in correct order
6
Completion: Remove needle, apply pressure, label tubes immediately

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Perform all three major blood collection techniques: evacuated tube, butterfly, and syringe methods.
  • Execute proper patient identification and preparation for diverse procedures.
  • Apply correct order of draw to prevent cross-contamination of additives.
  • Prepare patients and equipment for specialized tests like blood cultures and glucose tolerance.
  • Create diagnostic-quality blood smears for hematological analysis.
Patient Identification Critical: Always use two patient identifiers (name and date of birth or medical record number). Never rely on room numbers or bed locations. Ask the patient to state their name and DOB; don't just read it to them.
Blood Culture Collection: Requires meticulous antisepsis—clean with chlorhexidine or iodine, not just alcohol. Collect two sets from different sites for optimal detection of bacteremia. Fill aerobic bottle first, then anaerobic.
Butterfly Needle Tips: Use for difficult veins, pediatric patients, or hand veins. Hold at 10-15 degree angle. Advance slowly—the shorter needle requires less depth. Watch for flashback in the tubing.
Physiological Considerations: Remember that circadian rhythms affect hormone levels, fasting status impacts glucose and lipids, and patient position can alter protein and enzyme levels. Document any variations.
