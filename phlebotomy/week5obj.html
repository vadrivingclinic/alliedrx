Specialized Care & Complication Management

Vulnerable Populations • Problem-Solving • Safety Protocols

Week 5 addresses the unique challenges of working with vulnerable populations and managing complications. You'll learn specialized techniques for neonates and geriatric patients while developing skills to prevent and respond to phlebotomy complications.

These chapters are interconnected: understanding special populations helps prevent complications. Age-related changes, medical conditions, and patient cooperation levels all influence technique and outcomes. Proactive management reduces risks.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Perform safe blood collection from newborns using heel stick techniques.
  • Adapt venipuncture techniques for geriatric patients with age-related changes.
  • Prevent and manage common phlebotomy complications like hematomas and syncope.
  • Address uncooperative patients and difficult situations professionally.
  • Recognize and avoid technical problems that lead to unacceptable specimens.
Newborn Heel Stick Protocol: Use the lateral plantar surface of heel, not the center (avoids calcaneus bone). Warm heel first to increase blood flow. Puncture no deeper than 2.0mm to avoid bone injury. Wipe away first drop (contains tissue fluid).
Geriatric Vein Techniques: For fragile veins: use smaller gauge needles (23g), avoid tourniquet if possible, or use very loose application. Apply pressure longer after draw (3-5 minutes). Consider butterfly needles for better control.
Syncope Management: Watch for warning signs: pallor, sweating, nausea, lightheadedness. If patient faints: lower head immediately, ensure airway is clear, monitor breathing. Never leave patient unattended. Have ammonia inhalants available.
Uncooperative Patients: For dementia/confusion: simple, clear instructions, involve family/caregiver. For anxious patients: calm reassurance, distraction techniques. For combative patients: stop procedure, seek assistance, document behavior. Never force a procedure.
