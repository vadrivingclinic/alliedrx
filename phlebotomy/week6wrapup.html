Step 3

Key Vocabulary Flashcards

Select each card to reveal the definition. These terms are essential for understanding career advancement and professionalism.

Multiskilling
Being proficient at more than one job skill. For phlebotomists, this can include ECG, vital signs, specimen processing, and point-of-care testing.
Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)
Medical testing performed at or near the patient, providing immediate results (e.g., glucose monitoring, rapid strep tests, urinalysis).
Quality Control (QC)
Procedures performed to ensure that test equipment is working correctly and patient results are accurate before testing begins.
Waived Testing
Simple laboratory tests that have been approved by CLIA to be performed without extensive oversight (e.g., urine dipstick, glucose meters).
Empathy
The ability to understand and share the feelings of another person. Essential for effective patient communication and quality care.
Stereotyping
Making assumptions about individuals based on group characteristics. This should be avoided in professional healthcare settings.

Point-of-Care Testing (POCT)

Common POCT Procedures

Phlebotomists may perform these tests at the bedside or in outpatient settings:

Glucometer Testing
Blood glucose monitoring for diabetic patients
Urinalysis Dipstick
Chemical analysis of urine for various parameters
Rapid Strep Test
Detection of streptococcal antigens
Hemoglobin A1c
Average blood glucose over 2-3 months
Pregnancy Test
Detection of hCG hormone in urine
Coagulation Monitoring
PT/INR for patients on anticoagulants
Critical Quality Control Steps

1. Calibration: Adjust equipment using known standards
2. Control Testing: Run known samples to verify accuracy
3. Documentation: Record all QC results and maintenance
4. Troubleshooting: Know when to stop testing and seek help

Step 4

Professional Attributes & Communication

Essential Professional Qualities

Effective Communication

• Use clear, simple language (avoid jargon)
• Listen actively and confirm understanding
• Maintain appropriate eye contact
• Speak calmly and respectfully
• Provide clear instructions and expectations

Professional Appearance & Conduct

• Maintain clean, professional attire
• Practice good personal hygiene
• Arrive on time and prepared
• Maintain patient confidentiality (HIPAA)
• Show respect for all patients and colleagues

Patient Care Partnership Principles

• High quality care in a safe environment
• Involvement in care decisions
• Protection of privacy and confidentiality
• Help with billing claims and insurance
• Respect for personal values and beliefs

Continuing Education Importance

• Maintains certification and licensure
• Updates skills with new technologies
• Meets changing healthcare standards
• Enhances career advancement opportunities
• Demonstrates professional commitment
