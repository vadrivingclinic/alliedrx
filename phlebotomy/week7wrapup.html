Step 2

Quality Systems Framework

Understanding the differences between quality control, assurance, and improvement is essential for maintaining excellent phlebotomy services.

Quality Control (QC)

Definition: Specific technical activities that ensure test results are accurate and reliable.
Examples: Running control samples, calibrating equipment, verifying temperatures.
Focus: Detecting errors in testing processes.

Quality Assurance (QA)

Definition: Systematic approach to ensuring quality in all aspects of service.
Examples: Procedure manuals, competency assessments, continuing education.
Focus: Preventing errors through proper systems and training.

Quality Improvement (QI)

Definition: Continuous process of measuring quality and implementing changes to improve it.
Examples: Tracking metrics, analyzing errors, implementing process improvements.
Focus: Continuously enhancing quality beyond baseline standards.

The Phlebotomist's Role: Every phlebotomist contributes to quality through proper technique, accurate documentation, and reporting issues for system improvement.

Step 3

Key Vocabulary Flashcards

Select each card to reveal the definition. These terms are essential for understanding management and quality systems.

Operational Plan
A detailed plan outlining how a phlebotomy department will function day-to-day, including staffing, scheduling, and workflow processes.
Collection Schedule
A systematic plan for collecting inpatient specimens that coordinates with nursing activities and minimizes patient disturbance.
Product Review Committee
A committee that evaluates new products and equipment before they are purchased for use in the department.
Preanalytical Errors
Errors that occur before specimen analysis, including patient misidentification, improper collection technique, or incorrect tube selection.
Corrective Action
Steps taken to fix a problem identified through quality monitoring and to prevent its recurrence.
Thresholds
Pre-established limits for quality indicators that, when exceeded, trigger investigation and corrective action.

Step 4

Preanalytical Error Prevention

Common Phlebotomy Errors

Patient/Specimen ID Errors
  • Wrong patient drawn
  • Mislabeled tubes
  • Incomplete requisitions
  • Missing information
  • Duplicate labels
Collection Technique Errors
  • Wrong tube/additive
  • Incorrect order of draw
  • Hemolysis from rough handling
  • Insufficient sample volume
  • Clotted specimens
Specimen Handling Errors
  • Improper mixing
  • Delayed processing
  • Incorrect storage temperature
  • Transport delays
  • Improper packaging
Documentation Errors
  • Missing collection time
  • Incorrect collection site
  • No fasting status noted
  • Missing phlebotomist ID
  • Illegible handwriting
Procedure Manual Importance

• Standardizes techniques across all staff
• Provides reference for proper procedures
• Serves as training resource for new hires
• Supports quality assurance programs
• Required for regulatory compliance
• Must be reviewed and updated regularly
