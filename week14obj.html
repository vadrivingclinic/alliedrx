Comprehensive Urinalysis

Specimen Collection • Chemical Analysis • Microscopic Evaluation

Week 14 focuses on mastering comprehensive urinalysis procedures, one of the most common and informative diagnostic tests in clinical practice. You'll learn to collect, analyze, and interpret urine specimens to assess renal function, detect metabolic disorders, and monitor various health conditions.

Urinalysis provides a non-invasive window into systemic health, revealing information about kidney function, hydration status, metabolic processes, and potential infections. Mastery of these skills is essential for any medical assistant working in clinical or laboratory settings.

🧪 Chapter 28 Case Study: Rosa Gonzales, MA (AAMA)
Setting: Busy primary care practice with on-site laboratory
Responsibilities: Performing routine and specialized urine tests
Daily Cases: Routine UA for Mr. Parks, UA + pregnancy test for Mrs. Carpenter, UA + C&S for Ms. Hillman
Key Challenges: Ensuring quality assurance, proper specimen collection, accurate interpretation, patient education for various urine tests.

By the end of this week, you will be able to:

  • Collect urine specimens using proper techniques including clean-catch midstream and 24-hour collection methods.
  • Perform and interpret chemical urinalysis using CLIA-waived methods and quality control procedures.
  • Prepare urine specimens for microscopic evaluation and identify significant cellular elements, casts, and crystals.
  • Conduct specialized urine tests including pregnancy testing, glucose monitoring, and drug screening.
  • Educate patients on proper urine collection techniques and explain test results with sensitivity and professionalism.
Clean-Catch Technique: For optimal results, instruct patients to: 1) Clean genital area with provided wipes, 2) Begin urinating into toilet, 3) Collect midstream urine in sterile container, 4) Finish voiding into toilet. This minimizes contamination from skin flora.
Urine Color Interpretation: Normal urine is pale yellow to amber. Red/brown may indicate blood, dark orange/brown suggests bilirubin, blue/green could be from medications, and cloudy urine often indicates infection or crystals.
Drug Test Adulteration: Common adulterants include: bleach (oxidizes drugs), salt/vinegar (alters pH), water (dilutes specimen), peroxidase (from household cleaners). Always check temperature (90-100°F) and specific gravity immediately after collection.
Microscopic Findings: RBCs > 5/HPF suggest bleeding, WBCs > 5/HPF indicate infection, hyaline casts are normal after exercise, cellular/granular casts suggest renal disease, triple phosphate crystals are common in alkaline urine.
